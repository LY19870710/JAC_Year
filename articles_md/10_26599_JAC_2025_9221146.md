## 1 Introduction

With the rapid advancement of mobile communication technologies, the operational frequencies of microwave components have been extended to the millimeter wave and even terahertz ranges. In high-frequency communications, minimizing the signal delay is critical. The relative permittivity (*ε*r) of a dielectric substrate plays a pivotal role in determining the signal transmission delay, as it directly influences the propagation speed of electromagnetic signals. When the relative permittivity is reduced to below 4, the signal delay time can be decreased by more than 33% [1−4]. For millimeter-wave and terahertz applications, an ultra-low *ε*r (≤ 6) is necessary for a dielectric substrate to minimize cross-coupling with conductors and accelerate the circuit response speed. However, microwave dielectric ceramics with ultra-low *ε*r values remain scarce.

In the millimeter- and terahertz frequency ranges, the dielectric response originates solely from ionic and electronic displacement polarization [1,5]. Additionally, the frequency-dependent dielectric response can be interpreted through dielectric function models based on solid-state physics [6]. The relative permittivity in the optical frequency (*ε*r–∞), which corresponds solely to electronic polarization, can be derived from the refractive index (*n*) via the relation *ε*r–∞ = *n*2 for non-magnetic materials. *ε*r–∞ represents the lower bound of *ε*r, with typical *n* values of approximately 1.5 for dense inorganic ceramics [1]. Notably, the contribution of ionic displacement polarization to *ε*r generally exceeds that of electronic displacement polarization in inorganic ceramics. Consequently, the dense inorganic ceramics with *ε*r r ≤ 6 are relatively uncommon [7−9].

Ultra-low-*ε*r microwave dielectric ceramics are predominantly found in phosphates [10−15], molybdates [16−22], borates [23−30], and silicates [31−38]. However, phosphates, molybdates, and borates face significant limitations due to the volatilization of P, Mo, and B elements at high sintering temperatures, as well as their hygroscopic characteristics, which deteriorate their dielectric properties, restricting their applicability in high-frequency communications. In contrast, silicates achieve ultra-low *ε*r values (*ε*r ≤ 6) owing to the strong covalent Si–O bond within [SiO4] tetrahedra [39]. The ultra-low-*ε*r silicate ceramics are primarily Mg2Al4Si5O18-based [40−44], LiAlSiO4-based [32,33,45], and NaAlSi3O8-based [31] ceramics. However, the volatilization of Li and Na elements at high sintering temperatures limits the applications of LiAlSiO4-based and NaAlSi3O8-based ceramics. Cordierite Mg2Al4Si5O18-based ceramics exhibit ultra-low *ε*r values due to their [SiO4] tetrahedral six-membered ring structure [36], and their temperature coefficient of the resonance frequency (*τ*f) exhibited large negative values. Optimizing the crystal structure of Mg2Al4Si5O18 ceramics through ion substitution to control their *τ*f values to near zero is challenging [37,41]. The *τ*f values could be optimized by adding *τ*f regulators (TiO2, CaTiO3, and SrTiO3), but the *ε*r and quality factor (*Q*×*f*) consequently deteriorated [43,44]. The synergistic optimization of three parameters of dielectric properties (*ε*r, *Q*×*f*, and *τ*f) was difficult in microwave dielectric ceramics. Moreover, few single-phase silicate ceramics exhibit ultra-low *ε*r, high *Q*×*f*, and near-zero *τ*f values [46,47].

Furthermore, the coefficient of thermal expansion (CTE) is a critical performance parameter for ultra-low-*ε*r microwave dielectric ceramics in high-frequency applications. However, adjusting the CTE values of ceramics is significantly more challenging than optimizing their microwave dielectric properties. For high-frequency dielectric substrates, an ultra-low CTE is essential for microwave dielectric ceramics, which usually exhibit positive CTE values (~10 ppm/°C). Typically, the positive CTE values of microwave dielectric ceramics can be adjusted to near zero by adding negative CTE materials [48,49]. Unfortunately, such modifications often deteriorate their *Q*×*f* values. The co-optimization of the CTE and microwave dielectric properties remains a formidable challenge, and few single-phase ceramics simultaneously satisfy ultra-low *ε*r, high *Q*×*f* values, and near-zero *τ*f and CTE values.

BaMg2Al6Si9O30, with an osumilite-type structure, is characterized by six-membered double-inner rings of [Si/Al(1)O4] tetrahedra and outer octahedral [Mg6O36] and tetrahedral [Al(2)6O24] hexagonal rings. The ultra-low *ε*r (5.68), moderate *Q*×*f* values (28,534 GHz), and near-zero *τ*f value (−7.35 ppm/°C) of BaMg2Al6Si9O30 single-phase ceramics were observed in our previous work. However, a large negative CTE value (−9.43 ppm/°C) was obtained due to longitudinal vibrations of the two-coordinate O(1) ion, restricting its high-frequency applicability. To address this, we propose a crystal structural engineering strategy in which large Ge4+ substitutions for Si4+ enlarge the Si/Al(1)–O bond and reduce the Si/Al(1)–O(1)–Si/Al(1) angle. This modification suppresses O(1) ion vibrations, and the negative CTE values can be significantly optimized by reducing the Si/Al(1)–O(1)–Si/Al(1) angle. Herein, we successfully synthesized BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2.0) solid-solution ceramics, achieving dense microstructures alongside ultra-low *ε*r, high *Q*×*f*, and near-zero *τ*f and CTE values.

## 2 Experimental

The BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2) ceramics were synthesized using high-purity raw materials: BaCO3 (99.9%, Sinopharm), (MgCO3)4Mg(OH)2(H2O)5 (AR, Aladdin), Al2O3 (99.9%, Sinopharm), SiO2 (99.9%, Sinopharm), and GeO2 (99.9%, Aladdin). Notably, (MgCO3)4Mg(OH)2(H2O)5 was preferred over MgO as the magnesium source because of its superior chemical stability, facilitating the formation of single-phase BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 ceramics. All the raw materials were precisely weighed according to stoichiometric ratios and ball milled with ZrO2 grinding media in deionized water for 5 h. The resulting slurry was dried at 80 °C for 12 h and subsequently sieved through a 40-mesh screen. Thereafter, the obtained powder was calcined at 1200 °C for 5 h. After calcination, the calcined powder was reground, mixed with a polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) binder, and pressed into cylinders (*ϕ*12 mm × 6 mm). Finally, these samples were sintered at 1325–1350 °C for 5 h to obtain dense ceramics.

The sintered BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2) ceramics were crushed and ground into fine powders for structural characterization. The phase composition and crystal structure were analyzed using an X-ray diffractometer (XRD; X’Pert PRO), with Rietveld refinement performed via Fullprof software [50]. Microstructural examination and elemental distribution were performed via a scanning electron microscope (SEM; Zeiss Gemini 300) coupled with energy-dispersive spectroscopy (EDS). Before SEM observation, the polished ceramics (2000-grit finish) were thermally etched at 50 °C below their optimal sintering temperature for 30 min. Microwave dielectric properties were evaluated using the Hakki–Coleman method with a network analyzer (Keysight E5063A) [51]. High-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM) was conducted on a Tecnai G2 F30 (FEI), and the coefficient of thermal expansion was measured using rectangular samples with a dilatometer (Netzsch DIL402C). The observed density (*ρ*obs), theoretical density (*ρ*the), and relative density (*ρ*rel) of single-phase BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 ceramics were calculated using Eqs. (1) and (2):

(1) ρrel=ρobsρthe×100%

(2) ρobs=m1ρ0m1−m2

where *m*1 and *m*2 represent the sample masses in dry and water-immersed conditions, respectively; *ρ*0 represents the water density, and theoretical densities were derived from XRD refinement data.

Density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed using the Vienna *ab initio* simulation package (VASP) [52]. The exchange-correlation interactions were treated within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) using the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) functional. A plane-wave cutoff energy of 600 eV and a Γ-centered 2 × 2 × 1 *k*-point mesh were employed for Brillouin zone integration. The electronic and ionic convergence criteria were set to 1×10−6 eV and −0.01 eV/Å, respectively. Topological analysis of the electron localization function (ELF) was conducted using the Multiwfn code [53], enabling detailed wavefunction-derived property evaluation.

### 3.1 Evolution of crystal structure, energy structure, and microstructural characteristics of BaMg2Al6Si9−<i>x</i>Ge<i>x</i>O30 (0.25 ≤ <i>x</i> ≤ 2) ceramics

The XRD patterns of BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2) ceramics sintered at their optimal temperatures are presented in Fig. S1 in the Electronic Supplementary Material (ESM). All the diffraction peaks, which exhibited similar shapes, were successfully indexed to a hexagonal structure (*P*6/*mcc* space group) according to the profile of BaMg2Al6Si9O30 (PDF#01-083-0740), and no evident secondary phases existed in BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2) ceramics. Notably, the use of (MgCO3)4Mg(OH)2(H2O)5 as the raw material effectively suppressed the formation of BaAl2Si2O8 secondary phase, which arises from Mg deficiency in BaMg2Al6Si9O30 ceramics. As depicted in Fig. S1(b) in the ESM, the main diffraction peaks systematically shifted to lower angles with increasing *x*, resulting from the large ionic radius of Ge4+ substitution for Si4+. A single-phase solid solution of BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2) ceramics was successfully formed. Furthermore, Ge4+ substitution for Si4+ can effectively reduce the optimal sintering temperature of BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2) ceramics and adjust their crystal structure. Rietveld refinements were performed to investigate the evolution of crystal’s structural parameters, and the specific values of the crystal’s structural parameters and reliability factors are shown in Fig. S2 and Table S1 in the ESM. Good agreement was observed between the measured and calculated XRD patterns in Fig. S2 in the ESM, and small *χ*2 factors indicated the reliability of refinement results. Additionally, the refinement results confirmed the single-phase compositions of BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2) ceramics.

To further confirm the crystal structure and element distribution of BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2) ceramics, selected area electron diffraction (SAED) and HRTEM were performed. As depicted in Figs. 1(a)–1(c), Ba, Mg, Al, Si, Ge, and O elements were homogeneously distributed in the BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (*x* = 0.5) ceramics, while the detected C and Cu signals originated from the TEM sample support grid. No impurity phases were observed, and the EDS results confirmed the successful substitution of Ge4+ for Si4+, forming a homogeneous BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (*x* = 0.5) solid solution. Figures 1(d)–1(f) present the measured SAED patterns, simulated SAED patterns, and HRTEM images of BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (*x* = 0.5) ceramics. The measured SAED spots, acquired along the [0* 2¯1] axis, could be accurately indexed to a hexagonal structure (P*6/*mcc* space group), with clear and sharp diffraction spots (Fig. 1(d)). For comparison, the simulated SAED pattern along the same axis, generated using CaRlne crystallography software, exhibited an identical spot arrangement (Fig. 1(e)), indicating the accuracy of the calculation results. Additionally, the interplanar spacing observed in the measured SAED images was consistent with the measured results (0.863 nm) in the HRTEM patterns, corresponding to the (100) crystallographic planes of BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (*x* = 0.5). The consistency was further corroborated by fast Fourier transform (FFT) and inverse FFT (IFFT) analyses (Fig. 1(f)), confirming the reliability of the structural analysis.

SEM images of polished and thermally etched BaMg2Al6Si9−x*Ge*x*O30 (0.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2) ceramics are presented in Fig. 2. All samples exhibited dense microstructures, and surface grains were clearly observed. The microstructure of BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.5 ≤ *x* ≤ 2.0) ceramics was characterized by distinct grains and minimal porosity. The average grain size decreased progressively from 1.04 (*x* = 0.25) to 0.38 µm (*x* = 2.0), as illustrated in Fig. 2(h), suggesting that Ge4+ substitution for Si4+ effectively suppressed grain growth and enhanced the sintering behavior. The EDS mapping of BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 ceramics in Fig. S3 in the ESM confirmed the homogeneous distribution of Ba, Mg, Al, Si, and O elements, with no elemental segregation observed. The BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (1.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2.0) solid solutions were confirmed by SEM and EDS analyses.

As depicted in Fig. 3(a), the crystal structure of osumilite-type BaMg2Al6Si9O30 was characterized by six-membered double-inner rings of [Si/Al(1)O4] tetrahedra, along with outer [Mg6O36] octahedral and [Al(2)6O24] tetrahedral hexagon rings. The two-coordinate O(1) atom was prone to undergo vibrations along the *a* axis, which were responsible for the negative thermal expansion of the BaMg2Al6Si9O30 single-phase ceramics [54,55]. Notably, the vibrations of two-coordinate O(1) atom could be inhibited by decreasing the Si/Al(1)–O(1)–Si/Al(1) angle (*σ*2), suggesting that the elongation of the Si/Al(1)–O bond could contribute to decreasing the Si/Al(1)–O(1)–Si/Al(1) angle. The large Ge4+ was designed to substitute for the small Si4+, aiming to elongate the Si/Al(1)–O(1) bond and reduce the Si/Al(1)–O(1)–Si/Al(1) angle. As shown in Figs. 3(b) and 3(c) and Table S2 in the ESM, the Rietveld refinement results revealed that the roundness (*σ*1) of the [Si4Al(1)2O18] inner rings initially increased and then decreased with increasing *x*, whereas the *σ*2 gradually decreased with increasing Ge4+ substitution content. A large *σ*1 was obtained for the BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (*x* = 1.25) ceramics. This structural evolution confirmed that the elongated Si/Al(1)–O bonds effectively reduced *σ*2. Furthermore, the lattice parameters (*a*, *c*, and unit cell volume *V*) exhibited progressive expansion with increasing *x* (Fig. 3(d)), which is consistent with the observed peak shifts in the XRD patterns.

The relaxation of crystal structure refined by the XRD data was conducted, and the lowest energy state was achieved for BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 ceramics. To assess crystal structural changes, Table S3 in the ESM presents the optimized structural parameters of BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0 ≤ *x* ≤ 2) ceramics along with the standard deviation between the refined and relaxed structures. The relaxed structural parameters agreed well with the refined values, with standard deviations below 5%, confirming the reliability of the relaxation process. As depicted in Fig. 4 and Fig. S4 in the ESM, the energy band structure, total density of states (DOS), and electron localization function (ELF) were calculated. A similar calculated energy band structure could be observed in BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 ceramics, with a highly dispersed conduction band bottom and a nearly flat valence band top. For BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 ceramics, the band gap (*E*g) gradually decreased from 3.94 (*x* = 0) to 3.40 eV (*x* = 2.0) with increasing Ge4+ substitution, and the conduction band minimum was all located at the G-point, indicating their insulating behavior. Additionally, ELF analysis provides insights into bonding characteristics [56], and a high ELF corresponds to high electron localization [57]. The ELF distributions of [BaO12] decahedron, [MgO6] octahedron, [AlO4] tetrahedron, [SiO4] tetrahedron, and [GeO4] tetrahedron are presented in Figs. 4(b)−4(e). The Ba–O and Mg–O bonds (Figs. 4(b) and 4(c)) exhibited low ELF values of 0–0.1, indicating their ionic character. In contrast, higher ELF values for Si–O and Al–O bonds (Fig. 4(d)) indicated significant covalent character. Notably, the Ge–O bond (Fig. 4(e)) displayed even greater ELF values (0.4–0.5), demonstrating substantially stronger covalent character than both Si–O and Al–O bonds. The Ge4+ substitution for Si4+ could significantly enhance the relative covalency of the Si/Al–O bond.

### 3.2 Microwave dielectric properties, CTE, and structure–property relationships in BaMg2Al6Si9−<i>x</i>Ge<i>x</i>O30 (0.25 ≤ <i>x</i> ≤ 2) ceramics

The microwave dielectric properties and relative density (*ρ*rel) of BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2.0) ceramics sintered at their optimal temperatures are summarized in Table S4 in the ESM. Except for *x* = 0.25, all BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.50 ≤ *x* ≤ 2.0) ceramics exhibited high relative densities (> 95%), suggesting that Ge4+ substitution for Si4+ promoted densification. The experimental relative permittivity (*ε*r-exp) ranged from 5.37 to 6.07, while the *Q*×*f* values peaked at 32,351 GHz for *x* = 1.25. Notably, all compositions presented near-zero *τ*f values, indicating excellent temperature stability. The variation in microwave dielectric properties was correlated with the evolution of crystal structural parameters as a function of *x*.

The Phillips–van Vechten–Levine (P–V–L) theory, Clausius–Mossotti (C–M) equations, and bond valence theory were employed to elucidate the correlation between microwave dielectric properties and crystal structural parameters in BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2.0) ceramics. According to P–V–L theory, the *ε*r of a complex crystal can be derived by summing contributions from individual chemical bonds. For the hexagonal BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 system, the crystal structure was decomposed into binary crystal components. The binary crystals of osumilite-type BaMg2Al6Si9O30 were obtained in our previous work [54]. Based on P–V–L theory, the theoretical relative permittivity (*ε*r-PVL) of BaMg2Al6Si9O30 was calculated using Eqs. (3)–(5) [58,59]:

(3) εr−PVL=1+4πχ

(4) χ=Σμ⁡Fmnμχμ

(5) χμ=(ℏΩpμ)24π(Egμ)2

where *χ*μ represents the bond susceptibility; * Fmnμ is the fraction of bonds of type μ composing the actual crystal;  ΩPμ and  Egμ denote the plasma frequency and the average energy gap of the μ bond, respectively; Σχ*μ denotes the total susceptibility summation across all bonds.

As depicted in Fig. 5(a), the Ba–O bond exhibited the lowest Σ*χ*μ value among all bonds. For the BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 ceramics, the bond susceptibilities decreased in the following order: Σ*χ* (Si/Al(1)–O) > Σ*χ* (Al–O) > Σ*χ* (Mg–O) > Σ*χ* (Ba–O). Notably, the Si/Al(1)–O bonds contributed more than 66.8% of the total susceptibility, highlighting their dominant role in determining *ε*r-PVL.

Additionally, the relative permittivity of microwave dielectric ceramics was further evaluated via the C–M equation. The calculated relative permittivity (*ε*r-cal) of BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30l (0.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2.0) ceramics was calculated according to Eqs. (6) and (7) [60−62].

(6) αDT=α(Ba2+)+2α(Mg2+)+6α(Al3+)+(9−x)α(Si4+)+xα(Ge4+)+30α(O2−)

(7) εr−cal=1+2bαDT/Vm1−bαDT/Vm

where *α* and * αDT represent the ionic polarizability and total ionic polarizability, respectively; V*m represents the molecular volume.

As depicted in Fig. 5(b), the *ε*r-cal of BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2.0) ceramics ranged between 4.491 and 4.502, indicating remarkable stability. The *ε*r-cal values derived from C–M equations agreed better with the experimental results (*ε*r-exp) than the P–V–L predictions. However, a large deviation (20%–25%) between *ε*r-cal and *ε*r-exp existed, indicating that the large experimental relative permittivity may be associated with the crystal structure.

The crystal structural parameters can be calculated by bond valence theory, and the bond valence (*V*) and relative covalency (*r*c) of bonds can be obtained, as shown in Table S5 in the ESM. The *V*Ba ranged between +1.330 and +1.388, significantly lower than the theoretical values (+2). Similar bond valence deficiencies were observed for the Mg–O bonds, whereas the Al(2)–O and Si/Al(1)–O bonds maintained bond valence values that were consistent with the theoretical expectations. The deviation between the experimental and theoretical relative permittivity in BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2.0) ceramics was attributed to the “ratting effects” of Ba2+ and Mg2+, stemming from their bond valence deficiency. Additionally, the slight fluctuations in *ε*r-exp (Fig. 5(b)) with increasing *x* were related primarily to the variations in *ρ*rel, and the BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.5 ≤ *x* ≤ 1.75) ceramics with high *ρ*rel (> 95%) exhibited ultra-low relative permittivity (*ε*r ≤ 6). Although the P–V–L theory has limitations in predicting relative permittivity values for low-*ε*r ceramics, it successfully identifies the dominant role of Si–O bonds in determining the *ε*r of silicate-based materials.

Furthermore, the *Q*×*f* values of BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2.0) ceramics, as presented in Table S4 in the ESM and Fig. 6, gradually increased and then decreased with increasing *x*, reaching maximum values of 32,351 GHz at *x* = 1.25. The intrinsic dielectric loss of microwave dielectric ceramics can be evaluated via the far-IR reflectivity spectrum. The far-IR reflectivity spectra of BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2.0) ceramics were fitted using the Lorenz oscillator model and Fresnel equations (Eqs. (8)–(10)) [62,63].

where *ε**(*ω*) is the complex dielectric function; *ε*∞ denotes the dielectric constant from electronic polarization; *n* represents the number of transverse phonon modes; *γj* and *ω* correspond to the decay factor and transverse frequency, respectively; *ε′* and *ε″* are the real and imaginary parts of the complex permittivity.

As depicted in Figs. 6(a) and 6(b), similar far-IR reflectivity spectra can be observed for BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (*x* = 0.75 and 1.25) ceramics, indicating that they have the same crystal structure. The fitted curves (red solid lines) were in good agreement with the measured spectra. Based on extrapolated data fitted to the microwave frequency region, the fitted *ε′* at the microwave frequency (denoted by five-star points) closely matched the experimental relative permittivity. For BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 ceramics, the fitted *Q*×*f* values (*Q*×*f*-fitted) surpassed the experimental *Q*×*f* values, with a maximum *Q*×*f*-fitted of 40,426 GHz at *x* = 1.25. Both *Q*×*f* and *Q*×*f*-fitted followed similar trends as *x* increased (Fig. 6(c)), confirming that the *Q*×*f* values were controlled by the intrinsic dielectric loss. Notably, the intrinsic dielectric loss and *Q*×*f* values are influenced primarily by the crystal structural parameters, and the roundness (*σ*1) of [Si4Al(1)2O18] inner rings is considered to be an intrinsic factor affecting the *Q*×*f* values of BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2.0) ceramics, with a higher *σ*1 leading to higher *Q*×*f* values. A high *Q*×*f* value (32,351 GHz) was obtained at *x* = 1.25. Furthermore, the substitution of Ge4+ for Si4+ in BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2.0) ceramics could enhance the relative covalency (*r*c) of Si/Al(1)–O bonds, which is consistent with the first-principles calculation results (Fig. 4). However, the increase in *r*c (Si/Al(1)–O) could not explain the observed decrease in the *Q*×*f* values of BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (1.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2.0) ceramics. The average grain size (Fig. 2(h)) was identified as another extrinsic factor influencing the *Q×f* values. For the BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (1.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2.0) ceramics, the reduction in *σ*1 and the decrease in average grain size led to a deterioration in *Q×f* values, despite their high *r*c (Si/Al(1)–O) values.

Figure 7(a) displays the *τ*f values, *V*Si/Al(1), and *V*O(1) of BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2.0) ceramics, which presented self-near-zero *τ*f values (−8.69 ppm/°C ≤ *τ*f ≤ −5.46 ppm/°C). The nearest-zero *τ*f value of −5.46 ppm/°C was obtained at *x* = 2.0. For the BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 1.5) ceramics, the *τ*f values varied in close correlation with *V*Si/Al(1). A higher *V*Si/Al(1) ratio indicated greater rigidity in the chemical bonds, which contributed to enhancing the temperature stability. Consequently, the high *V*Si/Al(1) values were expected to correspond to near-zero *τ*f values. However, in BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (1.5 ≤ *x* ≤ 2.0) ceramics, *V*Si/Al(1) exhibited an inverse proportionality to the *τ*f values, indicating an anomalous trend. In our previous work, the *τ*f value was determined by the temperature coefficient of relative permittivity (*τ*ε) and CTE (*α*L), as described by *τ*f = − (*α*L + 0.5*τ*ε). For microwave dielectric ceramics with large |*τ*f| values (> 30 ppm/°C), the *τ*f values were predominantly governed by the *τ*ε values, whereas the influence of slight fluctuations in the CTE was negligible. Notably, the bond valence directly affected the *τ*ε values. In contrast, for BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 ceramics with near-zero *τ*f values, the effect of CTE on the *τ*f value was nonnegligible, where even minor variations significantly affected the *τ*f values. The observed increase in *τ*f values for BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (1.5 ≤ *x* ≤ 2.0) ceramics, coupled with the decrease in *V*Si/Al(1), might thus arise from corresponding changes in the CTE. As shown in Fig. 7(b), the BaMg2Al6Si7.75Ge1.25O30 (BMASG1.25) ceramics achieved the highest *Q×f* values alongside near-zero *τ*f values among all investigated BaMg2Al6Si9O30-based ceramics, including BaMg2+*x*Al6Si9O30+*x* (BMAS) [54] and Ba1−*x*Ca*x*Mg2Al6Si9O30 (BCMAS) ceramics [55]. These results demonstrate that substituting Si4+ with larger Ge4+ ions is the most effective strategy for optimizing the *Q×f* values of BaMg2Al6Si9O30-based ceramics.

Figure 8 shows the thermal expansion curves and corresponding CTE values at various measured temperatures. Notably, the large Ge4+ substitution for Si4+ significantly elongated the Si/Al(1)–O bond (Table S2 in the ESM) and reduced the Si/Al(1)–O(1)–Si/Al(1) angle (*σ*2) (Fig. 3), effectively suppressing the longitudinal vibrations of the O(1) atom along the *a* axis. Consequently, the negative thermal expansion peaks observed in the BaMg2Al6Si9O30 ceramics within the measured temperature range of 655 °C ≤ *T* ≤ 710 °C [54] were effectively inhibited. The thermal expansion curves of BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (1.0 ≤ *x* ≤ 2.0) ceramics confirmed the positive thermal expansion peaks in Figs. 8(a)−8(e). Within the operational temperature range, BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (1.0 ≤ *x* ≤ 2.0) ceramics presented ultra-low CTE values ranging from +1.07 to +1.44 ppm/°C, which could be attributed to the effective suppression of negative thermal expansion peaks. Notably, the BaMg2Al6Si7.75Ge1.25O30 (*x* = 1.25) ceramics achieved particularly ultra-low CTE values at −150 °C ≤ *T* ≤ 274 °C (Fig. 8(b)), surpassing the lowest values reported among typical low-CTE Mg2Al4Si5O18-based [64−67] and BaMg2Al6Si9O30-based [54] ceramics. Furthermore, BaMg2Al6Si7.75Ge1.25O30 (*x* = 1.25) combine these exceptional thermal properties with outstanding microwave dielectric performance, making it particularly promising for millimeter-wave and terahertz frequency applications.

## 4 Conclusions

In this work, dense BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2.0) solid-solution ceramics were successfully synthesized via conventional solid-state reaction methods. BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2.0) ceramics exhibited a single-phase composition, and their crystal structure was proven to be a hexagonal structure (*P*6/*mcc* space group). Large Ge4+ substitutions for Si4+ significantly elongated the Si/Al(1)–O bonds, reduced the Si/Al(1)–O(1)–Si/Al(1) angle, and optimized the roundness (*σ*1) of [Si4Al(1)2O18] inner rings while maintaining an ultra-low *ε*r in BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 ceramics. The *Q*×*f* values were found to correlate with the roundness (*σ*1) of the [Si4Al(1)2O18] inner rings. Additionally, self-near-zero *τ*f values (−8.69 ppm/°C ≤ *τ*f ≤ −5.46 ppm/°C) were obtained and were governed by both Si/Al(1)–O bond valence and the CTE. Notably, the elongation of the Si/Al(1)–O bond and decrease in the Si/Al(1)–O(1)–Si/Al(1) angle could effectively suppress the negative thermal expansion peaks in the BaMg2Al6Si9O30 ceramics, thereby decreasing the average CTE values and achieving ultra-low CTE values (+1.07 ppm/°C) at −150 °C ≤ *T* ≤ 274 °C. The ultra-low CTE contributed to the near-zero *τ*f values of the BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (0.25 ≤ *x* ≤ 2.0) ceramics. The strategic substitution of Si4+ with larger Ge4+ ions successfully achieved synergistic optimization of the *Q×f* and CTE values, yielding excellent microwave dielectric properties and ultra-low CTE values (*ε*r = 5.84, *Q*×*f* = 32,351 GHz, *τ*f = −7.27 ppm/°C, and CTE = +1.07 ppm/°C) in the BaMg2Al6Si9−*x*Ge*x*O30 (*x* = 1.25) ceramics, which highlighted its good application potential in high-frequency dielectric substrates.
