## Abstract

<p>Si<sub>3</sub>N<sub>4</sub> fiber-reinforced ceramic composites are promising candidates for high-temperature wave-transparent applications, yet the relationships among composition, interfacial characteristics, and mechanical performance remain inadequately understood. This study designs three kinds of composites, Si<sub>3</sub>N<sub>4f</sub>/BN, Si<sub>3</sub>N<sub>4f</sub>/SiO<sub>2</sub>, and Si<sub>3</sub>N<sub>4f</sub>/SiO<sub>2</sub>–BN, and systematically investigates their performances. The results reveal that the reaction between the BN precursors and silanol groups in the SiO<sub>2</sub> matrix during the fabrication of Si<sub>3</sub>N<sub>4f</sub>/SiO<sub>2</sub>–BN enhances the chemical compatibility between the fiber and matrix, promoting elemental interdiffusion and forming a thicker interfacial diffusion region. Consequently, the interfacial shear strength of Si<sub>3</sub>N<sub>4f</sub>/SiO<sub>2</sub>–BN is 1.86 and 2.35 times that of Si<sub>3</sub>N<sub>4f</sub>/BN and Si<sub>3</sub>N<sub>4f</sub>/SiO<sub>2</sub>, respectively. The stronger fiber-matrix bonding in Si<sub>3</sub>N<sub>4f</sub>/SiO<sub>2</sub>–BN suppresses fiber pull-out, whereas the weaker bonding in the other two composites permits it. Si<sub>3</sub>N<sub>4f</sub>/BN primarily exhibits fiber bundle pull-out, whereas Si<sub>3</sub>N<sub>4f</sub>/SiO<sub>2</sub> shows long single-fiber pull-out, indicating improved damage tolerance. In contrast, Si<sub>3</sub>N<sub>4f</sub>/SiO<sub>2</sub>–BN displays a typical brittle fracture behavior with minimal fiber pull-out and degraded mechanical properties. The excessive interfacial bonding, together with the thermal residual stress arising from the thermal expansion mismatch between the Si<sub>3</sub>N<sub>4</sub> fiber and the matrix, degrades the flexural and compressive strengths. Moreover, this excessive bonding restricts interfacial debonding and fiber pull-out, leading to a brittle fracture mode. Despite differences in interfacial microstructure, all three composites exhibit good dielectric properties. The use of SiO<sub>2</sub> and BN matrices effectively reduces both the dielectric constant and the dielectric loss tangent of Si<sub>3</sub>N<sub>4</sub> fiber-reinforced ceramic composites. These findings provide valuable insights into the design of high-temperature wave-transparent composites operated in extreme environments.</p>
