## 1 Introduction

With the increasingly complex international situation, aviation, and space vehicles such as high-Mach space vehicles and high-performance combat aircraft have become the main commanding heights in the competition of aerospace and aviation high-tech. The rudder surface of high Mach number space aircraft and the leading edge of the wing of high-performance combat aircraft are subjected to strong load impact, thermal impact, and external particle impact during service. The service temperature of these components is generally higher than 1200 ℃. At the same time, some high Mach number space vehicles are required to be reusable, so the oxidation resistance and ablative resistance of materials are required. SiC fiber reinforced SiC ceramic matrix composites (SiCf/SiC composites) retain the advantages of high-temperature resistance and corrosion resistance of SiC ceramics, and at the same time have the function of strengthening and toughening of SiC ceramic fiber, overcoming the inherent defects of low fracture toughness and poor external impact load resistance of SiC ceramics. The above structural characteristics make the SiCf/SiC composites possess excellent properties such as low density, high specific strength, high specific modulus, high-temperature resistance, oxidation resistance, ablation resistance, erosion resistance, thermal shock resistance, etc., making it increasingly key thermal structural materials supporting the development of aircraft [1−7].

At present, the research in the field of SiCf/SiC composites mainly focuses on the structural design and characterization of materials, the effect of preparation technology on the structure and intrinsic properties, and the evolution behavior of properties under a high-temperature oxidation environment. However, there is relatively little research on the synergistic oxidation and ablative behavior of reusable space vehicles in the service environment. The SiCf/SiC composites have excellent mechanical properties, but in the low-temperature domain (800–1200 ℃) oxidation ablative environment, the oxidation rate of the SiC ceramic matrix is relatively low, and the oxidation product SiO2 is in the form of powders, which are difficult to form a dense protective film to effectively fill the diffusion and penetration channels of oxidation ablative media, resulting in serious fiber damage. The phase structure of the SiC ceramic matrix changes from β-SiC to α-SiC when the temperature is higher than 1400 ℃, which seriously affects the structural stability of the SiCf/SiC composites. In addition, part of the reaction products escapes from the ceramic matrix under the strong oxidation and ablation environment, which also limits the structural stability of the materials to a certain extent [8−13].

Polymer precursors impregnated pyrolysis (PIP) is an important preparation process in the field of ceramic matrix composites for aerospace applications, which has the following obvious advantages: (i) The precursor component has strong designability, and the matrix design can be carried out according to the service environment of the component; (ii) the temperature of composite molding is low, so the damage to the fiber is relatively small; (iii) combined with the integrated design of the structure and function of the fiber preform, it can realize near net size molding of materials and low-cost development of large special-shaped components. Therefore, for the service environment of reusable space aircraft, B and Zr components are introduced into SiC ceramic matrix by the PIP process. On the one hand, the erosion resistance of the ceramic matrix composites is improved by exerting the high melting point effect of zirconium carbide and boride; on the other hand, the ablative performance of the ceramic matrix composites is enhanced by exerting the nailing effect of solid phases oxidation products such as zirconia and silicon oxide and the pore sealing effect of liquid phase oxidation products of borosilicate [14−16]. SiBCZr components are currently used for the preparation of carbon fiber reinforced ceramic matrix composites and thermal protective coatings, which are mainly used in short duration ablative environments (hundreds of seconds) or oxidation environments (thousands of seconds). The environmental stability of the ceramic matrix composites is mainly characterized by the ablation rate, but there are few studies on mechanical properties, especially the long-term mechanical stability of SiCf/SiBCZr composites after ablation [17−21].

In this study, the SiCf/SiBCZr composites were prepared by polymer precursor impregnation and pyrolysis (PIP) process using SiBCZr multiphase ceramic precursor as impregnating reagent and 2.5D preform from domestic near stoichiometric SiC fiber as reinforcement phase. In addition, the effects of the SiBCZr matrix on the oxidation resistance and ablative behavior of SiCf/SiBCZr composites were investigated, which laid the material foundation and technical support for the preparation of high-performance ceramic matrix composites.

### 2.2 Preparation of SiC<sub>f</sub>/SiBCZr composites

The SiCf/SiBCZr composites were prepared by the PIP process using the SiBCZr multiphase ceramic precursor as the impregnating reagent, the 2.5D preform from domestic near stoichiometric SiC fiber as a reinforcement phase, and pyrolytic carbon as an interface layer with a thickness of 0.20 μm prepared by the chemical vapor infiltration process. The fiber 2.5D preforms were impregnated in SiBCZr precursor solution for more than 10 h, and then pyrolyzed at 1200 ℃ for more than 30 min. The above steps of vacuum impregnation and high-temperature pyrolysis were repeated until the densification of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites was completed. The SiCf/SiBCZr composites were ablated to evaluate their environmental stability. The specific process route for the preparation of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites and SiCf/SC composites is shown in Fig.1.

### 2.3 Characterization of SiC<sub>f</sub>/SiBCZr composites

The multi-functional oxygen-acetylene ablation platform was used to imitate the service environment of hot-end components in the aviation and aerospace field. The flow rate of oxygen and acetylene was 900 and 180 L/h, respectively. The ablation temperature from 1200 to 1600 ℃ was controlled by automatic ablation distance movement. The bending strength of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites was measured by a three-point bending loading method on an universal test machine (WDW-5S, Jinan Testing Equipment IE Co.) according to the Chinese standard “GB/T 6659” with a test specimen size of 36 mm × 4 mm × 3 mm. The morphology and structure of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites were characterized by a scanning electron microscope (Quanta 200FEG, FEI), and the element composition of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites was tested by an energy spectrometer (Genesis XM-2, EDAX). The phases of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites after ablation at different temperatures were characterized by X-ray diffractometer (XRD; PANalytical X’Pert PRO, Panalytical).

### 3.1 Densification process of SiC<sub>f</sub>/SiBCZr composites

The 2.5D fiber preform of domestic near stoichiometric SiC fiber was impregnated with a mixture of SiBCZr multiphase ceramic precursor and xylene, and then densified in a vacuum pyrolysis furnace (HVP1000, ACME). The SiCf/SiBCZr composites were weighed after each impregnation−pyrolysis cycle to obtain the single-cycle weight gain and total weight gain shown in Fig. 2. The horizontal axis (*X*) represented the number of impregnation−pyrolysis cycles, and the vertical axis (*Y*) represented the weight gain rate.

In the early densification stage of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites (1st to 4th cycles), the weight gain trend became more and more obvious, and the single-cycle weight gain was between 8.0% and 15.0%. This was mainly because the pores in the fiber preformed at this stage generally existed in the form of through-holes, and the SiBCZr multiphase precursor solution had low impregnation resistance, which contributed to higher ceramic yield. As the densification continued, the internal pores in the SiC fiber preform were gradually filled and even partially closed. The SiBCZr multiphase ceramic precursor and xylene mixture experienced significantly increased resistance during the impregnation process, resulting in a decrease in the amount of SiBCZr multiphase ceramic precursor entering the SiC fiber preform and a gradual decrease in the densification efficiency. When the number of densification cycles ranged from 12 to 14, the mass gain per cycle was less than 2.0%, which was mainly because the SiBCZr precursor solution could only penetrate the surface of the materials, affecting the impregnation and densification efficiency. Predictably, if the densification cycle continued, the mass gain was largely due to the ceramic products produced by the precursor pyrolysis on the surface, which had very limited improvement in the structure and properties of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites.

### 3.2 Composition and structure characteristics of SiC<sub>f</sub>/SiBCZr composites after ablation

As shown in Fig. 3, the bending strength of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites before ablation was 571.5±38.1 MPa, and the long fiber bundles are pulled out successively, which can effectively exert the stress release mechanism and realize the strengthening and toughening shown. It is worth noting that the interface phase between the fiber and the matrix is partially attached to the fiber, indicating that cracks can be deflected at the interface junction during the process of structural failure caused by external load, which promotes the successive occurrence of fiber bridging and fiber fracture and the improvement of the mechanical properties of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites.

As shown in Fig. 5, the SiCf/SiBCZr composites remained intact after being ablated by oxygen-acetylene ablation flame at 1200 ℃/3600 s, and multiple wide “groove” streaks appeared on the surface of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites. The above structural characteristics were mainly attributed to the fact that the pyrolysis product of the SiBCZr multiphase precursor was attached to the uneven surface of the unmachined test piece during the later stage of preparation. After ablation, the SiBCZr multiphase ceramic surface was covered with molten materials, but the uneven state did not completely disappear. The ablative center and exterior were similar in morphology, both of which were covered by molten materials. The ablative surface mainly presents two types of convex and concave structures. Based on the element analysis, the composition of convex and concave structures in the center of the ablation was SiO2.62C0.73B0.17Zr0.12N0.28 and SiO1.22C0.47B1.30, and the composition of convex and concave structures in the exterior was SiO2.16C0.77B0.38Zr0.10N0.17 and SiO1.94C0.62B0.24, suggesting the ablation products for SiO2 and SiO2∙B2O3. As shown in Fig. 6, the XRD pattern of the ablation center of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites showed an obvious diffraction peak of SiO2 in Fig. 5. B2O3 and SiO2∙B2O3 are amorphous structures, so the diffraction peaks of the above two oxidation products did not appear on the XRD pattern.

At the initial stage of high-temperature ablation of SiBCZr multiphase ceramics, Si and B elements were oxidized to loose SiO2 and glassy B2O3, respectively. In this case, B2O3 has low viscosity and can be filled in loose SiO2 and spread on the surface. With the continuous ablative reaction, B2O3 and SiO2 further reacted to form SiO2∙B2O3 dense layer with high viscosity, glassy state, and low oxygen permeability, which effectively prevented the oxidation ablative media in the exterior layer and inner layer from further penetrating the matrix and fiber and formed an excellent protective barrier. In addition, a small amount of Zr elements appeared in the convex structure of the ablation center and the exterior, which was mainly attributed to the fact that the oxidation product ZrO2 was quickly covered by glassy B2O3 or SiO2∙B2O3 after the generation, preventing the oxidation reaction, so the detected content of Zr elements was very small [22−25].

The structure and composition of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites after ablation by the oxygen-acetylene flame at 1400 ℃/3600 s were shown in Fig. 7. Compared with the products after the oxygen-acetylene flame ablation at 1200 ℃/3600 s, the overall structure of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites remained intact, and the surface also showed “groove” structure streaks. The difference lay in the small number of cracks on the surface of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites, which might be attributed to the shrinkage caused by the rapid drop from 1400 ℃ to room temperature. The morphology of the ablative center and ablative exterior of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites were similar, both of which were covered by molten materials. The composition of convex and concave structures in the center of the ablation was SiO2.27C0.39Zr0.10N0.10 and SiO1.76C0.41B0.45N0.12, respectively, which meant that the main products were SiO2 with low saturated vapor pressure and a small amount of ZrSiO4 and ZrO2, forming a network structure with advantages of anti-erosion and anti-ablative. The composition of the convex and concave structures in the exterior was respectively SiO2.27C0.31Zr0.05N0.15 and SiO1.94C0.39B0.11N0.15, suggesting that the ablation products for SiO2 and SiO2∙B2O3, which could be considered as molten SiO2 and a small amount of SiO2∙B2O3 mixed oxides. Compared with the composition of the products after ablation by the oxygen-acetylene flame at 1200 ℃/3600 s, the contents of B and O decreased significantly, which was mainly attributed to the fact that in the scouring and ablation atmosphere of the strong oxygen-acetylene flame, the viscosity of B2O3 and part of SiO2∙B2O3 in the initial oxidation products of ablation decreased with the increase of the temperature, and then slowly escaped from the ceramic matrix. In this case, the SiO2 oxide layer of the oxidized product had suitable fluidity, which could fill the cracks in the surface layer, and the pores left by the escape of the low-viscosity materials and enabled SiBCZr multiphase ceramics to have excellent self-healing ability in the face of strong oxygen-acetylene flame scour and ablation. In the XRD pattern of the ablation center (Fig. 6), there were obvious SiO2 diffraction peaks and weak ZrSiO4 diffraction peaks, which were consistent with the element composition analyzed above.

In summary, the ablative product of SiCf/SiBCZr composites had a network structure composed of SiO2 and a small amount of ZrSiO4 and ZrO2, and a pore-sealing structure composed of molten SiO2 and a small amount of SiO2∙B2O3, which played a comprehensive role of solid phase napping and liquid phase sealing to provide self-healing, ablative resistance, and anti-erosion of SiCf/SiBCZr composites.

The structure and composition of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites after ablation by the oxygen-acetylene flame at 1600 ℃/3600 s were shown in Fig. 8. The results showed that the SiCf/SiBCZr composites exhibited a significantly molten structure after ablation, accompanied by a certain number of micron-sized pores, which were still covered by molten oxide. This was mainly attributed to the formation of pores due to the escape of some oxidation products in the form of gas components in the high-temperature ablative environment. At the same time, oxidation products with high viscosity and certain fluidity could continue to play a self-healing role to bridge the defects on the hole surface and form a dense protective film to inhibit the oxidation and ablation reaction. Therefore, the surface of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites had both the pore structure formed by gas escape and the dense structure covered by the molten oxide film. Based on the element analysis, the composition of convex and concave structures in the center of the ablation was SiO2.27C4.75Zr1.99 and SiO2..49C0.40N0.06, respectively, indicating that the main products were ZrO2 and ZrSiO4 with a low saturated vapor pressure. Compared with the products after ablation at 1400 ℃/3600 s, the content of ZrSiO4 increased significantly, which was mainly attributed to the fact that ZrSiO4 continuously generated by ZrO2 and SiO2 formed scour and ablative resistant network structure together with the increase of the reaction temperature. The composition of the convex and concave structures in the exterior was SiO3.07C0.88Zr0.88 and SiO2.07C0.40N0.15, which meant that the main ablative products were molten SiO2. At this time, almost all B2O3 or SiO2∙B2O3 in molten oxide escaped, resulting in the formation of micron-scale pores on the surface of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites. Meanwhile, molten SiO2 oxide spread to the surface of the micron-scale pores for bridging and filling. In the XRD pattern of the ablation center (Fig. 6), the diffraction peak of ZrSiO4 was strengthened to a certain extent, which might mean that the oxidation reaction between SiO2 and ZrO2 was accelerated.

### 3.3 Mechanical behavior of SiC<sub>f</sub>/SiBCZr composites after ablation

The center and exterior of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites with ablative conditions of 1200 ℃/3600 s, 1400 ℃/3600 s, and 1600 ℃/3600 s were processed into bending specimens (36 mm × 4 mm × 3 mm), then the three-point bending load method was used according to the Chinese standard “GB/T 6569-2006”, and each set of tests needed to take at least six valid values. The mechanical strength of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites is shown in Table 2, and the mechanical behavior under bending load is shown in Figs. 12−14. In addition, the bending strength of the SiC/SiC composites prepared by the same process was 571.5 MPa. The results showed that the strength retention rate of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites was higher than 90% and 85% after ablation at 1200 ℃/3600 s and 1400 ℃/3600 s, respectively. The above behavior was mainly attributed to the formation of a network structure with molten oxide as the sealing structure and solid oxide as the sealing structure during the high-temperature ablation process, which gave the advantages of self-healing oxidation resistance and scour resistance. In addition, the bending strength of the ablative outer zone was slightly higher than that of the ablative central zone, mainly due to the higher temperature and heat flux in the ablative central zone, which accelerated the ablative reaction and scour behavior.

The mechanical behaviors of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites with ablative conditions at 1200 ℃/3600 s were shown in Fig. 12. Results showed that the ablation center had similar mechanical behavior to the external after the ablation of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites at 1200 ℃/3600 s, meaning obvious pseudoplastic characteristics. The mechanical behavior of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites first entered the linear elastic stage under the action of external load without microcrack, fracture, and other microstructural damage. At this stage, the relationship between the load and the displacement was linear, which mainly depended on the relative value of the modulus of fiber and matrix, fiber content, fiber weaving mode, matrix structure, and other characteristics. When the external bending load continued to increase, the initial crack was generated in the matrix and gradually expanded, accompanied by matrix cracking, interface debonding, slippage, and other damage forms. According to the properties of the fiber and matrix, especially the degree of anisotropy, the relationship between the load and the displacement gradually changed from linear to nonlinear. If the external load exceeded the maximum carrying capacity of the SiC fiber, it would lead to the fiber de-bonding, fracture, and pull out step by step, and the character of step-down on the stress-strain curve. The above mechanical behavior meant that the bending strength and retention rate of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites after ablation at 1200 ℃/3600 s were up to 571.5 MPa and higher than 90%, respectively, which indicated that the ablation behavior mainly occurred on the surface and had little influence on the internal structural characteristics of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites.

The mechanical behaviors of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites with ablative conditions at 1400 ℃/3600 s were shown in Fig. 13. The mechanical behaviors were similar to those at 1200 ℃/3600 s ablation conditions, both of which showed the characteristics of gradual transition from the linear elastic stage to the stepped decline zone. At the same time, the mean values of the displacement change were similar between 0.30 and 0.40 mm when the two conditions reached the maximum load, which indicated that a load of fiber and matrix and the toughening effect of the interface phase were similar, that is, the ablative behavior had limited influence on the internal composition and structure of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites.

As shown in Fig. 14, when the ablative temperature increased to 1600 ℃, the mechanical behavior of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites also presented a typical pseudoplastic fracture behavior, indicating that the ablative medium had not penetrated the matrix completely, and the composition and structure of the interface phase remain intact. It indicated that when the SiCf/SiBCZr composites were in a high-temperature ablative medium, the matrix was first eroded, and the physical and chemical reactions occurred, leading to a certain degree of porosity in the matrix structure and affecting the load transfer ability in the ceramic matrix, which was manifested in the reduction of the maximum load and the reduction of the bearing displacement in the load–displacement curve.

### 3.4 Ablative mechanism analysis of SiC<sub>f</sub>/SiBCZr composites

The ablation process of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites involved a series of complex physical and chemical erosion behaviors. Physical erosion mainly referred to the impact of external loads dominated by the shear force on the surface of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites under the erosion of high-temperature gas, which was mainly determined by the strength of the ceramic matrix itself and the combination strength with the thermal protective coating. Chemical erosion referred to the multiphase chemical reaction that occurred on the surface of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites with an ablative medium, which was mainly affected by the chemical activity of the ceramic matrix composite itself and the environmental medium. Previous studies have shown that the matrix components mainly include BN, ZrB2, SiC, ZrC, and other substances. Through the analysis of chemical erosion reactions between the matrix components and the environmental media represented by abundant oxygen, the main chemical reactions are as Eqs. (1)–(7):

(1) BN+0.75O2(g)=0.5B2O3(l)+0.5N2(g)

(2) ZrB2+2.5O2(g)=ZrO2+B2O3(l)

(3) SiC+1.5O2(g)=SiO2+CO(g)

(4) SiC+2O2(g)=SiO2+CO2(g)

(5) SiC+O2(g)=SiO(g)+CO(g)

(6) ZrC+1.5O2(g)=ZrO2+CO(g)

(7) ZrC+2O2(g)=ZrO2+CO2(g)

Based on the above analysis, the relationship between the Gibbs free energy of the reaction and the temperature can be obtained (Fig. 15). In the temperature range from room temperature to 1600 ℃, the chemical reaction equations of SiC ceramic matrix components and O2 included Reactions (3)–(5), in which the Gibbs free energy of SiC ceramic components and O2 to produce SiO2 and CO2 was the lowest, corresponding to Reaction (4). Among all the reactions, the chemical reaction of the ZrB2 component with O2 to form ZrO2 and liquid B2O3 has the lowest Gibbs free energy, indicating the presence of ZrO2 and liquid B2O3 in the oxidation products, corresponding to Reaction (2). By comparing the Gibbs free energy of Reactions (6) and (7), the chemical reaction of ZrC components with O2 may preferentially occur in the form of Reaction (7), that is, the oxidation products are ZrO2 and CO2. The Gibbs free energy of the reaction of the BN component with O2 to form liquid B2O3 and N2 is higher than that of other reactions, that is, the reaction is more difficult, so the component boron element is preferred in the form of ZrB2, as seen in Reaction (2).

(1) Room temperature to 1200 ℃ stage: Combined with the analysis of components and structures in Figs. 5 and 6, the oxidation products mainly exist in the form of SiO2 and liquid B2O3, accompanied by a small amount of SiO2 and ZrO2. Liquid B2O3 was more active and less viscous than SiO2. At this time, the surface of SiO2 was likely to be covered with liquid B2O3, and the contact part of the two was in the form of SiO2∙B2O3. Liquid B2O3 would fill the gaps on the surface of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites and prevent the diffusion of oxygen into the interior, providing good antioxidant protection.

(2) 1200 to 1400 ℃ stage: Combined with the analysis of components and structures in Figs. 6 and 7, the rich liquid B2O3 with high activity gradually escaped in the form of gas, and the oxidation products mainly existed in the form of SiO2 and SiO2∙B2O3, while a small amount of high viscosity ZrSiO4 appeared. At this time, solid SiO2 and ZrSiO4 were formed as the network structure, and molten SiO2∙B2O3 and SiO2 were formed as the sealing structure. That is, the solid phase pinning function and liquid phase sealing function were combined to make the SiCf/SiBCZr composites have both self-healing anti-oxidation and anti-erosion properties.

(3) 1400 to 1600 ℃ stage: Combined with the analysis of components and structures in Figs. 6 and 8, the oxidation products were mainly composed of solid SiO2 and ZrSiO4 with higher viscosity, among which ZrSiO4 had stronger anti-erosion ability and became the main skeleton structure. Original liquid B2O3 and SiO2∙B2O3 in the matrix partly escaped in gaseous form, resulting in some holes in the matrix, which was very unfavorable to the maintenance of material properties.

## 4 Conclusions

(1) SiCf/SiBCZr composites exhibited excellent ablative properties between 1200 and 1400 ℃. After the ablative tests at 1200 ℃/3600 s and 1400 ℃/3600 s, the strength retention rates of the composites were higher than 90% and 85%, respectively, and the mechanical behaviors showed typical pseudoplastic characteristics. At this time, the nailing effect of solid phase oxidation products and the sealing effect of liquid phase oxidation products were combined to improve the self-healing anti-oxidation and anti-scouring properties of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites.

(2) When the ablation condition increased to 1600 ℃/3600 s, the mechanical strength of the SiCf/SiBCZr composites decreased obviously, which was mainly attributed to the loose matrix structure caused by the escape of the ablative products in the form of small molecules and the decrease of load transfer capacity of the matrix. The appearance of pores, cracks, and other defects in the SiCf/SiBCZr composites led to the intensification of the ablative reaction, which affected the structural stability and bearing capacity.
