## Abstract

Olivine-type A2BO4 ceramics possess a low permittivity (\u03B5r) and low dielectric loss, contributing to a high quality factor (Q\u00D7f). However, the large negative temperature coefficient of the resonant frequency (\u03C4f) restricts their practical applications. In this work, we prepared a series of CaYGa1\u2212xAlxO4 (x = 0\u20131) ceramics with low \u03B5r values via solid-state sintering. By controlling the evolution of orthorhombic olivine to the tetragonal perovskite-like structure (K2NiF4) and the ordered regulation of the A-site, an ultralow loss (tan\u03B4 = 1.38\u00D710\u22124, Q\u00D7f = 125,530 GHz, and f = 17.3 GHz for x = 0) and a nearly zero temperature coefficient (\u03C4f = \u22120.5 ppm\/\u00B0C for x = 0.9) were achieved. The significant changes in the dielectric properties (\u03B5r = 8.3\u201316.2, Q\u00D7f = 125,530\u201350,660 GHz, and \u03C4f = \u221250.9 to 2.9 ppm\/\u00B0C) of these ceramics are primarily influenced by the characteristics of the second phase, ion polarization, ion order and disorder, and the chemical bonds resulting from structural evolution. Furthermore, a cylindrical dielectric resonator antenna (CDRA) was designed using an ultralow-loss CaYGaO4 ceramic, achieving a high gain (5.36\u20136.15 dBi) and efficiency (\u0026gt; 90%) in the bandwidth region (5.065\u20135.747 GHz), thereby enhancing the efficiency and quality of 5G communications. This work advances the development of control strategies for high-performance dielectric ceramics and dielectric resonator antennas in high-frequency communications.
