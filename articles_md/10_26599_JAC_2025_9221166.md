## 1 Introduction

Flash sintering (FS) is a novel field-assisted sintering technique (FAST) in which ceramic materials are simultaneously subjected to a critical electric field and furnace temperature [1]. This method gives rise to a series of unique physical phenomena, including rapid densification, conductivity surge, and visible luminescence [1−4]. Since its first report in 2010, yttria-stabilized zirconia (YSZ) ceramics—particularly 3 mol% (3YSZ) and 8 mol% (8YSZ)—have been widely studied as models in flash sintering [1,5−7]. To date, flash sintering has been successfully expanded to multiple ceramic systems [8]. However, the underlying mechanism governing the onset of flash sintering is still not well understood, significantly limiting further advancements in this technique.

Several mechanisms have been proposed to explain the onset of flash sintering, primarily based on Joule heating and electric-field-induced defect formation [6]. Raj and coworkers [1,9,10] suggested that the formation and avalanche of Frenkel defects under a high electric field play a key role in initiating flash sintering. Furthermore, Gao *et al*. [11] studied the electrical conductivity of dense 3YSZ during the incubation stage and reported that flash onset is related to the extra oxygen vacancies induced by the high electric field. This claim was later confirmed by Liu and coworkers [12,13]. In particular, the transition of YSZ from purely ionic to mixed conductivity with the contributions of oxygen vacancies and internal reactions is a compelling mechanism for the onset of flash sintering [14−18]. Additionally, the transition of ceramic materials from insulators to conductors during flash sintering is similar to that during dielectric breakdown [19,20]. This finding indicates that the onset of flash sintering may be dominated by dielectric breakdown under high electric field and temperature.

Alternatively, several researchers have proposed a thermal runaway model based on Joule heating to illustrate the onset of flash sintering [21−23]. During the incubation stage, a weak current flows through the sample, gradually increasing its temperature due to Joule heating. When the heat generation surpasses the rate of heat dissipation, the electrical conductivity of the sample increases because of its negative temperature coefficient. This increased electrical conductivity causes an increase in the current passing through the sample and generates more Joule heating, ultimately resulting in thermal runaway—a process widely believed to trigger the onset of flash sintering [22]. In addition, it has been suggested that local grain boundary melting is the underlying reason for the onset of flash sintering [1,24]. Compared with the bulk material, the significantly higher electrical resistance at the grain boundaries generates localized Joule heating, which induces interfacial melting. This melting enhances the overall conductivity of the sample, ultimately leading to the onset of flash sintering and rapid densification [24]. The feature of localized Joule heating is the formation of hot spots, which results in undesired sintering of the sample during flash sintering (melting, pore, and sample damage) [25]. Clearly, the coupling of the electric field and temperature makes it difficult to gain insights into the onset mechanism of flash sintering.

Recent studies have shown that introducing conductive phases can significantly lower the onset temperature of flash sintering [26]. For example, Fele *et al*. [26] reported that the onset temperature of flash sintering an 8YSZ/graphene nanoplatelet composite is lower than that of pure 8YSZ under the same electric field. Similarly, Xiao *et al*. [27] reported that flash sintering of 3YSZ could be realized at room temperature in an air environment by adding reduced graphene oxide into the 3YSZ matrix. These findings indicate that the presence of conductive phases reduces the onset temperature of flash sintering, which may be related to the increase in conductivity. Furthermore, several studies have demonstrated that the initial particle size and neck formation also significantly influence the onset temperature of flash sintering [28−30]. Smaller particle sizes and accelerated necking formation before flash sintering contribute to a reduction in the onset temperature. This reduction is attributed primarily to the enhanced conductivity resulting from the presence of finer particles and the promotion of particle necking. Raj [31] reported a common feature across various ceramic systems: The transition from linear to nonlinear conductivity during flash sintering typically occurs within a narrow range of power densities (10–50 mW/mm3), suggesting that conductivity plays a key role in initiating flash sintering.

Unlike YSZ, ultrahigh-purity Al2O3, which is a strong electrical insulator, is generally resistant to flash sintering. However, flash sintering of 0.25 wt% MgO-doped Al2O3 has been achieved under an electric field of 500 V/cm at 1320 °C [32]. Similar results were reported by Biesuz and Sglavo [33] for 99.8 wt% pure Al2O3. These findings suggest that mixing Al2O3 with specific components can significantly reduce the critical conditions required for flash sintering, enabling the process under more moderate electric fields and temperatures [34,35].

Naik *et al*. [36] first reported the flash sintering of Al2O3–3YSZ composites in 2014. Under a constant electric field of 150 V/cm, 3YSZ exhibited flash sintering at ~750 °C, whereas pure Al2O3 showed no flash phenomenon even when the furnace temperature reached ~1400 °C. The onset temperature for the Al2O3–3YSZ composites was between those of 3YSZ and pure Al2O3 under the same electric field conditions. Since this initial report, several investigations have focused on the flash sintering behavior and microstructural evolution of Al2O3/YSZ composites [37−46]. These results have consistently demonstrated that incorporating a highly conductive ceramic phase, such as YSZ, into an electrically insulating ceramic matrix, such as Al2O3, significantly reduces the electric field/temperature required for flash sintering. For example, no flash phenomenon was observed in Al2O3–MgO composites at 500 V/cm and ~1400 °C, whereas composites with 50 vol% 8YSZ successfully flashed under a lower electric field of 300 V/cm at a furnace temperature of 950 °C [47]. Furthermore, under a constant electric field of 500 V/cm, the onset temperature of flash sintering for Al2O3–MgO composites decreased from 1014 to 817 °C when the 8YSZ content increased from 30 to 50 vol% [48]. These findings suggest that YSZ acts as a catalytic agent, promoting the onset of flash sintering in Al2O3/YSZ composite ceramics [49].

Notably, the onset of flash sintering in Al2O3/YSZ composites requires the YSZ content to exceed a critical volume fraction *v*c, known as the percolation threshold—the minimum conductive phase fraction needed to form a continuous current path in insulator–ionic conductor composites [47,48]. M’Peko [41] investigated flash sintering behavior in Al2O3–3YSZ composites with different mixing ratios under an electric field of 150 V/cm and reported a critical volume fraction of approximately 20%. Similar findings were reported for Al2O3–8YSZ by Biesuz and coworkers [43]. Across all the studies, flash sintering could not be triggered under a fixed electric field when the YSZ content was below the percolation threshold. These results demonstrated that the formation of a percolation network in YSZ is closely related to the onset mechanism of flash sintering in Al2O3-based composites. However, insulator–ionic conductor composites do not follow the percolation threshold model when the content of the conductive phase is too low [50]. Hence, two questions need to be considered: (i) Can flash sintering of Al2O3/YSZ composites be triggered when the content of the conductive phase is far below the percolation threshold? (ii) What is the relationship between the phase content and the onset mechanism of flash sintering in Al2O3/YSZ composites?

In this study, we investigated the effects of compositional changes on the onset temperature of flash sintering in Al2O3–8YSZ composites to better understand the relationship between the phase content and the onset mechanism of flash sintering. Our results reveal that the onset mechanism of flash sintering of Al2O3–8YSZ composites changes with the phase content, providing further evidence that the flash sintering of Al2O3 and 8YSZ shares a different onset mechanism.

### 2.2 Experiments

The flash-sintering experiments were conducted in a modified box furnace (KSL-1700X-A1, KJ Group, China). The green bodies were placed at the center of the box furnace with high-purity Pt wires. Two types of flash sintering experiments were performed in this study. First, a constant electric field of 900 V/cm was applied to the green bodies at room temperature via a direct-current (DC) power supply (APS DCP1200V-1A, Adaptive Power System, USA), and the green bodies were heated at a rate of 10 °C/min until flash sintering occurred. After the current density rapidly reached the preset value of 20 mA/mm2, the power supply transitioned from voltage control to current control. After operating in current-control mode for a certain time, the power supply was turned off, and the samples were cooled to room temperature at a cooling rate of 5 °C/min. The furnace temperature at which flash sintering occurred was referred to as the onset temperature. Second, to calculate the conductivity (*σ*) of samples in the stable stage of flash sintering, constant-temperature flash sintering experiments were designed. The green bodies were heated at a rate of 10 °C/min to a constant temperature (800 and 1200 °C). After holding for 10 min at these temperatures, an electric field of 700 V/cm was applied to the green bodies. When the current density reached the preset value of 70 mA/mm2, sintering was maintained for 2 min before the power supply was turned off, and the sample was cooled to room temperature at a cooling rate of 5 °C/min. The conductivity of the samples in the stable stage of flash sintering was calculated via the equation *σ = J*/*E* where *E* is the electric field in the stable stage, and *J* is the preset value of the current density.

### 2.3 Characterizations

The phase compositions of the samples were determined via an X-ray diffractometer (XRD; Empyrean, PANalytical, the Netherlands) with Cu Kα (*λ* = 1.54 Å) radiation. The microstructure of the samples was observed from the polished surface (covered with an Au layer) via a field-emission scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM; Sigma 500, ZEISS, Germany).

Furthermore, as shown in Table 1, the onset temperature for flash sintering of the Al2O3–8YSZ composites increased with increasing Al2O3 content under a constant electric field. Specifically, the onset temperature increased from 335 to 1548 °C as the Al2O3 content increased from 0 to 99 mol%, which is similar to previous reports [41,43]. Interestingly, this study reveals novel and previously unreported observations: Flash sintering of Al2O3–8YSZ composites can occur even with only 1 mol% (0.9 vol%) 8YSZ under a constant electric field of 900 V/cm. This concentration is far below the percolation threshold of 8YSZ content required for the onset of flash sintering of Al2O3–8YSZ composites reported in Ref. [43]. Clearly, the percolation threshold model alone cannot be responsible for the flash sintering of insulator–ionic conductor binary systems. This suggests that when the content of 8YSZ is below the percolation threshold, the onset of flash sintering for the Al2O3–8YSZ composites is dominated by other mechanisms.

### 4.1 Flash sintering of Al2O3-based composites

The flash sintering of Al2O3-based (MgO, 3YSZ, 8YSZ, YAG, and LaPO4) composites has been reported in many studies [32,36,46,53,54], reporting an interesting common finding that flash sintering can be initiated at relatively low furnace temperatures and electric fields when an easy-flash phase is added into the difficult-flash phase or when two difficult-flash phases are mixed. For example, single-phase Al2O3 or LaPO4 cannot flash at a furnace temperature of 1450 °C under a high electric field of 1130 V/cm [54]. However, the Al2O3–50 vol% LaPO4 composites were able to flash at a furnace temperature of 1450 °C under a lower electric field of 1080 V/cm (Table 2). One reasonable hypothesis is that the heterointerfaces of multiphase materials play a key role in the initiation of flash sintering [54]. Additionally, it is believed that the onset of flash sintering of composites is related to the phase with good conductivity [48].

The Al2O3/YSZ (3YSZ and 8YSZ) composite is a typical model used to understand the onset mechanism of flash sintering. M’Peko *et al*. [41] estimated the activation energy of Al2O3–3YSZ composites on the basis of current density during the incubation stage via Eq. (1):

(1) J=J0exp(−ΔE/(kBT))

where *J*0 is the preexponential factor; Δ*E* is the activation energy (eV); *k*B is the Boltzmann constant (5.67×10−8 W·m−2·K−4); *T* is the absolute temperature (K). The average value of the activation energy was measured to be 1.51±0.05 eV, which is comparable to the Δ*E* value of 3YSZ (1.48 eV). The onset of flash sintering of the Al2O3–3YSZ composites is closely related to 3YSZ. Based on these results, they assumed that the mechanism leading to charge transport was driven by oxygen vacancy (* VO⋅⋅) defects generated from the reaction: Y2O3 → 2 YZr′ + VO⋅⋅ + 3OX in YSZ [41]. Furthermore, Cao et al*. [55] estimated the activation energy of Gd2O3/m-ZrO2/8YSZ composites via the same equation and obtained a similar value as that reported by M’Peko *et al*. [41]. The activation energy was approximately 1.63 eV for the composites with 8YSZ contents greater than 50 wt%. Moreover, they reported that the activation energy was 2.69 eV for composites with 8YSZ contents lower than 25 wt%, implying a change in the transport mechanism due to the changes in 8YSZ content.

On the basis of these analyses, it can be concluded that the 8YSZ content has two effects on the flash sintering of the Al2O3–8YSZ composites: (i) An increase in the 8YSZ content reduces the onset temperature of flash sintering in the Al2O3–8YSZ composites, and (ii) a critical value for the 8YSZ content exists, which represents a change in the onset mechanism of flash sintering when the 8YSZ content is above or below this value. These results are systematically discussed in Sections 4.2–4.4).

Notably, the addition of small amounts of Al2O3 (56,57]. The formation of continuous conductive pathways at lower furnace temperatures ultimately reduces the onset temperature of flash sintering. However, given that the minimum alumina content in this study reaches 20 mol%, it can be inferred that the intrinsic properties of the Al2O3 phase exert a greater influence on the conductivity of the Al2O3–8YSZ composite than does its effect on the conductivity via changes in the particle neck. Furthermore, in region I (0–62.5 mol%), the increase in the Al2O3 content does not seem to influence the onset temperature of flash sintering of the composites. 8YSZ, an ionic conductor, easily undergoes flash sintering [58]. When the Al2O3 content was 62.5 mol%, 8YSZ formed a uniformly distributed network (Fig. 3(b)), offering a good conduction path for flash sintering. Hence, the change in the Al2O3 content in region I has little effect on the onset temperature due to the conduction path of the 8YSZ phases. This can also be inferred from the fact that the temperature does not play a leading role in the onset of flash sintering, and the athermal effect of the electric field determines the flash sintering of the Al2O3–8YSZ composites [11,59].

Cross et al*. [49] analyzed the mechanism of the catalytic effect of 8YSZ on the flash sintering of Al2O3 in a special layered structure (8YSZ and Al2O3 samples were stacked in a layered configuration). Under an electric field of 100 V/cm and a temperature of ~800 °C, the 8YSZ layer initially underwent flash sintering, followed by rapid propagation to the adjacent Al2O3 layer. Notably, pure Al2O3 is incapable of flash sintering under identical conditions. These authors suggested that Joule heating, dielectric breakdown, and ionic migration are not sufficient to explain the phenomenon of flash sintering from 8YSZ to Al2O3, which is likely related to the electron migration of 8YSZ in the flash sintering state [14−17].

In our previous study, an asymmetric temperature distribution of dense 8YSZ ceramics during flash sintering was observed, which was attributed to an electric field-driven electrochemical reaction [13]. The process involves the formation of oxygen vacancies and electrons in the anode and the generation of holes in the cathode according to Reactions (2) and (3) [18]:

(2) Anode:OO→12O2+VO⋅⋅+2e′

(3) Cathode:12O2+VO⋅⋅→OO+2h⋅

Furthermore, the onset mechanism of flash-sintered dense 8YSZ was found to be linked to the internal reaction and the transition from ionic to mixed electrical conductivity under the effect of a high electric field [18].

Building on their findings, we postulate, as shown in Fig. 4, that the onset of flash sintering of the Al2O3–8YSZ composites in region I is 8YSZ-dominated flash sintering [39]. Applying a high electric field to the Al2O3–8YSZ composites may lead to internal reactions inside the 8YSZ phase and the formation of a transient p-i-n structure at the incubation stage, which further results in the onset of flash sintering [16,18,60].

### 4.3 Percolation-activated flash sintering

A continuous increase in the fraction of the insulating phase may lead to some conducting phases becoming isolated and therefore being unable to contribute to the conduction of insulator–ionic conductor composites. Hence, the conduction of composites depends on the path formed by the partially conducting phases [61]. The minimum fraction of conducting phases needed to form a continuous conduction path is the percolation threshold [43,47].

In region II (62.5–80 mol%), the onset temperature of the composites increases dramatically with increasing Al2O3 content. When the Al2O3 content reached 80 mol%, the conduction path formed by 8YSZ was blocked by Al2O3 (Fig. 3(c)). Additionally, Al2O3 and ZrO2 are immiscible, i.e., there is no diffusion of zirconium and oxygen ions into Al2O3 [49,62]. Notably, the 8YSZ content of 20 mol% is also in good agreement with the percolation threshold [43]. This finding indicates that the onset of flash sintering of the Al2O3–8YSZ composites in region II is related to the percolation model [41,47,48], which is called percolation-activated flash sintering. The formation of percolation paths offers a good conduction path for current passing through 8YSZ. This indicates that the 8YSZ phases may become a heat source to heat the adjacent Al2O3, which further triggers the onset of flash sintering of the Al2O3–8YSZ composites. Thereafter, the percolating networks formed by the conductive 8YSZ in the green bodies determine whether the flash sintering of the Al2O3–8YSZ composites can be triggered. Furthermore, the conductivity *σ* of a sample decreases with increasing Al2O3 content. However, the sample conductivity *σ* increases with increasing temperature because of the negative temperature coefficient of the ceramics. Since *P* = *E*2*σ*, under a constant electric field, a higher temperature is necessary to increase *σ* to reach the power dissipation required for the onset of flash sintering (10–50 mW/mm3). Hence, the onset temperature rapidly increases from 410 to 1320 °C when the Al2O3 content increases from 62.5 to 80 mol%.

### 4.4 8YSZ-catalyzed flash sintering of Al2O3

In region III (80–99 mol%), the onset temperature increases further with increasing Al2O3 content, albeit at a lower rate than that of region II. Figure 3(d) presents the microstructure of the flash-sintered sample with an Al2O3 content of 99 mol%. 8YSZ is clearly distributed in the Al2O3 matrix in isolation. Clearly, the 8YSZ-dominated and percolation-activated flash sintering cannot be responsible for the initiation of flash sintering of the Al2O3–8YSZ composites in this content range. Al2O3 is extremely difficult to flash sinter because of its poor conductivity [32]. By adding a conductive phase (such as 8YSZ or MgO) to Al2O3, the conductivity can be increased to facilitate flash sintering [54,63,64]. Biesuz *et al*. [19] proposed that the flash sintering of Al2O3 results in highly similar behavior to dielectric breakdown. These processes share a common underlying mechanism, where electrical conductivity plays a crucial role. Notably, the electric field strength used in this study was much lower than the dielectric breakdown threshold of Al2O3 [19]. This occurred because the breakdown strength strongly depends on the furnace temperature, porosity, and powder purity. As shown in Fig. 2, under a constant electric field, a furnace temperature of 1700 °C did not induce flash sintering in Al2O3, suggesting that high-purity Al2O3 is highly resistant to breakdown under these conditions. However, increasing the furnace temperature or introducing a second phase can increase the conductivity of Al2O3, thereby reducing the conditions required for breakdown. Here, under a fixed electric field, incorporating 1 mol% 8YSZ into Al2O3 decreased the furnace temperature of breakdown from > 1700 to 1548 °C. Hence, in region III, we proposed that flash sintering originated from Al2O3, whereas 8YSZ acted as a conductive phase to increase the overall electrical conductivity of the samples, which catalyzed the flash sintering of Al2O3. In this process, the temperature determines the onset of flash sintering of the Al2O3–8YSZ composite, which is induced by dielectric breakdown at high temperatures [19].

On the basis of the above discussions, the onset mechanism of flash sintering of the Al2O3–8YSZ composites changes with the phase content. The Al2O3–8YSZ composites with low Al2O3 contents exhibit defect-dominated flash sintering associated with 8YSZ, whereas those with high Al2O3 contents follow a thermally controlled mechanism. The variation in mechanism with composition is due mainly to the flash sintering of single Al2O3 and 8YSZ phases, which share a different onset mechanism.

### 4.5 Abnormal conductivity

To further reveal the effect of an electric field on the conductivity of the Al2O3–8YSZ composites with and without an electric field, we measured the conductivity of the stable stage in the flash sintering of the Al2O3–8YSZ composites. Figures 5(a) and 5(b) show the variation in the electric field and current density with time for the Al2O3–8YSZ composites under the same flash conditions. Although there may be current localization, the conductivity at this stage can default to the conductivity of the overall sample.

*
Fig. 5Variation in (a) electric field and (b) current density with time for Al2O3–8YSZ composites under an electric field of 700 V/cm at a furnace temperature of 1200 °C. (c) Conductivity against content of conductive phase for insulator–conductor composites in Scarisbrick model [61]. (d) Conductivity of stable stage varies with 8YSZ content for flash-sintered Al2O3–8YSZ composites at a constant furnace temperature under an electric field of 700 V/cm.

The conductivity of insulator–conductor composites is usually estimated by the Scarisbrick model [61]. As shown in Fig. 5(c), the conductivity of the mixtures increases with increasing content of the conductive phase. This result can also be confirmed by the law of mixing: σ* = *V*1*σ*1 + *V*2*σ*2, where *V*1 and *V*2 represent the volume fractions of the conductor and insulator (*V*1 + *V*2 = 1), respectively, and *σ*1 and *σ*2 correspond to the conductivities of individual phases. Furthermore, M’Peko *et al*. [50] demonstrated that the relationship between the 3YSZ content and conductivity of Al2O3–3YSZ composites conforms to the Scarisbrick model. Consequently, we believe that the conductivity of Al2O3–8YSZ composites without exposure to an electric field also follows this model.

Figure 5(d) presents the conductivity of the stable stage varying with 8YSZ content for flash-sintered Al2O3–8YSZ composites at a constant furnace temperature under an electric field of 700 V/cm. The onset of flash sintering requires the combination of a critical electric field and a critical temperature [1]. As shown in Fig. 5(d), the electric field and temperature do not reach the critical value to trigger flash sintering of Al2O3–8YSZ composites with 8YSZ contents lower than 20 mol%; however, it initiates when the 8YSZ content further increases to 30 mol%. The Scarisbrick model indicates that the conductivity of the Al2O3–8YSZ composites increases with 8YSZ content at a constant temperature. However, the conductivity of the stable stage of the flash-sintered Al2O3–8YSZ composites does not follow this model within a certain range of 8YSZ contents. At a constant temperature of 800 °C, the conductivity of the stable stage of the flash-sintered Al2O3–30 mol% 8YSZ composites is equal to that of the flash-sintered Al2O3–37.5 mol% 8YSZ composites. This equivalence persists even at 1200 °C, indicating that the electric field disrupts the composition–conductivity relationship predicted by the classical mixed conductivity theory. This is because, in the absence of an electric field, the conduction behavior of the Al2O3–8YSZ composites is dominated by the intrinsic conductivity of the two phases. Therefore, the conductivity of the composites increases with the volume fraction of the conductive phase (Fig. 5(c)). However, after the application of a high electric field induces flash sintering, the 8YSZ phase transitions from an ionic conductor to an electronic conductor, resulting in mixed ionic-electronic conductivity [14,16,17]. Hence, it can be concluded that the mixed conductivity of the Al2O3–8YSZ composites under an electric field is significantly different from that without an electric field.

## 5 Conclusions

In this study, Al2O3–8YSZ composites with varying molar ratios were used to systematically investigate the onset mechanism of flash sintering. The onset mechanism of flash sintering of the Al2O3–8YSZ composites is dependent on composition, i.e., when the Al2O3 content was 0–62.5 mol%, flash sintering of the Al2O3–8YSZ composites was dominated by 8YSZ due to the electric-field-induced internal electrochemical reaction, so the onset temperature was not sensitive to the variation in the Al2O3 content. However, with an Al2O3 content of 62.5–80 mol%, the onset of flash sintering could be explained in terms of the percolation model, which also resulted in a significant increase in the onset temperature. Notably, when the Al2O3 content exceeded 80 mol%, 8YSZ increased the overall electrical conductivity of the samples, thus promoting flash sintering of Al2O3 through interfacial dielectric breakdown. In summary, the onset of flash sintering of the Al2O3–8YSZ composites with low Al2O3 contents is related to defect-dominated flash sintering associated with 8YSZ, whereas that of the composites with high Al2O3 contents follows a thermally controlled mechanism.
