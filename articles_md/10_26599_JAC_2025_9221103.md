## 1 Introduction

Ceramic dielectric capacitors are the core components used to store energy in electronic power devices. Because power systems and electronic devices have to operate in harsh environments, for example, at high temperatures, the inferior temperature stability of energy storage for dielectric capacitors has greatly hindered their practical application [1]. Among several dielectric materials used in energy storage, antiferroelectric (AFE) ceramic materials have gained intensive attention because of their unique double polarization hysteresis loops and antiparallel dipoles. In addition, because of the high specific capacity and small volume of the AFE ceramic-based capacity, it is possible to apply a wave filter that replaces the aluminum capacity, which hinders the miniaturization and integration of devices [2,3].

The total energy storage density (*W*tot), recoverable storage density (*W*rec), and energy storage efficiency (*η*) are important criteria for evaluating the energy storage performance of AFE ceramics and are based on the maximum polarization (*P*max), remanent polarization (*P*r), and external electric field (*E*). Based on the *P–E* hysteresis loops at different *E*, these variables can be calculated using Eqs. (1)–(4):

(1) Wrec=∫PrPmaxEdP

(2) Wtot=∫0PmaxEdP

(3) Wloss=Wrec−Wtot

(4) η=WrecWtot×100%

From Eq. (1), to achieve an optimum energy storage performance, improving the breakdown strength (BDS) of materials is important. However, at high temperatures, the BDS decreases with increasing temperature, and the application of AFE ceramic capacitors under extreme conditions is limited by the degradation of their dielectric properties at high temperatures. Therefore, the instability of modern AFE ceramic capacitors at high temperatures is an urgent problem that must be addressed [2]. For example, Luo *et al*. used Sr2+ to replace Pb2+, which increased *W*rec of Pb (Zr0.54Sn0.46)0.975Nb0.02O3 ceramics to 8.95 J/cm3 [4]; at only 85 °C, *W*rec decreases to approximately 6 J/cm3. Recent research has shown that at high temperatures (> 120 °C), the oxygen vacancy conductance of BNT-based ceramics increases greatly, resulting in a sharp increase in dielectric loss, which further leads to deterioration of energy storage performance [5]. The high-temperature dielectric performance is limited by the loss caused by a sharp increase in the conductance. Hence, how to solve the nonlinear increase in conduction caused by thermal and electrical fields is the key to achieve high-temperature capacitive energy storage.

To obtain AFE ceramics with high-temperature performance, recent reports have suggested AFE ceramics with doping elements and composite polymers. Because the B–O octahedron can withstand a greater degree of torsion in the temperature field, reports generally choose doping at the B-site to reduce the effect of temperature. Chen *et al*. [5] designed PN-based ceramics and decreased *W*rec (13.39–8.48 J/cm3) from room temperature (RT) to 180 °C by doping Fe3+ and Yb3+ into the B-site. However, the motion displacement of the B-ion delays the AFE–FE phase transition, resulting in a high phase conversion electric field, and the low breakdown field strength makes it difficult to show good energy storage performance [6−9]. For lead-free ceramics, other components are mostly combined to improve the high-temperature energy storage materials. Guo *et al*. [10−12] designed a composition of 0.9NN–0.1BF, and *W*rec was 4.43 J/cm³. In addition, stable energy storage performance was maintained over a wide temperature range (20–120 °C). On the other hand, nano-fillers composite polymers have been shown to increase the dielectric constant of composites, while the overall breakdown strength sharply deteriorates because of the abundant conductive paths inside the composites [13−15]. Furthermore, commonly used polymers have relatively lower heat resistance and *W*rec values due to their conjugated structures. Taking a commercial capacitor, biaxially oriented polypropylene (BOPP), as an example, *W*rec is only 2 J/cm3, and the temperature it can withstand is also below 85 °C [16−18]. Thus, maintaining high energy storage performance at high temperatures is still crucial.

To overcome this problem, we choose the PU–Cu composite, a hydrogen-bonding and dual crosslinked system, which has relatively stable dielectric properties (Fig. 1). The unique design enhances the mechanical properties, including the stress at break; the breakdown field strength of the polymers is related to their mechanical properties, and the unique structure of PU–Cu also provides good stability. The groups and ligands are helpful in constructing carrier traps. PTL2ZST–*x*PU–Cu is designed to introduce carrier traps into the composite, which significantly suppressed dielectric loss compared with that of pure PTL2ZST. Deep carrier traps can form within the interfaces, which hinders the interfacial charge transfer and thus weakens the charge carrier transport in composites at high temperatures [19−21]. Beyond that, the conduction current of the composite at high temperature is greatly suppressed with large bandgap PTL2ZST particles, and hence, high *W*rec and *η* values are available. Consequently, at the working temperature of 140 °C, ultrahigh *W*rec of 17.01 J/cm3 with *η* of 80.31% is obtained for the PTL2ZST–90% PU–Cu thick film at 2500 kV/cm. Compared with those of pure PTL2ZST, the *W*rec and *η* remain within the range of ±5% (*W*rec increases by 21.7%, and *η* increases by 9.4% at 100 °C). Owing to the carrier traps weakening the charge carrier transport in the composites, this study also enhanced the impedance to achieve ultrahigh wave filtering performance, and PTL2ZST–90% PU–Cu can work on a rectifier circuit to filter off 90% of the clutter. With further improvements in the specific capacity, this study is expected to replace the traditional filter capacitor.

### 2.1 Sample preparation

Commercially available powders SnO2 (99.95%, Aladdin), Pb3O4 (98%, Aladdin), Tm2O3 (99.99%, Aladdin), ZrO2 (99.0%, Macklin), TiO2 (99.85%, rutile phase, klamar), and La2O3 (99.99%, Macklin), indium tin oxide (ITO), N,N-dimethylformamide (99.9%, Macklin), and polyurethane (PU)–Cu were used as the raw materials.

PU-SO (SO short for the chelating ligand, 2 g) and CuCl2 (23 mg) were dissolved in 20 mL dimethyl formamide (DMF). Then the reactants were stirred for 8 h and concentrated. The concentrated solution was poured into a polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) plate. The plate was dried for 24 h. The PU–SO–Cu film was prepared. The details in the Electronic Supplementary Material (ESM).

(Pb0.64Tm0.04La0.2)(Zr0.55Sn0.44Ti0.01)–*x*PU–Cu (PTL2ZST–*x*PU–Cu,  x= 0, 50%, 70%, and 90%) were synthesized via the tape-casting method. The fillers were prepared as follows: The PTL2ZST ceramic samples were ground and passed through 300 mesh sieves. The filler/PU–Cu composite films were fabricated via the following steps. The PTL2ZST and PU–Cu samples were placed into N,N-dimethylformamide. The mixture was subsequently heated to 100 °C and stirred for 12 h to dry. A good liquid gel was formed, and then was placed on ITO via the tape-casting method. Finally, the thick films were dried at 70 °C for 3 h. The sample thickness was 0.34 mm, and the electrode radius was 2 mm.

### 2.2 Characterizations

The crystal structure of the films at room temperature was determined by an X-ray diffraction (XRD, Bruker-AXS D8, Germany). The morphology of the samples was investigated using a scanning electron microscope (SEM; FEI/Philips Sirion 2000, the Netherlands). The polarization–electric field (*P*–*E*) hysteresis loops were recorded by a ferroelectric test system (Precision Premier II, Radiant Technologies Inc., USA). A UV–visible (UV–Vis) spectrophotometer (UV-2600i, Shimadzu, Japan) was used to investigate the ultraviolet absorption spectrum. The filter characteristics of the films were determined via a rectifier circuit. The electric field distribution and electric tree evolution of the PTL2ZST–90% PU–Cu and PTL2ZST ceramics were simulated by the finite element simulation method with two-dimensional (2D) models using COMSOL software.

### 3.2 Energy storage performance analysis

The *P–E* loops of PTL2ZST–*x*PU–Cu as a function of composition under the maximum applied field are shown in Fig. 4(a). The compositions exhibit hysteresis loops, which belong a typical FE behavior. For the PU–Cu composite samples (Fig. 4(a)), *P*max increases from 350 to 2500 kV/cm, and *W*rec increases from 9.3 to 17.01 J/cm3 and then decreases to 8.9 J/cm3. PTL2ZST–90% PU–Cu has the best *W*rec. The variation in *W*rec as a function of PU–Cu content is consistent with the UV data and SEM images and is contiguous with increased resistivity, each of which has been reported to increase *E*b. Figure 4(b) shows representative energy storage properties of PTL2ZST–90% PU–Cu under different electric fields. *W*rec reaches a maximum of 17.01 J/cm3 at 2500 kV/cm, with *η* of 80.31%. These values of *W*rec and *η* are greater or equivalent to those of current composites and commonly used polymers (Fig. 4(d)). Owing to the greater number of mobile carriers at high temperatures, the high-temperature dielectric performance is limited by the loss caused by a sharp increase in the conductance. However, due to carrier trap mechanism, the carrier displacement of the material is inhibited, which improves the breakdown field strength of the material and thus improves the energy storage performance.

The electric field, electric potential, and polarization distribution with electrical tree propagation were determined via finite element simulation. Figures 5(a) and 5(b) illustrate the breakdown path evolution and electrical field distribution in the two models, which are compared at the same time slot. In the model of pristine PTL2ZST, the breakdown paths rapidly propagate with partial discharge, resulting in the splitting of each tip into more branches (Fig. S3 in the Electronic Supplementary Material (ESM)) [31,32]. In contrast, in the PTL2ZST–*x*PU–Cu model, the PTL2ZST fillers change the evolution behavior of breakdown paths. The breakdown paths tend to evolve towards PTL2ZST fillers, promoting them to split into multiple branches and suppressing the development of the main branch (Fig. S3 in the ESM). More branches appear when the local electric field increases. Simultaneously, as the number of branch channels increases, the electric energy is more evenly distributed to each tip, mitigating the electric field distortion. Moreover, deep carrier traps can form within the polymer/particle interfaces, which hinders the interfacial charge transfer and thus weakens the charge carrier transport in composites [33]. At the same time, the PTL2ZST is broken down, and in the PTL2ZST–*x*PU–Cu model, the electrical branch is still growing, indicating that the breakdown has been significantly improved.

### 3.3 High-temperature energy storage performance

Figure 6(a) shows the *P–E* curves of PTL2ZST at different temperatures, and *W*rec decreases from 8.15 to 6.11 J/cm3 (25–100 °C), and the *η* increases from 84.7% to 88.9%. The applications of PTL2ZST is limited by the degradation of its dielectric properties at high temperatures. At the same time, even heat up to 140 °C, the PTL2ZST–90% PU–Cu thick films at 2500 kV/cm have almost the same *W*rec (from 17.01 to 16.27 J/cm3) and elevated *η* (from 81.2% to 85.7%). This reveals that the carrier trap strategy can maintain the energy storage performance of PTL2ZST–90% PU–Cu at high temperatures. This finding highlights the effectiveness of the carrier trap strategy in enhancing the high-temperature energy storage density. Figure 6(d) shows a comparison of the energy density between this work and previously reported works, which reveals the superiority of this work. Notably, even compared with commercial BOPP films, the high-temperature energy storage characteristics of PU–Cu-based composites increase the operating temperature of the dielectric film by two-folds [27].

### 3.4 Wave filtering performance

Using the energy storage of the capacitor and the characteristics of alternating current (AC) resistance, the voltage waveform is smoothed through the charging and discharge process. When the AC signal passes through the capacitor, the high-frequency signal can easily pass, whereas the low-frequency signal is blocked because the voltage of the high-frequency signal changes quickly and the capacitor has a small hindering effect on it; while the voltage change of the low-frequency signal is slow, and the capacitor has a large hindering effect on it. In the rectifier circuit, when the pulsating DC voltage passes through the capacitor, the high-frequency voltage component is filtered out, and the output voltage becomes smooth (Fig. 7(a)) [39,40]. The electroelectrolytic capacitor has a large capacity but a large volume and short life; the tantalum capacitor has a small capacity but a small volume and long life [42]. In terms of materials, the experimental process of polymer materials is generally more complex. If surface modification and nanoparticles are added into materials, then the process difficulty of composite materials is greater than those of pure polymer and ceramic materials. Compared with the common filter capacitors on the market, the sample has a smaller capacity and faster speed, and the cost is slightly greater. Moreover, because of the antiferroelectric ceramics in the sample, a phase change occurs at high voltage, which is obviously different from the commonly used filter capacitors. This phase change process increases the filtering capability of the sample.

The two samples clearly filter out a large amount of clutter, and PTL2ZST–90% PU–Cu can filter out 90% of the clutter, which is better than that of PTL2ZST–50% PU–Cu because carrier traps weaken the charge carrier transport. PTL2ZST–90% PU–Cu has a stronger ability to block the electric current, showing the potential to replace the commonly used filter capacitors on the market.

## 4 Conclusions

By combining the advantages of a hydrogen-bonding dual crosslinked system and carrier traps, AFE PTL2ZST with PU polymer–Cu was designed and prepared via the tape-casting method. The good compatibility between PTL2ZST and PU–Cu enables the fabrication of high-quality polymer films. The high polarization of PTL2ZST filler and the interface polarization between the PU–Cu matrix and PTL2ZST filler significantly improve the polarization of the films. Carrier traps constructed in the interface region can reduce the leakage current, suppress conduction loss and increase *E*b. Owing to the increase in both *ɛ*r and *E*b, *W*rec of the films over a wide temperature range is significantly improved compared with that of pure PTL2ZST. At room temperature, *W*rec of PTL2ZST–90% PU–Cu reaches 17.1 J/cm3, with *η* of 80.31%, even at elevated temperatures and an outstanding energy storage of approximately 16.7 J/cm3 at 140 °C. The impedance is also enhanced and to ultrahigh wave filtering performance is achieved; PTL2ZST–90% PU–Cu can work on a rectifier circuit to filter off 90% of the clutter.
