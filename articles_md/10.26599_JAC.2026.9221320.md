## Abstract

<p>Microwave dielectric ceramics (MWDCs) are pivotal to modern wireless communication systems, with their performance governed by three key parameters: relative dielectric constant (<i>ε</i><sub>r</sub>), <i>Q</i>×<i>f</i> value (product of quality factor <i>Q</i> (reciprocal dielectric loss) and frequency <i>f</i>), and temperature coefficient of resonant frequency (<i>τ</i><sub>f</sub>). This review systematically summarizes the recent research progress on MWDCs from five interrelated aspects. In terms of performance characterization, standardized resonant methods achieve <i>ε</i><sub>r</sub> measurement errors below 1% and a dielectric loss (tan<i>δ)</i> detection limit as low as 10<sup>−5</sup>. Theoretically, frameworks from complex crystal chemistry to the recently elucidated cation rattling effect enable quantitative interpretation of dielectric behavior. In processing, the cold sintering process achieves ceramic densification below 300 °C, reducing energy consumption by over 97% in comparison with conventional sintering. For applications, these materials have been widely deployed in high-performance substrates, resonators, and filters for 5G/6G communications, with device insertion loss maintained below 1 dB. Additionally, data-driven approaches, particularly machine learning, can accurately predict key dielectric properties with a coefficient of determination (<i>R</i><sup>2</sup>) higher than 0.9, accelerating the exploration and development of novel MWDCs. By integrating these perspectives, this review offers systematic insight into the state-of-the-art progress and future development directions of MWDC research.</p>
