## 1 Introduction

Dielectric ceramics, as the core components in high-power systems, benefit from their ultrafast discharge rates and superior power density and have attracted considerable interest in energy storage capacitor applications [1−3]. Generally, the energy-storage performance of a dielectric capacitor is determined by its polarization–electric field (*P*–*E*) loop; the recoverable energy density (*U*e) and the efficiency (* η) can be calculated as:  Ue=∫PrPmEdP,  η=Ue/(Ue+Uloss), where P*m, *P*r, and *U*loss are the maximum polarization, the remnant polarization, and the energy loss, respectively [4,5]. However, their low recoverable energy storage density (*U*e) and low efficiency (* η) impede their practical applications [6,7]. Moreover, the high-temperature reliability and stability of capacitors must be ensured to guarantee the performance of the circuit operating at high temperature in scenarios such as natural gas exploration, aerospace and military equipment, and under-hood automotive electronics [8−10]. Therefore, simultaneously achieving excellent energy storage performance and temperature insensitivity at elevated temperatures (up to 200 °C) is still a major challenge for dielectrics.

Although lead-containing perovskite dielectrics can operate at relatively high temperatures [11,12], concerns about the toxicity of lead oxide have prompted present research into lead-free alternatives. BaTiO3 (BTO) ferroelectrics have received extensive attention because of their high permittivity, long-range ordered spontaneous polarization, and moderate Curie temperature (120 °C) [13,14]. However, undoped BTO ceramics exhibit strong ferroelectricity, as indicated by a square P‒E* loop and high remnant polarization. As a result, pure BTO ceramics have the energy storage density of only 0.4 J·cm−3, which is not conducive to achieving high-performance ceramic capacitors [15]. Therefore, some strategies have been carried out to improve the performance of ferroelectric ceramics, such as refining the grain size [16] or controlling the grain orientation [17] to increase the breakdown strength, as well as constructing nanodomains or polymorphic relaxation phases to disrupt the long-range ferroelectric order [5,18]. With a rational combination of the above strategies, high *U*e of 10.0 J·cm−3 and high *η* of 93.5% have been achieved. Despite these efforts, research on the temperature insensitivity and reliability of BTO-based ceramic capacitors is still lacking. It remains a formidable challenge to meet the stringent requirements of modern devices for high-temperature reliable energy storage capacitors.

Most recently, solid solutions of (1−*x*)BaTiO3–*x*BiMeO3, where Me represents one or more metal ions, have shown promising dielectric properties and broad temperature range permittivity stability, revealing high-temperature application potential [19,20]. For example, research in this area has substantially increased with the emergence of the solid-solution system (1−*x*)BaTiO3–*x*Bi(Mg0.5Ti0.5)O3. A relatively flat region of the temperature dependence of the dielectric properties could be observed for higher *x* values (* ≥ 0.4) between 100 and 500 °C, which was attributed to the highly diffuse and dispersive relaxation behavior [21]. However, a large amount of BiMeO3 can be detrimental because of its tendency to form secondary phases segregated within the grains [22]. High-entropy effects might appear to be a promising effective strategy to stabilize this solid-solution system [23−25]. Moreover, the high-entropy strategy helps design “local polymorphic distortion” [26,27], which has revolutionized the enhancement of comprehensive energy storage. Therefore, designing BTO-based high-entropy ceramics containing high amounts of BiMeO3 is expected to yield superior energy storage performance and high-temperature reliability.

In this work, a series of entropy-modulated BTO-based ceramics were designed and prepared via a conventional solid-state reaction method. The phase structure, surface gain morphology, dielectric properties, energy storage and reliability at high temperatures have been studied in detail. Incorporating the BiMeO3, accompanied by Ca2+ and Sr2+ ions, is incorporated to replace Ba2+ ions at A sites to regulate the configuration entropy, the high-entropy (Ba0.34Bi0.5Ca0.1Sr0.06)(Mg0.25Ti0.75)O3 (BCST–BMT) ceramic achieves stable U*e of 5.76 J·cm−3 and efficiency of 89%. Importantly, the excellent temperature stability renders a remarkable energy storage density of 4.90 ±0.14 J·cm−3 with  η above 89% across a wide temperature range of 25–250 °C, as well as superior cycling reliability with negligible property degradation even after 3 ×105 cycles at 300 kV∙cm−1 and 200 °C. The excellent high-temperature energy storage performance of the high-entropy BCST–BMT ceramic highlights its great potential for applications across a broad temperature range.

### 2.1 Raw materials

BT-based ceramics were prepared via the traditional solid-state reaction method. The raw powders, including BaCO3 (99.8%), Bi2O3 (99.99%), TiO2 (99.8%), Sc2O3 (99.99%), CaCO3 (99.99%), MgO (99.99%), SrCO3 (99.95%), and MnO2 (99.99%), were all purchased from Aladdin (China).

### 2.2 Ceramic preparation

A series of entropy-designed dielectrics with the compositions BaTiO3, (Ba0.5Bi0.5)(Mg0.25Ti0.75)O3 (BT–BMT), (Ba0.4Bi0.5Ca0.1)(Mg0.25Ti0.75)O3 (BCT–BMT), and (Ba0.34Bi0.5Ca0.1Sr0.06)(Mg0.25Ti0.75)O3 (BCST–BMT) were fabricated via a conventional solid-state reaction. The detailed configuration entropy (*S*config) can be found in Table S1 in the Electronic Supplementary Material (ESM). All the raw materials were weighed according to the stoichiometric composition, and an excess of 5% Bi2O3 was added to reduce the impact of Bi evaporation. The weighed raw material was ball-milled for 12 h with ethanol, dried at 100 °C for 3 h, and then calcined at 850 °C for 3 h. The resulting powders were further ball-milled for 18 h with ethanol using an additional 0.5 wt% MnO2 (Aladdin, 99.95%) as a sintering aid. In addition, high-entropy compositions (Ba0.4Bi0.5Ca0.1)(Mg0.25Ti0.75)O3 and (Ba0.34Bi0.5Ca0.1Sr0.06)(Mg0.25Ti0.75)O3 were added to 1.5 wt% Sc2O3 as a sintering aid. After drying at 120 °C for 3 h and adding polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) solution as a binder, the powders were pressed into pellets with a 10 mm in diameter. Later, the pellets were heated to 600 °C for 3 h to eliminate the binder and then sintered at 1080–1300 °C for 2 h to obtain dense ceramics. The sintered ceramics were thinned and polished to achieve parallel surfaces. Fire-on silver pastes or sputtered gold films were used as electrodes for electrical measurements.

### 2.3 Structure characterizations

The crystal structure was characterized via an X-ray diffractometer (XRD; Malvern PANalytical Empyrean) with Cu Kα X-ray source (wavelength  λ = 0.1542 nm). A scanning electron microscope (SEM; Zeiss Merlin Compact) was used to characterize the ceramic morphology and crystal size. Before the measurements, the ceramics were polished and thermally etched at 50 °C below the corresponding sintering temperature for 30 min. The average grain size and grain distribution were analyzed by the software Nano Measurer.

### 2.4 Electrical measurements

Dielectric properties were measured as a function of temperature (25–450 °C) and frequency (100 Hz–1 MHz) by an impedance analyzer (E4990A, Keysight) combined with a temperature control stage (HFSE91–PB2, Linkam). The impedance spectroscopy measurements were conducted with an impedance gain-phase analyzer (1260, Solartron) equipped with a dielectric interface (1296, Solartron) at a temperature of 300–400 °C and a frequency range of 0.01 Hz to 1 MHz. The ferroelectric measurements (*P‒E* loop), leakage current density, breakdown strength (BDS), cycling reliability and temperature stability were driven at 10 Hz by a ferroelectric test system (Radiant Technologies Inc.) equipped with a heating holder. The Weibull distribution (*P*(*E*i)=1−exp(−*E*i/*E*b)*β*) was used to characterize BDS of the ceramics. *P*(*E*i) is the cumulative breakdown probability of a certain electric field, and *E*i is BDS of each sample. *E*b and  β are the Weibull characteristic BDS and the Weibull modulus, respectively.

### 3.1 Design and synthesis of high-entropy BaTiO3-based ceramics

Here, we design entropy-modulated BaTiO3-based (BTO-based) ceramics by introducing high-mole-content Bi(Mg0.5Ti0.5)O3 (BMT) as an end-member, accompanied by Ca2+ and Sr2+ ions to replace the Ba2+ ions at A sites. The XRD patterns of different BTO-based ceramics are displayed in Fig. 1. The pure BTO ceramic shows a typical Tet phase with two distinct splitting XRD peaks corresponding to (002)T/(200)T at approximately 45°. With the BMT incorporation, the splitting of the XRD peaks disappears, indicating a pseudocubic phase with single (200) peaks. However, undesirable peaks appear, which can be attributed to the Bi4Ti3O12 and Bi12TiO20 phases [28]. When the configuration entropy further increases to 1.67*R* (the ideal gas constant), the crystallization peak associated with the impurity phase becomes undetectable. Consequently, the samples once again exhibit a single perovskite structure (ABO3), indicating that the improved solubility limit is enabled by the high-entropy design [29]. Moreover, the peaks shift to higher angles (Fig. 1(b)) because of the lattice shrinkage and distortion caused by the difference in radii between the doped cations and matrix cations. Specifically, Bi3+ (1.03 Å Bi3+ 2+ (1.44 Å), and Ca2+ (1.34 Å) ions are slightly smaller than Ba2+ (1.61 Å) ions owing to 12-fold coordination in the A-site, whereas the B-site ion Mg2+ (0.72 Å) has a smaller radius compared to Ti4+ (0.604 Å) (six-fold coordination).

Figures 2(a)–2(d) display the scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images, which present the surface morphology of the four samples, along with an illustration demonstrating the statistical distribution of the grain sizes (G*a) in the corresponding samples. The average grain size of various BTO-based ceramics decreases from 24.2 to 0.63 μm as the configurational entropy values increase. This reduction is attributed to the lattice distortion that results from increased configurational entropy, which significantly impedes atomic diffusion. As a consequence, the average grain size is reduced, resulting in an increased grain boundary density [18]. Grain boundaries with higher resistivity play an important role in preventing electric breakdown by absorbing energy from electric tree expansion within the grains [30], contributing to an obvious negative correlation between the average grain size and the breakdown field (*E*b), that is, * Eb∝(Ga)−α( 0.2⩽α⩽0.4) [32]. This obviously refined grain size contributes to an increase in E*b by altering the electric field distribution within the ceramic under an applied electric field.

The temperature-dependent dielectric properties under various frequencies are given in Fig. 3. The BaTiO3 ceramics exhibited relatively sharp and temperature-sensitive dielectric peaks at approximately 126 °C, corresponding to the Curie point where the phase transition occurred [31]. When BMT is introduced, there is a single diffuse and dispersive peak over a wide temperature range. The observed diffuse phase transition and substantial frequency dispersion indicate typical relaxation behavior, attributed to the increased disorder at both the A- and B-sites. As the entropy value further increases, the significant differences in the valences and radii of the ions between B-site ions (Ti4+, Mg2+) and small A-site cations (Bi3+, Sr2+, Ca2+) disrupt the long-range ferroelectric order [21], fostering the generation of polar nanoscale regions. In addition, these high-entropy compositions also have quite a low dielectric loss in the temperature range of 150–450 °C (Figs. 3(b)–3(d)). This BTO-based high-entropy ceramic with obvious lattice distortion, a refined grain size, enhanced relaxation behavior and low dielectric loss has great potential in dielectric capacitor applications.

### 3.2 Room-temperature energy storage performance

To investigate the effect of configurational entropy on the electric field response of BTO-based ceramics, bipolar *P‒E* loops with different entropy values were measured under the same electric field (100 kV·cm−1), as shown in Fig. S1 in the ESM. With increasing configuration entropy, both *P*max and *P*r gradually decrease, indicating weakened ferroelectricity and enhanced relaxation characteristics, as demonstrated by the observed diffuse phase transition and substantial frequency dispersion shown in Figs. 3(a)–3(d). The breakdown strength is a critical factor that influences the energy storage performance of dielectric ceramics. To more accurately evaluate the breakdown strength of BTO-based ceramics, breakdown experiments are carried out several times, and the standard Weibull distribution analysis is calculated according to the results (Fig. 4(a)). The *E*b values of the BTO-based ceramics correlate positively with increasing entropy values, increasing from 140 to 375 kV·cm−1. Moreover, the Weibull modulus (* β) also increases from 5.8 to 15.1, indicating the improved reliability and uniformity of the ceramics. As depicted in Fig. 4(b), the P‒E* hysteresis loops for different compositions are presented at their breakdown electric field. *P*r is negatively correlated with configurational entropy, decreasing from 16.9 to 1.4 μC·cm−2. Both an enhanced breakdown electric field and diminished *P*r imply an increase in the energy storage density. The relationship between the corresponding energy storage performance and configuration entropy is established, as shown in Fig. 4(c). As the entropy of BTO-based ceramics increases, a pronounced improvement in energy storage performance is achieved, with *U*e increasing from 0.43 J·cm−3 (0*R*) to 5.76 J·cm−3 (1.67*R*), indicating a substantial enhancement exceeding 100%. Moreover, the energy storage efficiency (* η) also improved significantly, increasing from 31% to 89%. In addition, reliability and stability are also key parameters for dielectric applications. As shown in Fig. 4(d), cyclic P*–*E* measurements of the BCST-BMT composition were performed at 300 kV/cm and repeated for up to 3* ×105 cycles. The calculated U*e and * η as functions of the number of cycles, accompanied by negligible changes in U*e (4.20–4.69 J·cm−3) and * η (86.99%–89.53%), indicate excellent fatigue resistance and superior cycling reliability.

In addition to the lattice distortion, grain size refinement, and enhanced relaxation behavior, which are closely related to the improved *E*b and high *U*e, leakage current density and impedance spectrum measurements were further carried out to analyze the conduction and resistance changes to clarify the origin of the substantial improvement in *E*b. As shown in Fig. 5(a), the leakage current decreased from 0.95×10−5 to 1.3×10−7 A·cm−2 at an electric field of 100 kV·cm−1, which can effectively reduce the conduction loss at high electric fields and is conducive to the high efficiency and *E*b of dielectrics. Figure 5(b) also shows the impedance spectrum of the BTO-based ceramic at 400 °C. It is apparent that the radius of the impedance spectrum arc gradually increases as the configurational entropy increases, highlighting a gradual rise in resistance. This observation implies that higher configurational entropy enhances the insulation performance of ceramics [32]. Therefore, the decreased conduction loss and increased resistance also contribute to the increased *E*b.

### 3.3 High-temperature energy storage performance

Given the advantages of excellent resistance and low dielectric loss at high temperatures for high-entropy BTO dielectric ceramics, high-temperature energy storage performance and temperature stability are of paramount importance. To assess the energy storage performance of high-entropy BTO ceramics at high temperatures, the *P‒E* loops of BCST–BMT ceramics were tested at 200 °C under various electric fields. As shown in Fig. S2 in the ESM, these *P*–*E* loops remain slim even under a high electric field of 340 kV/cm, leading to both high *U*e of 4.82 J·cm−3 and high *η* of 93.07% (Fig. 6(a)). Furthermore, temperature stability was also demonstrated across the temperature range of 25–250 °C, as shown in Fig. 6(b). The stability test is conducted at 340 kV·cm−1 (the inset shows the *P‒E* loops at different temperatures). *U*e and * η consistently maintain high values, with a remarkable energy storage density of 4.90 ±0.14 J·cm−3 and  η above 89% across a wide temperature range of 25–250 °C. The change rates are approximately 5% and 6%, respectively. In particular,  η remains above 90% at high temperatures, which is higher than that at room temperature.

For practical high-temperature applications, the cycling reliability at high temperatures is also crucial. The energy storage properties and *P*–*E* loops measured at 200 °C and a repeated electric field of 300 kV∙cm−1 are shown in Fig. 6(c) (the inset shows the *P*–*E* loops at different cycles). The *P*–*E* loops exhibit negligible changes after 3* ×105 cycles, leading to stable U*e (4.11–4.43 J·cm−3) and  η (93.19%–95.44%) values with very low variations, revealing superior reliability at elevated temperatures up to 200 °C. Given their excellent high-temperature energy storage performance, broad temperature range, stable energy storage performance and cycling reliability, high-entropy BTO ceramics possess great potential for high-temperature capacitor applications. Figure 6(d) shows a comparison of this work with other recently published BTO-based dielectric ceramics for variable-temperature energy storage performance, indicating the excellent broad-temperature energy storage performance of the high-entropy BCST–BMT ceramic.

## 4 Conclusions

In summary, we designed BTO-based ceramics with various entropy values by introducing BMT as an end member, accompanied by Ca2+ and Sr2+ ions, to further regulate the configuration entropy, successfully achieving *U*e of 5.76 J·cm−3 and efficiency of 89% in the high-entropy BCST–BMT ceramic. Notably, the excellent temperature stability enables the BCST–BMT ceramic to maintain a remarkable energy storage density of greater than 4.76 J·cm−3 at 250 °C while achieving an efficiency of up to 89%. It also exhibits superior cycling reliability, with negligible degradation of properties even after 3 ×105 cycles at 300 kV∙cm−1 and 200 °C. A comprehensive structural analysis revealed that the enhanced relaxation behavior, excellent resistance, and low dielectric loss at high temperatures due to the high entropy effect are responsible for the prominent high-temperature energy storage performance. This work highlights the great potential of BTO-based ceramics for energy storage applications over a broad temperature range.
