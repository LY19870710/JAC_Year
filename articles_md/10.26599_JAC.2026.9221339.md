## Abstract

<p>Practical deployment of Li<sub>4</sub>SiO<sub>4</sub> as a high-temperature CO<sub>2</sub> sorbent requires pelletization, which inevitably densifies the microstructure and imposes severe CO<sub>2</sub> diffusion limitations. Conventional sacrificial pore-forming agents address this issue but remain single-purpose, serving solely as structural templates without conferring chemical benefits. Here, we demonstrate that spent coffee grounds (SCGs), an abundant food-industry waste, can serve as a single-source modifier that achieves three colocalized enhancements in Li<sub>4</sub>SiO<sub>4</sub> pellets: hierarchical pore engineering, <i>in situ</i> K-doping, and oxygen vacancy generation. The thermal decomposition of SCG creates an interconnected hierarchical macroporous network that effectively reduces intraparticle CO<sub>2</sub> diffusion resistance. Meanwhile, the mineral-rich SCG ash provides <i>in situ</i> potassium doping, generating a localized eutectic molten carbonate phase that accelerates liquid-phase ion transport. Crucially, the transient reducing atmosphere during biomass combustion introduces oxygen vacancies into the silicate lattice; density functional theory (DFT) calculations reveal that these vacancies serve as highly active CO<sub>2</sub> adsorption sites with a strongly exothermic adsorption energy of −0.914 eV. Benefiting from this triple-synergistic enhancement, the SCG-modified sorbent (LSO-50) achieves a CO<sub>2</sub> adsorption capacity of 0.275 g/g at 650 °C under 15 vol% CO<sub>2</sub>, representing a more than fourfold improvement over unmodified pellets. When further combined with Na<sub>2</sub>CO<sub>3</sub> codoping to promote additional eutectic formation, the optimized sorbent (LSON-50) reaches 0.330 g/g, retains 0.284 g/g after 50 adsorption–desorption cycles, and exhibits robust mechanical stability (&lt; 10% attrition loss). By colocating structural, chemical, and defect features within a single biomass-derived modifier, this work establishes a scalable waste-valorization route for high-performance, eco-friendly CO<sub>2</sub> capture.</p>
