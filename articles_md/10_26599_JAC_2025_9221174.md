## 1 Introduction

Ferroelectric (FE) materials, which exhibit switchable spontaneous polarization, have long provided the foundation for a broad range of electronic and energy storage applications [1−6]. In addition to conventional ferroelectrics, antiferroelectrics (AFEs) and relaxor ferroelectrics (RFEs) offer distinct polarization behaviors that arise from their antipolar or nanoscale-disordered dipole configurations [7−9]. More recently, materials exhibiting relaxor antiferroelectric (R-AFE) behavior, where long-range antipolar order is disrupted by compositional or structural disorder, have gained attention as promising candidates for advanced dielectric energy storage applications [10−12]. Within this category, R-AFEs are especially appealing because of their slim polarization-electric field (*P*–*E*) loops. These loops enable high recoverable energy density (*W*rec) and excellent energy storage efficiency (*η*) with minimal hysteresis loss. These characteristics are crucial for pulsed power systems, power electronics, and next-generation capacitive devices, which demand rapid and high-density energy storage in conjunction with reliable discharge under demanding operating conditions [11,13].

In the search for sustainable and high-performance dielectric materials, significant research efforts have focused on developing lead-free AFE ceramics, particularly those based on NaNbO3 (NN) and AgNbO3 (AN). Although these materials exhibit promising polarization behavior, their practical applications are often constrained by moderate dielectric breakdown strength (*E*b), substantial hysteresis losses, and low *η* [14−16]. To overcome these limitations, researchers have investigated various modification strategies, including A-site and B-site doping, defect control, domain structure optimization, and phase boundary engineering. These techniques aim to reduce the remanent polarization (*P*r) and enhance the dielectric breakdown characteristics [17−19]. Despite these efforts, most approaches have achieved only marginal gains, and the concurrent realization of high *W*rec, high *η*, and fast discharge performance remains an unresolved challenge [20−22]. A particularly critical limitation of many prior studies is that their reported high *W*rec values are often obtained only under very high electric fields. While this may be suitable for specialized high-power pulsed applications, it poses considerable challenges for broader capacitor integration owing to reliability concerns and increased dielectric losses. In contrast, the *W*rec-to-*E*b ratio (*W*rec*/E*b) provides a more practically meaningful figure of merit, as it represents how efficiently a material utilizes its dielectric strength. Despite their importance, only a few lead-free systems to date have demonstrated both a high *W*rec*/E*b and a high *η*, especially under moderate electric fields and application-relevant conditions [23−25]. Furthermore, capacitor-level demonstrations, such as rapid charge‒discharge performance and stable operation across a wide range of temperatures and frequencies, are seldom verified. These limitations highlight the need for compositionally and structurally engineered AFE ceramics that not only exhibit enhanced intrinsic properties but also deliver robust, application-ready performance suitable for practical energy storage and pulsed power systems.

To address these limitations, recent studies have increasingly focused on dual-site doping strategies in AgNbO3-based systems to modulate both global symmetry and local structural order. In particular, B-site substitution with Ta5+ has been shown to effectively suppress octahedral tilting and stabilize the antiferroelectric phase owing to its lower polarizability and electronegativity than those of Nb5+ [15,26−28]. Simultaneously, A-site doping with small-radius cations or dopants such as rare-earth ions (e.g., La3+, Sm3+) and Bi3+ has been employed to induce structural heterogeneity, disrupt long-range order, and promote relaxor-like behavior [29−33]. While these efforts have led to improved dielectric and energy storage properties, most prior studies rely on mono-substitution strategies and focus primarily on *W*rec enhancement under high electric fields or structural stabilization through local displacement suppression.

In contrast, our study introduces a binary aliovalent codoping strategy at the A-site, combining Li+ and Nd3+ along with fixed 20 mol% Ta5+ doping at the B-site. This approach involves a greater degree of chemical disorder and ionic size mismatch, enabling a systematic transition from the conventional AFE to the relaxor-antiferroelectric-like (R-AFE-like) regime. We define the R-AFE-like regime as beginning from *x* = 0.03 in the (Ag1−4*x*Li*x*Nd*x*)(Nb0.8Ta0.2)O3 system (*x* = 0, 0.01, 0.03, 0.05, and 0.07), where the disruption of long-range antipolar order and the emergence of local polarization instability become evident, as supported by various experimental results. Unlike previous reports that emphasized individual dopant effects or phenomenological phase control, this study provides a mechanism-driven analysis of relaxor evolution, crystallographic symmetry refinement, and nanodomain formation [34]. Among the different compositions, the optimized (Ag1−4*x*Li*x*Nd*x*)(Nb0.8Ta0.2)O3 ceramic with *x* = 0.03 has a maximum *W*rec of 7.2 J/cm3 and an *η* of 92.3% at a moderate breakdown field of 327 kV/cm, along with excellent frequency and temperature stability. Furthermore, practical capacitor-level performance is demonstrated through rapid charge‒discharge cycling in both overdamped and underdamped circuits, achieving a peak power density of 172 MW/cm3 and a discharge time constant of approximately 40 ns. These results highlight the potential of compositionally engineered AgNbO3 ceramics with tunable R-AFE-like behavior as high-performance candidates for next-generation pulsed power capacitors and advanced energy-storage systems.

### 2.1 Sample preparation

AgNbO3 (AN), Ag(Nb0.8Ta0.2)O3 (ANT), and (Ag1−4*x*Li*x*Nd*x*)(Nb0.8Ta0.2)O3 (denoted as ANT-*x*LN with *x* = 0.01, 0.03, 0.05, and 0.07 for ANT-0.01LN, ANT-0.03LN, ANT-0.05LN, and ANT-0.07LN, respectively) powders were synthesized via a conventional solid-state reaction method. Stoichiometric amounts of Ag2O (99%, Thermo Scientific), Li2CO3 (99.0%, Alfa Aesar), Nd2O3 (99.9%, Sigma‒Aldrich), Nb2O5 (99.9%, Sigma‒Aldrich), and Ta2O5 (99%, Sigma‒Aldrich) were ball milled in anhydrous ethyl alcohol (99.9%, Samchun) via ZrO2 milling media for 24 h. The resulting slurries were dried and calcined at 900°C for three hours. The calcined powders were subsequently remilled for 24 h with the addition of 0.2 wt% MnO2 (99.0%, Sigma‒Aldrich) as a sintering aid. After drying, the powders were uniaxially pressed into pellets (with a diameter of 6 mm and a thickness of 1 mm). The pellets were sintered via a two-step process: (i) primary sintering at 1100 °C for two hours and (ii) secondary sintering at 1190 °C for three hours under an O2 atmosphere. For electrical measurements, the sintered pellets were polished to a thickness of ~0.1 mm. Silver paste was applied to the bottom surface, and a platinum electrode (~1 mm in diameter) was sputter-deposited on the top surface.

### 2.2 Characterizations

The microstructures of the AN, ANT, ANT-0.01LN, ANT-0.03LN, ANT-0.05LN, and ANT-0.07LN samples were examined via field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM; JSM-IT700HR, JEOL) and high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HR-TEM; JEM-2100HR, JEOL). The elemental distribution and chemical composition were determined via energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS, INCA X-sight 7421, Oxford Instruments). The crystal structures were investigated via X-ray diffraction (XRD; MiniFlex600, Rigaku) with Cu Kα radiation (*λ* = 0.1542 nm). Raman spectra were obtained via a Raman spectrometer (inVia Reflex, Renishaw) with a 532 nm excitation laser. The temperature- and frequency-dependent dielectric properties were measured via a microprobe-equipped chamber, a temperature controller (EC10, SUN Electronic System; MPS-CHTC, Nextron), and a precision LCR meter (4284A, HP). Bipolar and unipolar polarization-electric field (*P–E*) hysteresis loops were recorded via a ferroelectric evaluation system (TF analyzer 2000E, aixACCT GmbH). The time-dependent charge‒discharge behavior was investigated via a high-speed switching circuit in conjunction with an oscilloscope (MDO34, Tektronix). For the overdamped test, a simple RC circuit incorporating a 10 Ω resistor was used. In the underdamped configuration, an RLC circuit comprising a 10 Ω resistor and a 33 μH inductor was employed.

### 3.1 Overview of this work

The practical performance of AN is limited by several intrinsic drawbacks, including high *P*r, low *E*b, and substantial hysteresis loss. These factors collectively result in a low *W*rec and poor *η* [35]. The parameters are calculated as Eqs. (1) and (2):

(1) Wrec=∫PmaxPrEdP

(2) η=WrecWrec+Wloss

where *P*max is the maximum polarization, *E* denotes the applied electric field, and *W*loss represents the energy loss, estimated from the area enclosed by the *P‒E* loop. The performance bottlenecks of AN originate from strong Nb5+ off-centering, structural asymmetry, and defect formation arising from Ag+ volatility and weak AFE-phase stabilization [21,36].

To overcome these challenges, we employed a stepwise compositional design strategy (see Fig. 1). First, the Ta5+ content was fixed at 20 mol% in Ag(Nb1−*x*Ta*x*)O3 (ANT), a composition previously reported [37,38] and verified through our preliminary screening, to effectively suppress polar distortion and octahedral tilting while preserving adequate polarizability. B-site substitution reduces structural asymmetry, improves the AFE phase stability, and moderately enhances the dielectric performance. Building upon this foundation, codoping with Li+ and Nd3+ at the A-site was introduced to further tune the structure. More specifically, dual A-site substitution decreases the tolerance factor, refines the grain size, and introduces local chemical and structural disorder. Moreover, these combined effects suppress long-range ferroelectric order and influence relaxor-like behavior. The resulting (Ag1−4*x*Li*x*Nd*x*)(Nb0.8Ta0.2)O3 ceramics with *x* ≥ 0.03 exhibit significantly reduced *P*r, elevated *E*b, and slim *P‒E* loops, characteristic of R‒AFE-like behavior. This study describes a novel comprehensive structure-to-property design approach for the engineering of high-performance AN-based ceramics. The developed compositions show remarkable enhancements in both *W*rec and *η*, which makes them strong candidates for real-life energy-storage applications. In the final section of this work, their performance is further validated under pulsed operating conditions via charge‒discharge testing. They demonstrate fast response dynamics, well-defined underdamped and overdamped discharge profiles, and high power density. The following sections present detailed analyses of the crystal structure, microstructure, dielectric properties, energy storage performance, and capacitor-level functionality, all of which support the strategy framework illustrated in Fig. 1.

### 3.2 Crystal structure of AN-based ceramics

Figure 2(a) shows the X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the sintered AN, ANT, and (Ag1−4*x*Li*x*Nd*x*)(Nb0.8Ta0.2)O3 (ANT-*x*LN) ceramics. All the samples exhibit a single-phase perovskite structure with no detectable secondary phases, which confirms the successful incorporation of Li+, Nd3+, and Ta5+ into the AgNbO3 lattice to form stable solid solutions. A magnified view of the peak at 2*θ* values between 45° and 47° (Fig. 2(b)) reveals that the (220) and (008) reflections become increasingly close with increasing Li+ and Nd3+ contents. This progressive merging of peaks is indicative of reduced orthorhombic distortion, which is commonly associated with suppressed tilting of the NbO6 octahedra and changes in phase symmetry [39,40].

The pristine AN sample consists of ~48% polar and ~52% nonpolar phases. For ANT, the nonpolar content slightly increases to ~53%. With 1 mol% Li+/Nd3+ codoping (ANT-0.01LN), the nonpolar phase fraction increases to ~65%, and at *x* ≥ 0.03, the *Pbcm* phase becomes dominant (~100%), which is summarized in Table 1. Simultaneously, the unit cell volumes of both the *Pmc*21 and *Pbcm* phases decrease with increasing dopant concentration (Fig. S2(a) in the ESM), primarily due to the substitution of larger Ag+ ions (*r* = 1.54 Å, CN = 12) with smaller Nd3+ (*r* = 1.27 Å, CN = 12) and Li+ (*r* = 1.18 Å, CN = 12) [14,17]. Note that lattice compaction is closely tied to reduced octahedral tilting [40]. Further insights are obtained from the refined lattice parameters. For the *Pmc*21 phase, the *a* and *b* lattice constants decrease, whereas c slightly increases with increasing Ta5+ and Li+/Nd3+ substitution (Fig. S2(b) in the ESM). For the *Pbcm* phase, a decreasing *b/a* ratio points to the suppression of orthorhombic distortion [41]. These trends are consistent with a decreasing tolerance factor resulting from both A-site ionic-size mismatch and B-site substitution. In particular, Ta5+, with lower polarizability (2.82 Å3) and electronegativity (1.5) than Nb5+ (3.10 Å3, 1.6), helps reduce distortion in [Nb/TaO6] octahedral tilting [35,39,40].

Schematics of the polar *Pmc*21 and nonpolar *Pbcm* structures are shown in Fig. 2(c) and Fig. S2(c) in the ESM, with corresponding octahedral tilting angles (*Φ*) (rotation about the *a*-axis for *Pmc*21 and *c*-axis for *Pbcm*) and *Θ* (rotation about the *c*-axis for *Pmc*21 and *b*-axis for *Pbcm*) plotted in Fig. 2(d). The *Pmc*21 phase is characterized by cooperative Nb5+ displacements and asymmetric octahedral rotations that produce net polarization along the *c*-axis [27,42]. Conversely, the *Pbcm* phase exhibits antiparallel Nb5+ displacements and more symmetric octahedral tilting, which cancels macroscopic polarization [43]. This structural contrast captures the compositional transition from polar to nonpolar symmetry with increased Li+/Nd3+ doping. Quantitative analysis via XRD refinement and VESTA visualization reveals that both tilt angles decrease as the dopant content increases, indicating the gradual suppression of octahedral rotation, a structural feature closely tied to AFE behavior in AN-based systems. Moreover, the distribution of the Nb–O bond lengths decreases with increasing doping level, as illustrated in Fig. 2(e). This trend suggests a reduction in Nb5+/Ta5+ off-center displacement within the octahedra, which correlates with stabilization of the nonpolar *Pbcm* structure.

Raman spectroscopy was employed to further probe the local lattice dynamics and symmetry evolution (Fig. 3). The schematic in Fig. 3(a) illustrates the key vibrational modes of the Nb/TaO6 octahedra: nondegenerate symmetric stretching A1g (*ν*1), doubly degenerate symmetric stretching Eg (*ν*2), and triply degenerate symmetric bending F2g (*ν*5) modes [21,44]. These modes are susceptible to octahedral distortions and thus can serve as effective probes of local structural order and symmetry. As shown in Fig. 3(b), the pristine AN sample exhibited several broad Raman bands, notably, the bending mode near ~250 cm−1 (*ν*5) and the symmetric stretching modes at ~530 cm−1 (*ν*2) and ~580 cm−1 (*ν*1). In addition, a low-frequency band near ~103 cm−1, typically attributed to rotational vibrations of the Nb/TaO6 octahedra, is also evident in AN, but its intensity decreases with increasing Li+/Nd3+ content. This suppression indicates gradual compression and stiffening of the oxygen octahedra [38,40] which is consistent with the decreasing unit cell volume and tilt angles shown in Fig. 2(d) and Fig. S2(a) in the ESM. Furthermore, as the octahedra become more rigid and less prone to rotational distortions, the vibrational freedom of this rotational mode is restricted, which weakens Raman activity. With increasing dopant concentration, further changes in the high-frequency modes become evident. The *ν*2 peak near 530 cm−1 gradually disappears, whereas both the *ν*1 (near 580 cm−1) and *ν*5 (near 250 cm−1) modes broaden and become less intense. These changes reflect increased local structural disorder and suppressed Nb5+ off-center displacement [45].

In summary, the reduction in the tolerance factor resulting from Li+/Nd3+ codoping suppresses orthorhombic distortion and octahedral tilting, stabilizing the centrosymmetric nonpolar *Pbcm* phase over the polar *Pmc*21 phase. The stabilized *Pbcm* matrix resists field-induced long-range ferroelectric ordering, preserving an antiferroelectric-like character even under applied fields. Simultaneously, the chemical disorder introduced at the A-site locally perturbs the nonpolar framework, enabling the formation of polar nanodomains within the stable *Pbcm* lattice. This coexistence of a robust nonpolar matrix and field-responsive nanodomains provides the structural basis for the observed evolution from conventional AFE to relaxor-like behavior in the codoped compositions, as later corroborated by TEM and electrical measurements.

### 3.3 Microstructure of AN-based ceramics

The microstructural evolution of the AN, ANT, and ANT-*x*LN ceramics was examined via field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM; Fig. 4 and Fig. S3 in the ESM). The pristine AN sample exhibited relatively large and irregular grains with an average size of 3.18 μm (Fig. S3(a) in the ESM). This exaggerated grain growth is attributed primarily to the volatilization of Ag2O during high-temperature sintering, which generates silver (* V′Ag) and oxygen ( V¨O) vacancies. These point defects enhance lattice and grain boundary diffusion by increasing Ag+ and O2− mobility, which facilitates mass transport and leads to grain coarsening. Furthermore, to suppress excessive grain growth and minimize Ag+ loss, a reverse two-step sintering approach was employed, starting from a lower to a higher temperature stage. This method promotes uniform densification while preserving the fine-grain size, as previously demonstrated in Nd-doped AN systems [46,47]. The associated defect reaction can be formulated as Eq. However, at higher doping levels (*x* = 0.05 and 0.07), the grain size increases again to 2.07 and 2.52 μm, respectively (Figs. 4(c) and 4(d)). This grain coarsening is known to be due to the *in situ* formation of Li2O during the decomposition of Li2CO3. The presence of Li2O promotes the formation of transient eutectic phases with Nb2O5 and Ta2O5, which facilitates a liquid phase, enhances grain boundary diffusion, and accelerates grain growth at lower temperatures [49,50]. At moderate doping levels, this liquid phase promotes uniform densification, whereas excessive liquid formation at higher Li+ concentrations enhances grain boundary diffusion and thus grain growth. This microstructural trend is also reflected in the relative densities of the ceramics, which were measured at 90%, 91%, 92%, 94%, 93%, and 95% for AN, ANT, ANT-0.01LN, ANT-0.03LN, ANT-0.05LN, and ANT-0.07LN, respectively.

In other words, grain refinement through compositional design is an effective way to improve *E*b and, consequently, *W*rec and *η*. These observations highlight the importance of microstructural engineering in optimizing the energy-storage performance of AN-based ceramics, as discussed further in Section 3.4.

To explore the nanodomain structure and crystallography, transmission electron microscopy (TEM) analysis was conducted on two representative samples: ANT (as a model of AFE behavior) and ANT-0.03LN (representing R-AFE-like behavior). The bright-field TEM image of the ANT ceramic (Fig. 5(a)) shows a well-defined domain structure composed of lamellar regions approximately 150–200 nm wide. The corresponding high-resolution TEM (HRTEM) image (Fig. 5(b)) taken near the domain boundary shows clear lattice fringes corresponding to the ( 101¯) and (0 1¯1) planes, with interplanar spacings of 0.139 and 0.278 nm, respectively [24,52]. The selected area electron diffraction (SAED) pattern (Fig. 5(c)) indexed along the [111] zone axis further confirms the long-range order and orthorhombic symmetry of the ANT ceramic.

In comparison, the Li+/Nd3+ codoped ANT-0.03LN sample (Fig. 5(d)) displays much finer nanodomain structures, with widths reduced to approximately 20–30 nm. This domain miniaturization indicates a collapse of the large-scale morphology associated with long-range antipolar ordering and a transition toward relaxor-like behavior. Such a reduced domain size can facilitate faster and more uniform switching under an external electric field while lowering hysteresis loss. The HRTEM image (Fig. 5(e)) displays lattice fringes that correspond to the (200) plane with an interplanar spacing of 0.279 nm. Together, these materials provide strong microstructural support for the superior energy-storage performance observed in Li+/Nd3+-codoped AN-based ceramics.

### 3.4 Dielectric, ferroelectric, and energy-storage properties of AN-based ceramics

The temperature-dependent dielectric permittivity (*ε*r) and loss (tan*δ*) of AN-based ceramics were measured from 25 to 500 °C at various frequencies (10 kHz, 100 kHz, and 1 MHz) to investigate the phase transitions and thermal stability (Fig. 6 and Fig. S5 in the ESM). The corresponding phase transition temperatures are summarized in Table S1 in the ESM. Pristine AN exhibits four distinct dielectric anomalies at approximately 90, 286, 390, and 417 °C, which correspond to the M1‒M2, M2‒M3, M3‒O, and O‒T phase transitions, respectively (Fig. S5(a)), which is consistent with earlier reports [53,54]. Furthermore, B-site Ta5+ doping (ANT) caused a downward shift in these transitions, with the M1–M2 and M2–M3 transitions occurring at 85 and 207 °C, respectively (Fig. S5(c) in the ESM). With additional A-site codoping of Li+ and Nd3+, the transitions are further suppressed; for example, the M1–M2 and M2–M3 transitions in ANT-0.01LN appear at 47 and 174 °C, respectively (Fig. 6(a1)). For *x* ≥ 0.03, the transition temperatures between M1–M2 further shifted toward lower temperatures (Figs. 6(b1)–6(d1)), which indicates that polar-to-nonpolar transitions (*Pmc*21 → *Pbcm*) are facilitated by higher dopant concentrations. This stabilization of the nonpolar *Pbcm* phase at room temperature is consistent with the Rietveld refinement results, which show nearly 100% nonpolar phase in highly doped samples (Table 1).

### 3.5 Applications of dielectric capacitors

To assess the practical viability of ANT-based ceramics in high-power capacitor applications, the charge‒discharge performance of the ANT-0.03LN ceramic was studied under real-life circuit conditions. Figure 9 shows the discharge behavior of the ANT-0.03LN ceramic under both overdamped and underdamped conditions, with the RC (for the overdamped test) and RLC (for the underdamped test) circuit configurations illustrated in Fig. S10 in the ESM. In overdamped mode (Fig. 9(a)), the peak discharge current increases from 12 to 23 A with increasing electric field strength. The corresponding energy delivery dynamics are plotted in Fig. 9(b), where the saturation time constant *t*0.9, the time needed to release 90% of the stored energy, is approximately 40 ns. This ultrafast response highlights the material’s ability for rapid energy release and makes it a strong candidate for high-power pulsed electronic applications.

## 4 Conclusions

This study presents a compositionally engineered AgNbO3-based perovskite system that achieves high-performance dielectric energy storage through dual A-/B-site substitution. By fixing the Ta5+ content at 20 mol% on the B-site and systematically introducing Li+/Nd3+ codoping on the A-site, we induced a tunable transformation in the ferroelectric behavior of the material, from the conventional antiferroelectric (AFE) to the relaxor-antiferroelectric-like (R-AFE-like) regime. This evolution is supported by a combination of structural, electrical, and microstructural evidence, including an increase in the Curie‒Weiss diffuseness exponent, the suppression of field-induced switching behavior, and the formation of nanodomains. Structural analysis further confirmed that A-site chemical disorder plays a primary role in disrupting long-range polar coherence, whereas B-site Ta5+ substitution assists in suppressing octahedral tilting and stabilizing the nonpolar phase. The optimized composition, (Ag1−4*x*Li*x*Nd*x*)(Nb0.8Ta0.2)O3 at *x* = 0.03, delivered an impressive recoverable energy density of 7.2 J/cm3 and a high energy efficiency of 92.3% under a moderate electric field of 327 kV/cm. In addition to these intrinsic properties, the material also showed excellent frequency and thermal stability as well as ultrafast discharge behavior (*t*0.9 ≈ 40 ns) and peak power density (172 MW/cm3). These results validate the strong practical potential of next-generation pulsed power capacitors. Overall, this work offers a clear and effective structure–property–performance roadmap for designing tunable relaxor antiferroelectrics with significantly enhanced dielectric energy storage capabilities in lead-free ceramic systems.
