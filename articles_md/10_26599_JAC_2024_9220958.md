## 1 Introduction

Piezoelectric materials that allow the conversion of mechanical and electrical energy represent an important technological frontier in biomedical instrumentation, electronic information, microelectromechanical systems, automatic control, and electromechanical energy transfer [1−4]. Among the piezoelectric population, PbTiO3 (PT)-based relaxor ferroelectrics are considered very promising candidates for high-performance electromechanical devices because of their excellent piezoelectric properties. For relaxor ferroelectrics, a key feature is the existence of polar nanoregions (PNRs), which possess nanoscale inhomogeneity and coexist with normal domain structures and have important effects on piezoelectric and dielectric properties. In 2016, Li *et al*. [5] quantitatively characterized the contribution of PNRs to the room-temperature piezoelectric and dielectric properties of relaxor ferroelectrics, which is in the range of 50%–80%. The mechanism of the origin of high piezoelectricity in relaxor ferroelectrics was also proposed, which is the polarization rotation promoted by the PNRs aligned in the ferroelectric matrix, suggesting that the local nanostructures of the ferroelectrics play a key role in their macroscopic properties. Afterward, they used Sm doping modification strategy to enhance the local structural heterogeneity of Pb(Mg1/3Nb2/3)O3–PbTiO3 (PMN–PT) ceramics at the nanoscale, which in turn manipulated the interfacial energies, flattened the energy landscape and ultimately achieved an ultrahigh piezoelectric coefficient (*d*33, ~1500 pC/N) in Sm-doped PMN–PT ceramics [6]. Subsequently, the efficiency of rare-earth doping in improving piezoelectricity was widely validated in other ceramic systems [7−9]. However, the low Curie temperature (*T*C 10−12].

Among the currently reported high-temperature piezoelectric ceramics, binary BiScO3–PbTiO3 (BS–PT) is a typical candidate for achieving large *d*33 and high *T*C. For example, Ren *et al*. [13] recently reported large *d*33 of 553 pC/N and an ultrahigh *T*C of 445 °C in Nb-doped BS–64PT ceramics. However, the piezoelectric response of BS–64PT:Nb ceramics dramatically fluctuates within the temperature range of 30–400 °C, and *d*33 of BS–64PT:Nb ceramics increases from 553 to 1528 pC/N at 400 °C. A similar phenomenon has also been observed in Sb-doped BS–64PT ceramics [14]. Moreover, the high price of Sc2O3 is another non-negligible factor that hinders the widespread application of BS–PT ceramics [13−20]. Moreover, various strategies have been proposed for PT-based relaxor ferroelectric ceramics to achieve collaborative optimization of their piezoelectricity and temperature stability. For example, a high *d*33 of ~562 pC/N and superior thermal stability (the *d*33 variation is within 7% from 20 to 330 °C) are achieved in ZnO-doped Pb(Sb1/2Nb1/2)O3–PbZrO3–PbTiO3 (PSN–PZT) ceramics [10]. Excellent *d*33 of 680 pC/N and high *T*C of 330 °C are simultaneously obtained in Sm-modified Pb(In1/2Nb1/2)O3–PbZrO3–PbTiO3 (PIN–PZT) ceramics [21]. Recently, the texturing technique has attracted much attention for its remarkable ability to simultaneously achieve high piezoelectric properties and high Curie temperatures in piezoelectric ceramics. Li *et al*. [22] successfully prepared textured PZT ceramics with a high Curie temperature of 360 °C and a large piezoelectric coefficient *d*33 of 760 pC/N. In addition, several other ternary and quaternary PT-based ceramics have also been widely investigated to develop suitable candidates with both high piezoelectricity and high-temperature stability [23−28].

Based on current research findings, the high piezoelectricity of Pb-based ferroelectric ceramics usually depends on the composition-induced morphotropic phase boundary (MPB). However, the presence of MPB is always accompanied by a phase transition between ferroelectric phases at relatively low temperatures, even around room temperature. This ferroelectric phase transition, such as the rhombohedral (R) to T phase transition occurring at the temperature *T*r–t, results in thermodynamic imbalances, further leading to dramatic thermal fluctuations in electromechanical properties [29−31]. Taking Pb(Sc1/2Nb1/2)O3–Pb(Mg1/3Nb2/3)O3–PbTiO3 (PSN–PMN–PT) [25] and PIN–PZT [21] ceramics as examples, the variations in *d*33 values in MPB-composition samples were reported to be approximately 15%–65% around *T*r–t compared with those measured at room temperature. Therefore, suppressing/avoiding ferroelectric phase transition while maintaining a high Curie temperature is vital for improving the temperature stability of Pb-based ferroelectric ceramics.

Recent investigations have demonstrated that ternary Pb(Ni1/3Nb2/3)O3–PbZrO3-PbTiO3 (PNN–PZT) ceramics have excellent tunability between piezoelectric properties and the Curie temperature. For example, ultrahigh *d*33 of ~1200 pC/N was obtained in 0.552PNN–0.135PZ–0.313PT ceramics with the triple point composition [32,33]. High *T*C of 326 °C was obtained for 0.1PNN–0.9PZT ceramics [34]. Both high *d*33 of 1165 pC/N and high *T*C of 197 °C were achieved in [001]C-textured 0.36PNN–0.25PZ–0.39PT ceramics [35]. These studies show that the T-phase PNN–PZT ceramics positioned near the MPB, characterized by high Curie temperatures and the absence of ferroelectric phase transitions, represent promising candidates for achieving high-temperature stability. However, T-phase ferroelectric ceramics typically exhibit inferior piezoelectricity owing to their inherently stable polarization and limited response to external stimuli (electric field and stress). Current studies on rare-earth-doped ferroelectrics propose that the Sm-modification method can address this deficiency of T-phase ferroelectric ceramics. This suggests that Sm modification not only significantly improves the piezoelectricity of Pb-based ferroelectric ceramics but also preserves high Curie temperature while promoting T-phase structure formation and suppressing ferroelectric phase transitions [6−9].

Therefore, in this work, MPB compositions of 0.12PNN–0.46PZ–0.42PT ceramics with high Curie temperatures were selected to implement the Sm-modification strategy. The anticipated Sm-doped PNN–PZT ceramics with large piezoelectric response and high temperature stability were successfully obtained through the conventional solid-state reaction method. The dielectric, ferroelectric, and piezoelectric properties and temperature stability of prepared samples were systematically investigated. Moreover, the phase structures, microstructure morphologies, and local domain patterns of the Sm-doped PNN–PZT ceramics were also characterized and analyzed to elucidate the underlying physical mechanisms responsible for enhancing the piezoelectricity and temperature stability achieved through Sm doping.

## 2 Experimental

The 0.12PNN–0.46PZ–0.42PT:*x* mol% Sm (abbreviated as PNN–PZT:Sm) ceramics were prepared via the conventional solid-state reaction method. The raw materials of ZrO2 (99.9%, Aladdin, China), Nb2O5 (99.99%, Aladdin, China), Sm2O3 (99.99%, Aladdin, China), TiO2 (99.9%, D&B, China), 1.5 mol% excess PbO (99.9%, Aladdin, China) and 0.5 mol% excess NiO (99%, Aladdin, China) were processed sequentially according to the procedure of weighing, mixing, and ball-milling in alcohol for 24 h. Next, the mixed powders were dried and calcined at 750 °C for 2 h to obtain the PNN–PZT:Sm powders, and then the calcined powders were re-milled for 24 h and dried. Finally, the powders were pressed into a disc-shaped pellet under a pressure of 100 MPa and sintered at 1200–1260 °C for 2.5 h in air.

The phase structures and microstructure morphologies of PNN–PZT:Sm ceramics were investigated by an X-ray diffractometer (XRD; MiniFlex 600, Rigaku, Japan) and a scanning electron microscope (SEM; VEGA3/XUM, TESCAN, USA), respectively. The local domain structures were observed by using a piezoelectric force microscope (MFP-3D Origin+, Asylum Research, USA), in which the drive amplitude, set point and scanning rate were 0.5 V, 0 V, and 1 Hz, respectively. The temperature-dependent dielectric constant at various frequencies was measured via an LCR meter (E4980A, Agilent, USA). All ceramics were polished at room temperature by applying a direct current (DC) electric field of 35 kV/cm for 30 min, and the electric piezoelectric coefficient (*d*33) and dielectric constant (*ε*r) were measured after 24 h. The *ex-situ* quasi-static piezoelectric coefficient (*d*33) was measured by a piezo *d*33 meter (ZJ-4A, Institute of Acoustics, Chinese Academy of Sciences, China). A wide-temperature-range *d*33 test system (TZFD-600, Harbin Julang Technology Co., Ltd., China) was used to record the *in-situ* *d*33 values. A Premier II system (Radiant Technologies Inc., USA) was used to measure the ferroelectric hysteresis loops and electric-field-induced strain curves in combination with a temperature control system.

### 3.1 Phase structure and microstructure morphology

Figure 1(a) illustrates the phase diagram of PNN–PZT systems [35]. The selected ceramic composition of 0.12PNN–0.46PZ–0.42PT is located at the R–T phase boundary. Figure 1(b) shows the XRD patterns of undoped and Sm-doped PNN–PZT ceramics at room temperature. The pure perovskite structure of all the ceramics indicates that Sm additives are fully integrated into the crystal lattices. Moreover, the undoped sample exhibits a broadened (200) peak at approximately 44.5° without discernible splitting, indicating that the undoped sample has an MPB composition characterized by the coexistence of R and T phases. Furthermore, the (200) peak gradually splits into two peaks of (200) and (002) with increasing Sm content, which indicates that increasing the Sm content increases the percentage of the T-phase in PNN–PZT ceramics. In addition, to further investigate the phase structure evolution induced by Sm doping, the Rietveld refinement of the XRD patterns of the PNN–PZT:Sm ceramics, as well as the lattice parameters and refinement parameters, is given in Fig. S1 and Table S1 in the Electronic Supplementary Material (ESM). The samples with high Sm-doping contents (≥ 1 mol%) predominantly present a T-phase structure. According to the findings of Li *et al.* [6,36,37], the dopant Sm3+ is the smallest ion among rare-earth elements that can reach the A-site in PMN–PT rather than the B-site. This selective occupation not only induces significant local structural variations and enhances the piezoelectric response but also mitigates the detrimental effects associated with the disruption of ferroelectric long-range order and the generation of oxygen vacancies [6,36−40]. Consequently, Sm3+ is considered to be the most effective dopant for enhancing electromechanical responses, leading to the highest levels of piezoelectricity in specific PMN–PT materials [6,36−38]. Motivated by these pivotal and innovative studies on Sm-doped PMN–PT materials, we have extended this research to explore Sm-doped PNN–PZT ceramics. For Sm-doped PNN–PZT ceramics, the ionic radius of Pb2+ (CN = 12) at the A-site is 1.49 Å, while the ionic radii of Nb5+ (CN = 6), Ni2+ (CN = 6), Ti4+ (CN = 6), and Zr4+ (CN = 6) at the B-site are 0.64 Å, 0.69 Å, 0.605 Å, and 0.72 Å, respectively. It can thus be inferred that Sm3+ (1.24 Å, CN = 12), when doped into PNN–PZT, should substitute for Pb2+, thereby preferentially occupying the A-site, which is consistent with the behavior observed in Sm-doped PMN–PT systems.

The cross-section SEM images and grain size distributions of PNN–PZT:Sm ceramics are shown in Fig. 2. The overall elemental and Sm elemental distributions of the PNN–PZT ceramics doped with 2 mol% Sm are given in Figs. S2 and S3 in the ESM, respectively. As shown in Fig. 2, all the samples exhibit characteristics of high density, uniform grain distribution, and absence of pores. It can be observed that the incorporation of Sm significantly reduces the grain size of PNN–PZT ceramics. The average grain size of the undoped PNN–PZT ceramics is approximately 9.7 μm. As the Sm content increases, the grain size decreases consistently. At a Sm content of 2.5 mol%, the average grain size notably decreases to 1.7 μm. This finding indicates that Sm doping suppresses the grain growth of PNN–PZT ceramics. This phenomenon can be attributed to the presence of cation vacancies and vacancy-aliovalent ion pairs in Sm-doped PNN–PZT ceramics [41−44]. The comparable ion radii of the 12-coordinate Sm3+ ions (1.24 Å) and Pb2+ ions (1.49 Å) facilitate the substitution of A-site Pb2+ ions in the perovskite structure by introducing Sm3+ ions. Consequently, this substitution generates cation vacancies with negative charges that are predominantly concentrated at the grain boundaries [41,45,46]. Furthermore, the positively charged Sm3+ ions are drawn toward the cation vacancies, resulting in the formation of vacancy-aliovalent ion pairs in proximity to the grain boundaries through Coulombic interactions [41,47]. These vacancy-aliovalent ion pairs hinder the mass and energy transfer, thereby inhibiting grain growth. This phenomenon is consistent with previous studies involving Sm-doped PZT, PNN–PZT, Pb(Zn1/3Nb2/3)O3 (PZN)–PZT, PMN–PZT, and (K,Na)NbO3 (KNN) ceramics [44,48−51]. This fine-grain structure is anticipated to enhance the mechanical strength and electromechanical properties without size-dependent effects, which will be advantageous for high-frequency ultrasound transducer applications [51].

### 3.2 Ferroelectric and electromechanical properties

Figure 3 depicts the dependency of the piezoelectric coefficient (*d*33) of the PNN–PZT:Sm ceramics on the Sm content, which was measured by using a *d*33 piezo meter at room temperature. The corresponding room-temperature dielectric constant in samples with varying Sm contents is also presented in Fig. 3. Evidently, *d*33 substantially increases with increasing the Sm content. Specifically, *d*33 reaches the maximum value of 575 pC/N for the sample with 2 mol% Sm, representing a remarkable 3.6-fold increase over the undoped counterpart. Moreover, the dielectric constant of the Sm-doped PNN–PZT ceramics follows a similar trend as *d*33, which indicates that Sm doping is an effective strategy to improve the dielectric and piezoelectric properties of PNN–PZT ceramics.

Comparative analysis with other Pb-based piezoelectric ceramics reveals that the values of *S*max and *d*33* in PNN–PZT:2 mol% Sm ceramics surpass those of Sm-doped PSN-PMN–PT ceramics (*S*max and *d*33* measured at 20 kV/cm are 0.153% and 765 pm/V, respectively) [23], PMN–PT–BNZ ceramics (*S*max and *d*33* measured at 20 kV/cm are 0.175% and 875 pm/V, respectively) [52], Pb0.905Ba0.095(Zr0.54Ti0.46)O3–Nb2O5–Bi2O3–CdCO3 ceramics (*S*max and *d*33* measured at 25 kV/cm are 0.181% and 724 pm/V, respectively) [53], and various other systems [54,55]. This suggests that 2 mol% Sm-doped PNN–PZT with a T-phase structure possesses remarkable strain characteristics and holds significant potential for use in piezoelectric strain-type devices.

It is observed that all samples exhibit typical symmetric *P‒E* loops, and the Sm‒doped ceramics present a higher rectangular degree than their undoped counterparts. Specifically, the values of *P*s, *P*r, and Δ*P*/*P*s for the 2 mol% Sm-doped sample are 45.1 μC/cm2, 40.5 μC/cm2, and 10.2%, respectively, while for the undoped sample, these values are 22.5 μC/cm2, 16.3 μC/cm2, and 27.6%, respectively.

### 3.3 Temperature stability of electric performance

Figure 6 shows the temperature- and frequency-dependent dielectric constants of PNN–PZT:Sm ceramics. As depicted in Fig. 6(a), the undoped sample displays two distinct dielectric anomalies at *T*r–t (~175 °C) and *T*m (~324 °C), corresponding to the R–T and T–C phase transitions, respectively. For the 0.5 mol% Sm-doped sample, *T*r–t decreases to approximately 130 °C, as shown in Fig. 6(b). When the Sm content is increased to 1 mol%, the R‒T phase transition is suppressed and disappears within the measured temperature range. This phenomenon can be attributed to the increase in the T phase composition of the sample after Sm doping. In addition, *T*m decreases with increasing Sm content, as shown in Fig. 7. *T*m decreases by 53 °C from 324 °C for the undoped sample to 271 °C for the 2.5 mol% Sm-doped sample. Similar observations have been reported for other rare-earth-doped PT-based ferroelectric ceramics, such as Sm/Dy/Eu/Ho-doped PMN–PT [6−9,56], Sm/Pr-doped PIN–PMN–PT [24,57], Sm-doped PMN–PZT [51], PZN–PZT [58], and PNN–PZT [59,60]. However, it is noteworthy that the 1.5 and 2 mol% Sm-doped PNN–PZT ceramics still exhibit high *T*m values of approximately 300 and 280 °C, respectively, along with notable piezoelectric coefficients (*d*33) of 481 and 575 pC/N. These findings suggest that the introduction of Sm additives can slightly lower *T*m in PNN–PZT ceramics while significantly enhancing *d*33. Consequently, high-performance Sm-doped PNN–PZT ceramics with both a large piezoelectric response and high *T*m have been successfully achieved.

To comprehensively assess the temperature stability of Sm-doped PNN–PZT ceramics, the present work investigated temperature-dependent unipolar *S‒E* curves (measured at 20 kV/cm) and analyzed the variations in *d*33* and *d*33 with the temperature. In comparison, both *S*max and *d*33* for 1, 2, and 2.5 mol% Sm-doped samples exhibit minimal changes within the range of 30–180 °C. For example, the *d*33* of the 2 mol% Sm-doped samples is 890 pm/V at 30 °C and 965 pm/V at 180 °C, with a slight 8% variation, indicating excellent temperature stability of strain behavior in the Sm-doped samples.

### 3.4 Local domain structures

Owing to the crucial significance of domain structures in the electromechanical properties of ferroelectric materials, exploring the Sm-doping-induced evolution of local ferroelectric domain structures will contribute to understanding the underlying mechanisms for the high piezoelectricity observed in PNN–PZT:Sm ceramics. Therefore, the domain patterns of PNN–PZT ceramics with various Sm contents were investigated via vertical PFM imaging. The PFM amplitude and phase images of all the samples were captured over a consistent scanning region of 5 μm × 5 μm, as shown in Fig. 10. Evidently, the domain configuration and domain size undergo obvious alterations as the Sm content varies. For the undoped and 0.5 mol% Sm-doped samples, irregular lamellar domains with large domain dimensions can be observed, as delineated by the “☆” symbols in Figs. 10(a1) and 10(b1). These lamellar domains exhibit large domain sizes with widths spanning microns. The microstructure of the PNN–PZT:Sm ceramics shown in Fig. 2 suggests that the presence of these large lamellar domains in the undoped and 0.5 mol% Sm-doped ceramics can be attributed primarily to their large grain dimensions. Furthermore, some lamellar domains are interspersed with regularly striped domains approximately 100 nm in width.

### 3.5 Discussion

First, we discuss the underlying mechanisms driving the increase in piezoelectricity in Sm-doped PNN–PZT ceramics. According to the thermodynamic theory, there exists a classical relationship of *d* = 2*P*s*Qε*, where *P*s and *Q* represent the spontaneous polarization intensity and the electrostriction coefficient, respectively, and *ε* denotes the dielectric constant. Generally, the *Q* value is assumed to be constant within the same system. It can be inferred that the increased piezoelectric constant (*d*33) in the Sm-doped PNN–PZT ceramics stems from the increase in both their dielectric constant (*ε*r) and remanent polarization (*P*r). The experimental results demonstrate that the *d*33, *ε*r, and *P*r values of the 2 mol% Sm-doped sample are increased by 266%, 170%, and 148%, respectively, compared with those of the undoped sample. This observation confirms the collaborative role of *ε*r and *P*r in improving the piezoelectric properties.

Research on the microscopic domain structure suggests that the enhanced *ε*r and *P*r in Sm-doped PNN–PZT ceramics can be attributed to the evolution of the domain structure, specifically, a reduction in domain size leading to performance enhancement. The decreased domain size of PNN–PZT ceramics can primarily be ascribed to decreased grain size following Sm-doping. It is generally known that stable domain configurations in ferroelectric systems arise from equilibrium among various energies, including electric energy, elastic energy, and domain wall energy. Previous thermodynamic theories have investigated the energy equilibrium involving elastic interactions between the system and a semi-infinite medium, as well as that of the domain walls, resulting in a parabolic scaling (domain size * ∝ (grain size)m*, *m* = 1/2) in the relationship between the grain size and domain size [61−63]. Consequently, the decreased grain size owing to the Sm additives suppressing grain growth leads to a decrease in domain size in Sm-doped PNN–PZT ceramics.

The miniaturized ferroelectric domains can reduce the domain wall energy in crystals while enhancing the ability of domain wall motion [64−66]. Moreover, a high domain density can also improve the contribution of overall domain wall motion to the extrinsic dielectric response. In addition, the small-sized domains (as shown in Fig. 10(e1)) can serve as “accelerators” during the polarization switching process, effectively lowering the energy barrier for polarization switching [66−68]. Consequently, the decreased domain size can improve the dielectric response in both intrinsic (polarization switching/lattice deformation) and extrinsic (domain wall motion) contributions. This finding is not consistent with the general observation that the coercive field decreases with increasing piezoelectricity. In this study, the concurrent increase in the piezoelectricity and coercive electric field is attributed to the synergistic effect of domain and grain engineering. First, the miniaturized ferroelectric domains enhance piezoelectricity and improve domain motion. Moreover, the reduced grain size can enhance the coercive electric field due to the space-charge effect. According to the “space-charge layer model”, the space-charge layer formed by impurity ions or vacancies during the aging process of ceramics typically exists at grain boundaries and domain walls [69−72]. Under external electric fields, an internal electric field opposite to the external field is generated, impeding the domain wall movement. As the grain size decreases and the number of grain boundaries increases, the intensity of this internal electric field also increases, leading to an increased coercive field. Therefore, the synergistic and competitive effects of reduced domain size and decreased grain size result in the simultaneous enhancement of piezoelectricity and coercive electric field. As an example, in the case of 2.5 mol% Sm-doped example with a minimum grain size of 1.7 μm, a decrease in *d*33 and *ε*r, coupled with an increase in *E*c, is observed due to the dominant influence of the space-charge layer on the electromechanical properties. In this case, the inhibitory effect on domain motion by the space-charge layer surpasses the promoting effect of domain motion by the domain size effect. Consequently, the 2.5 mol% Sm-doped sample exhibits a decrease in *d*33 and *ε*r, while *E*c increases.

According to established thermodynamic theories, for PbTiO3–PbZrO3–∑Pb(B1B2)O3 solid solution systems, the intensity of *P*r can be ascribed to the intrinsic strain parameter (*δ*) and extrinsic wall mobility (*η*) of non-180° domains [73,74]. In this case, *P*r within the R- and T-phases can be expressed as Eqs. (1) and (2) [73,74]:

(1) Pr=A1k(13+23η)δ(T-phase)

(2) Pr=B1k(14+34η)δ(R-phase)

where *A* and *B* are the structure coefficients, and *k* represents the proportionality coefficient between the magnitudes of *δ* and saturated polarization (*P*s), according to the relation of *δ* = * kps2. Typically, T-phase samples exhibit higher δ* due to their intrinsic structure featuring a high *c*/*a* value [73,74]. During the general phase structure evolution induced by PT composition changes in PZT-based ceramics progressing through R–MPB–T phases, the parameter *η* gradually decreases in an inversely proportional manner to *δ*, leading to reduced *P*r in the T-phase [73,74]. This reduction is attributed to elevated internal stresses generated during the significant lattice distortion that accompanies the formation of spontaneous polarization of T-phase compositions, which in turn hinders domain wall motion [73,74]. For special examples of Sm-doped PNN–PZT ceramics, the presence of fine grains leads to a miniaturized domain structure with a high density of domain walls. It is hypothesized that the miniaturized domain structure and high domain wall density enhance the mobility of non-180° (90°) domain walls and increase the *η* value, thus disrupting the typical relationship between *δ*, *η*, and *P*r. These miniaturized domain structures may alleviate internal stresses associated with 90° domain reorientation and promote domain wall mobility.

The excellent temperature stability exhibited by Sm-doped PNN–PZT ceramics can be attributed to the following two factors: (i) high *T*m in T-phase PNN–PZT:Sm ceramics; (ii) Sm-doping improves the composition of the T-phase structure and suppresses the R–T ferroelectric phase transition within a practical temperature range. Table 1 summarizes the comparison of piezoelectric properties and temperature stability between 2 mol% Sm-doped PNN–PZT ceramics and other reported typical piezoelectric ceramics. It is evident that the T-phase PNN–PZT:Sm ceramics exhibit a significant advantage in temperature stability while maintaining excellent piezoelectricity. These findings indicate the considerable potential of T-phase PNN–PZT:Sm ceramics in applications requiring high-temperature stability.

## 4 Conclusions

In summary, T-phase Sm-doped PNN–PZT ceramics with a large piezoelectric response and high temperature stability were successfully fabricated. Compared with existing piezoelectric ceramics, the prepared ceramics present significant application advantages. The crystal structures, microstructures, local domain structures, electromechanical properties, and temperature stability of Sm-doped PNN–PZT ceramics were systematically investigated. The results show that 2 mol% Sm-doped ceramics exhibit large piezoelectric coefficient (*d*33) of 575 pC/N and high *T*m of 279 °C, with effective piezoelectric strain constant (*d*33*) of 890 pm/V. Furthermore, the prepared samples exhibit outstanding temperature stability of piezoelectric properties across a wide temperature range, with *d*33 varying by only 2.6% within the temperature range of 30–250 °C and *d*33* changing by 8% within the temperature range of 30–180 °C. The enhanced temperature stability of the Sm-doped ceramics is attributed to the suppression effect of Sm doping on the R–T ferroelectric transition. Moreover, the *d*33, *ε*r, and *P*r values of the 2 mol% Sm-doped samples are increased by 266%, 170%, and 148%, respectively, compared with those of the undoped samples. Analysis of the microstructure morphology and domain structure suggests that Sm-doing effectively reduces the grain size, subsequently decreasing the domain size and thereby contributing to improvements in the dielectric, ferroelectric, and piezoelectric properties. Our results indicate that the introduction of Sm additives can effectively modulate the microstructures and electromechanical properties of PNN–PZT ceramics to obtain high piezoelectricity and high-temperature stability simultaneously.
