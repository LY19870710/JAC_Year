## 1 Introduction

Since the first laser device was reported in 1960 [1], laser technology has become widespread and is used in many fields [2,3]. However, it has not stopped developing along with technological advances by adapting through the progression of materials and systems to the requirements of innovative applications. In high-performance laser devices, Faraday isolators are one of the important components that can prevent the front-end system from disturbance and damage caused by a back-reflected beam [4]. Magneto-optical materials are thus widely studied as they are key elements of Faraday isolators [5]. Among magneto-optical materials applied in the visible to near-infrared wavelength band, Tb3Ga5O12 (TGG) single crystals are now dominating the market [6] due to their excellent comprehensive performance, including low optical loss, high Verdet constant, high thermal conductivity, high laser damage threshold, and good thermally induced depolarization performance [7,8]. To further improve the performance of Faraday isolators, research into new magneto-optical materials with better performance has not stopped in recent years. Up to now, Tb2O3 [9−12], Dy2O3 [13], Ho2O3 [14,15], Tb3Sc2Al3O12 [16−18], KTb3F10 [19], TbF3 [20], LiTbF4 [21], CeF3 [22,23], Tb2Hf2O7 [24−26], Tb2Ti2O7 [27], Tb2Zr2O7 [28], rare-earth (RE) doped Y2O3 [29], and many other magneto-optical materials are under extensive study.

As one of the most promising alternative materials, Tb3Al5O12 (TAG), with its higher Verdet constant and thermal conductivity compared with TGG crystals, has attracted much attention over the last years [30−32]. However, TAGs are incongruent melting materials, so the manufacturing of TAG single crystals with large apertures and high quality is rather challenging. Therefore, fabricating TAG in the form of transparent ceramics may be more appropriate as melting is not required. Transparent TAG ceramics were first reported in 2011 [33] and have proven to possess excellent thermally induced depolarization properties due to their high Verdet constant and good thermo-optical properties [34−37]. In recent years, much experimental work has been carried out to improve the performance of TAG ceramics [38−44]. However, the manufacturing process of TAG ceramics should be further optimized to reduce the optical loss and make them applicable for practical use. Some works have previously shown that Y2O3 may have a positive effect on the properties of TAG ceramics. For example, Chen *et* *al*. [45,46] and Duan *et* *al*. [47] added Y2O3 to TAG ceramics and improved the optical quality. On the other hand, Aung and Ikesue [48] succeeded in fabricating (Tb0.6Y0.4)3Al5O12 (TYAG) ceramics, not only with high optical quality but also increasing their size. However, the mechanism of action of the Y2O3 additive leading to the improvement of the optical quality of TYAG ceramics has not been described. In addition, thermal conductivity is an important parameter for magneto-optical ceramics, but the effect of Y2O3 on the thermal conductivity of TYAG ceramics has also not been reported yet. As Y3+ has a smaller ionic radius compared with Tb3+ [49], the anti-site defect can more easily form after Y substitution in TAG, which implies a possibility of increasing the solid-solution range, suppressing the secondary phases, and improving the optical quality of TAG ceramics.

In this work, Y2O3 was added in various amounts as an additive to investigate the mechanisms leading to the improvement of the optical quality of TAG ceramics. (Tb1−*x*Y*x*)3Al5O12 (*x* = 0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, and 0.3) ceramics were fabricated by solid-state reaction sintering. Vacuum sintering combined with hot isostatic pressing (HIP) post-treatment was conducted. The crystal structure, microstructure, optical transmittance, Verdet constant, and thermal properties of obtained ceramics were investigated in detail.

## 2 Experimental

High-purity micron Tb4O7 powders (99.999%, Sheeny Metal Materials Co., Ltd., China), high-purity submicron α-Al2O3 powders (99.99%, Sumitomo Chemical Co., Ltd., Japan), and high-purity micron Y2O3 powders (99.999%, Golden Dragon Rare-earth Co., Ltd., China) were used as the raw powders. The raw powders were firstly calcined at 800 ℃ for 4 h in a muffle furnace. Then, the calcined powders were weighed according to the (Tb1−*x*Y*x*)3Al5O12 (*x* = 0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, and 0.3) stoichiometry and 0.3 wt% tetraethyl orthosilicate (TEOS, 99.999%, Alfa Aesar Chemical Co., Ltd., China) was added as the sintering aid. The raw powders and sintering aid were ball-milled in anhydrous alcohol for 15 h. The milled slurries were dried and sieved in 200-mesh screens to obtain mixed powders. Then, the mixed powders were uniaxially pressed into pellets under 20 MPa and cold isostatically pressed under 250 MPa for 5 min. The obtained green bodies were calcined at 1300 ℃ for 2 h to remove impurities, and the raw powders were partially reactive sintered to prevent the green bodies from cracking during the following sintering. Then, the calcined green bodies were pre-sintered at 1750 ℃ for 20 h in a vacuum and HIP post-treated at 1700 ℃ for 3 h under 200 MPa in argon. After annealing in a muffle furnace at 1450 ℃ for 20 h and mirror polishing, the transparent ceramics were obtained.

Phase characterization was performed by an X-ray diffractometer (XRD; D8 ADVANCE, Bruker, Germany) equipped with a copper target X-ray tube in the 2*θ* range of 10°–80° (step length of 0.02°, scanning rate of 0.3 s/step). Compositions, lattice parameters, and theoretical densities of the (Tb1−*x*Y*x*)3Al5O12 ceramics were calculated from the XRD patterns by Rietveld refinements of the crystallographic structure. Archimedes’ method was used to measure the density of ceramics. Microstructure of the thermally etched surfaces and energy-dispersive X-ray spectrometry (EDS) mapping were analyzed by a field emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM; SU-8220, Hitachi, Japan). To characterize the microstructures of the fabricated ceramics, the polished (Tb1−*x*Y*x*)3Al5O12 ceramic specimens were thermally etched at 1300 ℃ for 20 h in a muffle furnace before characterizations. The average grain size and grain size distribution map were characterized by more than 100 grains. Field emission transmission electron microscopy (TEM; Tecnai G2 F20, FEI, USA) was used to analyze the fine structure of the ceramics. A transmission optical microscope (BX51TF, Olympus, Japan) equipped with two polarizers was also used to characterize the microstructure of the ceramics. The X-ray photoelectron spectroscope (XPS; ESCALAB 250, ThermoFisher, UK) was used to characterize the valance state of Tb in the ceramics annealed in air. The obtained binding energy was calibrated using the C 1s peak at 284.8 eV. The in-line transmittances of (Tb1−*x*Y*x*)3Al5O12 ceramics were characterized with an ultraviolet–visible-near-infrared (UV–Vis–NIR) spectrophotometer (Cary-5000, Varian, USA). The Verdet constants of the ceramics with different Y2O3 contents at 633 nm were characterized by an extinction method with a Faraday effect experimental instrument (FD-FZ-C, Fudan Tianxin, China). Thermal diffusivity was measured by a laser flash method conducted on a high-temperature laser thermal conductance instrument (TA DLF2800, Waters, USA). Measurement was conducted at 25 and 100 ℃ in air. To measure the thermal diffusivity, (Tb1−*x*Y*x*)3Al5O12 ceramics were processed to a plate with a diameter of 12.7 mm and a thickness of 1.0 mm. The surface of ceramics was finely ground and washed, then coated with carbon as the absorption coating (2–5 μm thick). Specific heat was measured by differential scanning calorimetry on a high-temperature specific heat tester (MHTC96, Setaram, France). Specific heat measurement was also conducted at 25 and 100 ℃ in the N2 atmosphere, and the sample size was 5.5 mm in diameter and 0.5 mm in thickness.

## 3 Results and discussion

All the ceramics with different Y contents have a single garnet phase, while the diffraction peaks shift to a greater angle with the Y substituting, as Y3+ has a smaller ionic radius compared with Tb3+ [49]. The lattice parameters and theoretical densities of (Tb1−*x*Y*x*)3Al5O12 ceramics were calculated from the XRD results, as shown in Fig. 1(b) and Table 1. With the increase of Y content, the lattice parameter decreases from 12.074 to 12.054 Å, and the corresponding calculated density decreases from 6.065 to 5.629 g/cm3. The relative density of the (Tb1−*x*Y*x*)3Al5O12 ceramics was measured to be higher than 99% in this work. A good agreement between nominal composition and refined composition was obtained with a difference lower than 1%, as shown in Table 1.

Figures 2(a)–2(e) show the thermally etched surface micrographs and grain size distribution of the (Tb1−*x*Y*x*)3Al5O12 (*x* = 0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, and 0.3) ceramics pre-sintered at 1750 ℃ for 20 h and HIP post-treated at 1700 ℃. The small bright dots in Fig. 2 are impurities brought during the FE-SEM sample preparation. The dark spots (circled) can be ascribed to secondary phases in the TAG ceramic sample, as shown in Figs. 2(a)–2(c), and with the increase of Y content, the observed number of dark spots reduces significantly. When the Y content is higher than 10 at%, no obvious secondary phases can be detected. The EDS patterns of TAG ceramics (*x* = 0) are shown in Fig. 3. The secondary phase is identified to be the Al-rich phase, which is most likely to be the Al2O3 secondary phase [50]. The Al-rich phase may arise from the deviation of the stoichiometric ratio, mainly resulting from two causes: The first one is the abrasion of Al2O3 ball-milling media, which can be reduced by using other media with better wear resistance, such as yttria-stabilized zirconia (YSZ) and Si3N4, but the introduction of Zr4+ and Si4+ may also make a difference on the sintering behavior and may deteriorate the magneto-optical performance [43,51−53]; secondly, the error of powder weighing. The accurate composition of Tb4O7 raw powders is difficult to determine because it can be a mixture of TbO1.74 and TbO1.81 [54]. The changes of microstructure in Fig. 2 reveal that the addition of Y2O3 may increase the solubility of (Tb1−*x*Y*x*)3Al5O12 ceramics and suppress the forming of Al-rich secondary phases. The mechanism of the increase of the solubility can be explained as that the ionic radius of Y3+ is smaller than Tb3+, so the anti-site defects * YAlx and  AlYx can be formed more easily compared with  TbAlx and  AlTbx, respectively [55−57]. In this work, the excess Al2O3 is presumed to form  AlYx in (Tb1−x*Y*x*)3Al5O12 ceramics. The grain sizes of (Tb1−*x*Y*x*)3Al5O12 ceramics with different Y contents have similar distributions, and the average grain sizes of the obtained ceramics are shown in Fig. 2(f). When substituting Tb3+ with 0, 5, 10, 20, and 30 at% Y3+ in TAG, the average grain sizes are 20.0±0.7, 20.7±0.7, 26.3±0.6, 35.3±1.1, and 34.8±1.0 μm, respectively. There is no abnormal grain growth, but a grain size increasing trend is observed with the increasing Y substitution, which is similar to SiO2 [58] and ZrO2 [43] additives with reasonable addition amounts. The mechanism of Y2O3 promoting grain growth may be the suppression of the secondary phase, so the pinning effect of the secondary phase is also suppressed. This can also explain that the average grain size of (Tb0.7Y0.3)3Al5O12 ceramics is almost equal to that of (Tb0.8Y0.2)3Al5O12, as the secondary phases have been mostly suppressed after 20 at% Y substitution. From this perspective, Y2O3 is a workable additive that increases the solubility of TAG ceramics to suppress the forming of secondary phases, and it is not likely to cause abnormal grain growth.

These results have a good accordance with those in Fig. 2 and show again that the addition of Y2O3 may increase the solubility of (Tb1−*x*Y*x*)3Al5O12 ceramics and suppress the forming of secondary phases.

Figure 6(a) shows the photograph and in-line transmittance of the (Tb1−x*Y*x*)3Al5O12 ceramics. All the ceramic samples are transparent and colorless, which may imply that the Tb4+ concentration is quite low as Tb4+ causes strong absorption and makes the ceramics in brown color [59]. When the Y content increases from 0 to 20 at%, the in-line transmittance increases. When the Y content reaches 30 at%, there is a decline of the in-line transmittance, as shown in Fig. 6(a), which can be attributed to the residual pores scattering the light, as shown in Fig. 4(i). As the composition varies a lot, the sintering schedule in this work may be not optimal for the (Tb0.7Y0.3)3Al5O12 ceramics. The results for optical transmittance are in good accordance with the microstructures shown in Figs. 2 and 4. Herein, the (Tb0.8Y0.2)3Al5O12 ceramics possess the highest in-line transmittance, which is 82.9% at 1064 nm and 82.2% at 633 nm, respectively. Verdet constant represents the ability of the materials to rotate the polarization plane of light and is one of the most important performance parameters for magneto-optical ceramics. For paramagnetic ceramics, the Verdet constant can be calculated by Eq. (3):

(3) θ/L=V⋅B

where θ* is the Faraday rotation angle; *L* is the sample length along the direction of light transmission; *V* is the Verdet constant; and *B* is the magnetic field intensity. Figure 8(a) shows the relation of *θ*/*L* versus *B*. Apparently, the slope of the fitted line is the value of the Verdet constant. The Verdet constants of (Tb1−*x*Y*x*)3Al5O12 ceramics with *x* = 0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, and 0.3 are measured to be −188.1, −175.4, −168.5, −143.0, and −119.9 rad/(T·m), respectively, as shown in Fig. 8(b). Y2O3 additive reduces the Verdet constant of (Tb1−*x*Y*x*)3Al5O12 ceramics as Y3+ substitutes Tb3+ thereby directly reducing the concentration of paramagnetic ions [24]. There is no doubt that a reduction of the Verdet constant is unfavorable for high-performance magneto-optical ceramics. However, though the Verdet constant decreases after adding Y2O3 in TAG ceramics, the Verdet constant of (Tb0.8Y0.2)3Al5O12 ceramics is still higher than that of TGG crystals (about −136 rad/(T·m)). The practical performance of magneto-optical ceramics not only depends on the Verdet constant but also has a close relation with optical quality, thermal properties, thermo-optic properties, and so on.

Thermal effects play a significant role in high-power isolator applications. For TAG ceramics, which is one of the most promising magneto-optical materials for high-power isolators, thermal properties are quite important parameters. First of all, as is known, thermal conductivity significantly affects the performance of thermally induced depolarization of magneto-optical materials [63]. Figure 9 shows the thermal diffusivity, specific heat capacity, and thermal conductivity of (Tb1−*x*Y*x*)3Al5O12 (*x* = 0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, and 0.3) ceramics measured at 25 and 100 ℃. With the increase of Y content, the thermal diffusivity decreases, and the thermal diffusivities at 100 ℃ are lower than those at 25 ℃, as shown in Fig. 9(a). After adding Y2O3 in TAG ceramics, the specific heat capacity changes less than 0.04 J/(kg·K) at 25 ℃, as shown in Fig. 9(b), and there is a similar tendency at 100 ℃. The thermal conductivity of ceramics can be calculated by Eq. (4) [62]:

At 25 ℃, the thermal conductivities of (Tb1−*x*Y*x*)3Al5O12 ceramics with *x* = 0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, and 0.3 are calculated to be 5.23±0.21, 5.16±0.21, 4.78±0.19, 4.74±0.19, and 4.29±0.17 W/(m·K), respectively. The results show that the Y additive causes a decrease in thermal conductivity in (Tb1−*x*Y*x*)3Al5O12 ceramics because doping ions are usually phonon scattering centers and shorten the mean free path of phonon. The obtained thermal conductivity of TAG ceramics in this work is lower than some reported values of TAG ceramics [33,36] and crystals [30] (~7.13 W/(m·K)). However, the obtained value is still higher than TGG materials [64] and TSAG crystals [65]. Apart from the measurement errors, the difference in the thermal conductivity of TAG ceramics between this work and other works is mainly attributed to the residual defects in the ceramics, for example, residual pores and secondary phases, as they bring phonon scattering [62]. After adding 20 at% Y, the thermal conductivity of the TAG ceramics decreases by 9.4% in this work. Based on this reduction value (9.4%) and the thermal conductivity of TAG single crystal (7.13 W/(m·K)) [30], the (Tb0.8Y0.2)3Al5O12 ceramics without significant residual pores may have a thermal conductivity of 6.46 W/(m·K) in an ideal situation, which is still much higher than that of TGG crystals and ceramics.

## 4 Conclusions

Magneto-optical (Tb1−*x*Y*x*)3Al5O12 (*x* = 0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, and 0.3) ceramics with high optical transmittance were fabricated successfully. The effect of Y2O3 on the phase and crystal structure, microstructure, optical transmittance, Verdet constant, and thermal properties of (Tb1−*x*Y*x*)3Al5O12 ceramics was investigated in detail. The lattice parameter and calculated density of (Tb1−*x*Y*x*)3Al5O12 ceramics decrease with the increase of Y2O3. Y2O3 can improve the optical transmittance of the ceramics by suppressing the secondary phase, probably due to the increase of solubility. Furthermore, Y2O3 additive is not likely to cause abnormal grain growth when the Y amount is less than 30 at%. The (Tb0.8Y0.2)3Al5O12 ceramics show thin, clean, and distinct grain boundaries. The optical loss at 1064 nm of TAG ceramics decreases from 0.486 to 0.0654 cm−1 after 20 at% Y substitution. The (Tb0.8Y0.2)3Al5O12 ceramics possess the highest in-line transmittance value in this work, which is 82.9% at 1064 nm and 82.2% at 633 nm, respectively. The Verdet constant of TAG ceramics is measured to be −188.1 rad/(T·m) at 633 nm, and with the 5, 10, 20, and 30 at% Y substitution, the Verdet constant decreases by 6.8%, 10.4%, 24.0%, and 36.2%, respectively. The thermal conductivity of TAG ceramics is calculated to be 5.23 W/(m·K) at 25 ℃, and the value decreases by only 9.4% after adding 20 at% Y. As the optical loss directly affects the extinction ratio, insertion loss, change of beam quality, and many other performances for Faraday isolators, it is one of the most concerning properties for magneto-optical ceramics. Therefore, Y2O3 can be preliminarily considered to be a promising and effective additive for improving quality of TAG ceramics for magneto-optical applications.
