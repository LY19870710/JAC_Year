## 1 Introduction

With the rapid advancement of electronic information technology, ferroelectric materials have gained significant attention across various fields, including energy storage/harvesting devices, piezoelectric/dielectric devices, neuromorphic devices, memristors, and nonvolatile memories [1−8]. However, conventional ferroelectric materials such as BaTiO3 and Pb(Zr,Ti)O3 typically have thicknesses exceeding 100 nm and require large operating voltages (≥ 10 V) and high crystallization temperatures (> 600 °C) to achieve optimal ferroelectric properties, presenting challenges for device miniaturization and integration [9−14]. Notably, ferroelectricity was first reported in 2011 for Si-doped HfO2 thin films with a thickness of 10 nm [15]. Since then, novel HfO2-based ferroelectric thin films have been extensively investigated due to their excellent scalability and superior compatibility with the complementary metal oxide semiconductor (CMOS) process [16,17].

In general, the ferroelectricity of HfO2-based thin films is primarily attributed to the noncentrosymmetric polar orthorhombic phase (o-phase) [18−20]. However, the o-phase in HfO2-based ferroelectric thin films is metastable, and as-grown HfO2-based thin films tend to form a centrosymmetric monoclinic phase (m-phase) [21]. Consequently, HfO2-based ferroelectric thin films often exhibit polycrystalline and multiphase characteristics, leading to a pronounced wake-up effect and poor endurance, thereby limiting their practical application [22,23]. To optimize the ferroelectricity of novel HfO2-based thin films, recent studies have focused on methods such as element doping (Zr, Si, Al, Y, La, etc.) [24−27], electrode capping [28], oxygen vacancy [29,30], and interface engineering [31]. Among the various compositions of HfO2-based ferroelectric thin films, (Hf,Zr)O2 stands out for its lower crystallization temperature (350–600 °C) and wide processing window. Moreover, HfO2-rich to ZrO2-rich (Hf,Zr)O2 thin films exhibit linear dielectric, ferroelectric, and antiferroelectric-like polarization responses [32−34]. Previous reports have indicated that (Hf,Zr)O2 thin films with top ZrO2 layers exhibit higher polarization and superior fatigue resistance [34,35]. The bottom HfO2 layers contribute to realizing uniform ferroelectricity and domain switching in (Hf,Zr)O2 thin films grown on differently oriented Si substrates [36]. Recently, Park *et* *al*. [37] conducted a comprehensive investigation into the structural characteristics and ferroelectric performances of nanolaminate and superlattice thin films composed of ZrO2 and HfO2 stacks. Peng *et* *al*. [38] further experimentally demonstrated that ZrO2/HfO2 nanolaminate superlattice capacitors exhibit significantly improved endurance compared to that of their solid-solution (Hf,Zr)O2 counterparts. Despite some recent improvements in the endurance of (Hf,Zr)O2 thin films, there remains a lack of an effective solution to simultaneously optimize both ferroelectricity and endurance. Compared to traditional ferroelectrics such as BaTiO3 and Pb(Zr,Ti)O3, novel (Hf,Zr)O2 ferroelectric thin films still face numerous challenges, such as wake-up and fatigue effects, which manifest as variations in polarization during electric field cycling. As a result, the wake-up effects and poor endurance of (Hf,Zr)O2 ferroelectric thin films continue to impede their application in advanced electronic devices. It is imperative to minimize the wake-up effect while enhancing the endurance of (Hf,Zr)O2 ferroelectric thin films.

In this study, we implemented a design strategy involving the inhomogeneous distribution of Hf/Zr in (Hf,Zr)O2 thin films to optimize their endurance while minimizing anti-ferroelectric-like wake-up effects. Leveraging the distinct phase structure characteristics and electrical properties of HfO2, Hf-rich Hf0.8Zr0.2O2, Hf0.5Zr0.5O2, Zr-rich Hf0.2Zr0.8O2, and ZrO2 [39,40], we designed and fabricated both homogeneous and inhomogeneous element distribution for (Hf,Zr)O2 thin films with a 1 : 1 ratio of Hf : Zr, as illustrated in Fig. 1(a). For simplicity, we referred to the homogeneous Hf0.5Zr0.5O2 thin films with alternating ZrO2 and HfO2 distributions as H0.5. The inhomogeneous Hf0.5Zr0.5O2 ferroelectric thin films were labeled H0.2/0.5/0.8 and H0/0.2/0.8/1, representing compositions of Hf0.2Zr0.8O2, Hf0.5Zr0.5O2, and Hf0.8Zr0.2O2, and ZrO2, Hf0.2Zr0.8O2, Hf0.8Zr0.2O2, and HfO2 from top to bottom, respectively. Our experimental results reveal that the distribution of Hf/Zr plays a critical role in the ferroelectric properties of Hf0.5Zr0.5O2 thin films. H0.2/0.5/0.8 thin films exhibited large remnant polarization (2*P*r, where 2*P*r = |+*P*r| + |−*P*r|) values of 32 μC/cm2 without demonstrating antiferroelectric-like characteristics in their pristine state and 41 μC/cm2 after undergoing 104 wake-up cycles under 2.5 V. Remarkably, the 2*P*r values remained at ~94% of their initial values even after 109 cycles, indicating a prolonged device lifetime with sustained large polarization achievable in this study. Additionally, the fabricated thin films also exhibited high dielectric tunability (≥ 26%) under a low operating voltage of 2.5 V both before and after wake-up.

### 2.1 Fabrication

Three variations of hafnium zirconium oxide ferroelectric thin films, designated H0.5, H0.2/0.5/0.8, and H0/0.2/0.8/1, with a combined Hf/Zr ratio of 1 : 1 were prepared utilizing atomic layer deposition (ALD) in this study. Metal–ferroelectric–metal (MFM)-structured capacitors were deposited on a Si substrate to evaluate the ferroelectric properties of these thin films. A schematic diagram illustrating the fabrication process for the MFM structures is shown in Fig. 1(a). After cleaning the Si substrate, tungsten (W) bottom electrodes were sputtered using a magnetron sputtering system. Subsequently, the substrates were placed into the ALD chamber at 270 °C to deposit the HfO2 and ZrO2 layers. As shown in Fig. 1(a), H0.5 consists of a periodicity of ZrO2 (1 cycle)–HfO2 (1 cycle), which is repeated for a total of 54 periods. H0.2/0.5/0.8 is composed of layers with varying periodicities, progressing from bottom to top: ZrO2 (1 cycle)–HfO2 (4 cycles), ZrO2 (1 cycle)–HfO2 (1 cycle), and ZrO2 (4 cycles)–HfO2 (1 cycle), repeated for 8, 14, and 8 periods, respectively. Similarly, the H0/0.2/0.8/1 features layers arranged from bottom to top: HfO2 (14 cycles), ZrO2 (1 cycle)–HfO2 (4 cycles), ZrO2 (4 cycles)–HfO2 (1 cycle), and ZrO2 (14 cycles), repeated for 1, 8, 8, and 1 periods, respectively. The precursors [(CH3)(C2H5)N]4Hf, [(CH3)(C2H5)N]4Zr, and H2O were used as Hf, Zr, and oxygen sources, respectively. The top W electrodes were then formed using a stencil mask and magnetron sputtering system. Finally, the MFM-structured capacitors underwent annealing via rapid thermal annealing at 450 °C for 30 s under a nitrogen (N2) atmosphere.

### 2.2 Characterizations

Grazing incidence X-ray diffractometer (GIXRD; Smartlab 9 kW, Rigaku, Japan) was used to identify the crystalline characteristics and phase structures. Transmission electron microscope (TEM; Talos F200X, FEI, USA) was employed to observe the microstructures and element distribution across the cross-sections. Piezoresponse force microscope (PFM; Cypher ES, Asylum Research, USA) was utilized to investigate the domain switching characteristics. The polarization vs. voltage hysteresis loops (*P*–*V* loops), current vs. voltage (*I*–*V*) curves, and capacitance vs. voltage (*C*–*V*) curves were analyzed using a ferroelectric tester (aixACCTF Analyzer 2000E, Aachen, German). To assess the endurance of the samples, endurance measurements were performed using a voltage amplitude of 2 V and a frequency of 1 MHz.

## 3 Results and discussion

The pure ZrO2 thin films are dominated by the tetragonal (t)-phase and characterized by double *P*–*V* loops and four polarization switching current peaks, while the pure HfO2 thin films exhibit a conspicuous nonferroelectric monoclinic (m)-phase and a linear relationship between the polarization and applied voltage, as shown in Fig. S1 in the Electronic Supplementary Material (ESM). This result is consistent with previous reports [33,41,42]. Figure 1(b) shows the GIXRD patterns of the H0.5, H0.2/0.5/0.8, and H0/0.2/0.8/1 thin films. An enlarged view focusing on the 2*θ* range of 28°–32° is presented in Fig. 1(c). Obviously, the diffraction peaks corresponding to the o-phase (111) and/or t-phase (101) at approximately 30°–31° can be easily identified, indicating the likely presence of a noncentrosymmetric polar o-phase in all three types of thin films, exhibiting high crystallinity. Some weak diffraction peaks of the m-phases (* 1¯11) and (111) are observed near ~28.5° and ~31.5°, respectively.

To further examine the crystallinity and thickness of the fabricated thin films at the nanoscale, TEM characterization was performed. Figures S2 and S3 in the ESM depict low-magnification and high-resolution cross-sectional TEM images, respectively, of representative MFM-structured capacitors of H0.5. The cross-sectional high-resolution TEM (HRTEM), fast Fourier transform (FFT), and inverse fast Fourier transform (IFFT) images of H0.5, H0.2/0.5/0.8, and H0/0.2/0.8/1, marked with red squares, are given in Figs. 1(d)–1(o). According to the TEM images, the thicknesses of the top electrode, ferroelectric films, and bottom electrode are ~35, ~10, and ~45 nm, respectively. Moreover, clear lattice fringes are visible in the HRTEM images. FFT diffractograms were obtained for each thin film in the red-framed regions using digital micrograph software, further confirming the formation of the ferroelectric o-phase. In addition to the o-phase, the m-phase is also observable in the FFT results for representative H0.5 (Fig. S3 in the ESM). Moreover, the clear lattice fringes and diffraction patterns also indicate the high crystallinity of the thin films. Generally, the crystallographic structure of HfO2 closely resembles that of ZrO2, with relatively minor differences in their lattice parameters and atomic positions. This similarity often leads to their classification as isostructural species, exhibiting a complete solid solution across the entire composition range [9,33,43]. As observed in the IFFT, the lattice structure exhibits no distortion, likely due to the small difference in the ionic radii of Zr and Hf. To validate the designed structures of H0.5, H0.2/0.5/0.8, and H0/0.2/0.8/1, cross-sectional high-magnification high-angle annular dark-field (HAADF) images and corresponding energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) maps of W, Zr, and Hf as well as line scans of Hf and Zr along the thickness direction were obtained and are displayed in Fig. 2. Along the thickness direction from top to bottom, Zr and Hf are uniformly distributed, with the Hf and Zr peaks overlapping for H0.5. In contrast, Zr and Hf gradually change, and the peak position of Hf does not coincide with that of Zr for H0.2/0.5/0.8 and H0/0.2/0.8/1. Compared with H0.2/0.5/0.8, the weight fraction of Zr tends to increase sharply within the thickness direction from top to bottom for H0/0.2/0.8/1. This result indicates the formation of inhomogeneous structures, as shown in Fig. 1(a).

To investigate the impact of the distribution of Hf/Zr on the ferroelectric properties of Hf0.5Zr0.5O2 thin films, the P*–*V* loops were measured at pristine states and after 104 wake-up cycles. Figure 3(a) displays the pristine *P*–*V* loops of ferroelectric capacitors with thin films of H0.5, H0.2/0.5/0.8, and H0/0.2/0.8/1 under the same voltage of 2.5 V. The corresponding *P*–*V* loops and *I*–*V* curves under various voltages are presented in Figs. S4(a)–S4(c) and S5(a)–S5(c) in the ESM. As the applied voltage increases from 0.4 to 2.5 V, the *P*–*V* loops become increasingly saturated with increasing current intensity in the *I*–*V* curves for all ferroelectric capacitors. Even in pristine states, all ferroelectric capacitors exhibit distinct ferroelectric properties without anti-ferroelectric-like pinched *P*–*V* loop characteristics. This behavior is likely due to a larger proportion of the ferroelectric o-phase in the thin films, which agrees well with the GIXRD results. Despite a tendency for the remnant polarization to decrease with increasing inhomogeneous distribution of Hf and Zr, the 2*P*r consistently remains above 24 μC/cm2 under 2.5 V in the pristine state. Moreover, the remnant polarization and squareness of the *P*–*V* loops improve further after 104 wake-up cycles, as depicted in Fig. 3(b). The values of 2*P*r and the coercive voltage (2*V*c, where 2*V*c = |+*V*c| + |−*V*c|) in the pristine state and after 104 wake-up cycles under the same voltage of 2.5 V are summarized in Fig. 3(c). Both 2*P*r and 2*V*c exhibit higher values in pristine states and after 104 wake-up cycles. Specifically, at 2.5 V in the pristine state, the values of 2*P*r and 2*V*c are approximately 37 μC/cm2 and 2.18 V, 33 μC/cm2 and 1.81 V, and 25 μC/cm2 and 1.76 V for H0.5, H0.2/0.5/0.8, and H0/0.2/0.8/1, respectively. After 104 wake-up cycles, the values of 2*P*r and 2*V*c increase to approximately 45 μC/cm2 and 2.58 V, 41 μC/cm2 and 2.33 V, and 33 μC/cm2 and 2.53 V for H0.5, H0.2/0.5/0.8, and H0/0.2/0.8/1, respectively, under 2.5 V. Notably, the enhancement of 2*P*r is less than 35% after 104 wake-up cycles for all ferroelectric capacitors. Among these ferroelectric capacitors, inhomogeneous thin films of H0.2/0.5/0.8 exhibit greater remnant polarization (2*P*r ≥ 37 μC/cm2) while maintaining a lower coercive voltage (2*V*c ≤ 2.33 V) regardless of whether they are awake. The values of 2*P*r only increase by approximately 24% after 104 wake-up cycles, indicating weak wake-up effects.

Figures 3(d)−3(f) display the *P*−*V* loops of ferroelectric capacitors with thin films H0.5, H0.2/0.5/0.8, and H0/0.2/0.8/1 under different voltages from 0.4 to 2.5 V after 104 wake-up cycles, respectively. The corresponding ±*P*r and ±*V*c values, as well as the *I*−*V* curves, are presented in Figs. S4(e) and S5(d)−S5(f) in the ESM, respectively. Clearly, the squareness of the *P*−*V* loops becomes more pronounced with increasing current intensity in the *I*−*V* curves and ±*P*r and ±*V*c values. The enhancement of the remnant polarization slows as the applied voltage exceeds 2.0 V, indicating polarization saturation. Notably, two typical ferroelectric switching current peaks are observed in the *I*–*V* curves with increasing applied voltage. To gain insight into the local ferroelectric switching characteristics of the thin films, PFM experiments were performed. Figures 3(g) and 3(h) show the out-of-plane PFM amplitude and phase images for representative ferroelectric thin films of H0.2/0.5/0.8. Box-in-box switched patterns were written with ±6 V using conducting tips. It is evident from the PFM amplitude and phase images that the scanned squares exhibit clear bright and dark contrast, corresponding to the upward and downward polarization states, respectively. Figure 3(i) illustrates the hysteresis loops of the PFM phase and amplitude for representative ferroelectric thin films of H0.2/0.5/0.8. A near 180° change in the PFM phase and a clear butterfly loop in the PFM amplitude further validate the excellent ferroelectric switching behavior of the thin films.

Figures 4(a)–4(c) present the *P*–*V* loops of ferroelectric capacitors with H0.5, H0.2/0.5/0.8, and H0/0.2/0.8/1 thin films across a wide frequency range from 100 Hz to 5 kHz after 104 wake-up cycles. Clearly, the remnant polarizations for inhomogeneous H0.2/0.5/0.8 and H0/0.2/0.8/1 are more stable than that for homogeneous H0.5 within the measured frequency range. To better visualize the variation in the remnant polarization and coercive voltage, the values of 2*P*r vs. frequency as well as *P*r and *V*c normalized by the initial 100 Hz (denoted as *P*r/*P*r(initial) and *V*c/*V*c(initial)) as a function of frequency are depicted in Figs. 4(d)–4(f). It is evident that both 2*P*r and *P*r/*P*r(initial) tend to decrease, while the absolute values of the positive coercive voltage (+*V*c) and negative coercive voltage (−*V*c) tend to increase for all the thin films as the frequency increases. Generally, a higher coercive voltage makes it more difficult to switch ferroelectric devices. A comparison among the different ferroelectric thin films reveals that the variations in 2*P*r and *P*r/*P*r(initial) for homogeneous H0.5 are greater than those for inhomogeneous H0.2/0.5/0.8 and H0/0.2/0.8/1, which may be attributed to the presence of defect charges and their high sensitivity to frequency [44]. Interestingly, the H0.2/0.5/0.8 thin films not only exhibit a larger remnant polarization but also maintain excellent frequency stability. Moreover, the coercive voltages of H0.2/0.5/0.8 are smaller than those of H0.5 and H0/0.2/0.8/1. The reduced coercive voltage and weak frequency dependence of ferroelectrics are expected to improve endurance.

Figures 5(a) and 5(b) depict the endurance characteristics of ±*P*r and ±*V*c for ferroelectric capacitors with thin films H0.5, H0.2/0.5/0.8, and H0/0.2/0.8/1, respectively, after 104 wake-up cycles under 2.0 V. Corresponding *P*–*V* loops and *I*–*V* curves during the endurance cycling process are presented in Figs. 5(c)–5(h). As the number of cycles increases, the *P*–*V* loops and the current peak of the *I*–*V* curves slightly shift toward a negative voltage. Namely, the *P*–*V* and *I*–*V* characteristics evidence a slight imprint voltage toward the negative axis. The initial values of 2*P*r for H0.5, H0.2/0.5/0.8, and H0/0.2/0.8/1 are approximately 42, 38, and 31 μC/cm2, respectively. Throughout the endurance measurements, the 2*P*r values of all the samples exhibit a downward trend with increasing cycling number. Although the homogeneous thin films of H0.5 exhibit the largest *P*r value, they experience hard dielectric breakdown after 108 cycles. In contrast, the endurance cycles can be improved by more than one order of magnitude for inhomogeneous thin films of H0.2/0.5/0.8 and H0/0.2/0.8/1. The ±*P*r and ±*V*c for these thin films remain stable after 109 cycles. Particularly for H0.2/0.5/0.8, the 2*P*r values reach ~36 μC/cm2 after 109 cycles, retaining ~94% of the initial values (~38 μC/cm2) without hard breakdown. In general, pure HfO2 and ZrO2 exhibit m- and t-phases, respectively. Hf0.8Zr0.2O2 is dominated by the m-phase, while Hf0.2Zr0.8O2 is dominated by the t-phase [45]. Hf0.5Zr0.5O2 contains a significant amount of ferroelectric o-phase. The higher endurance observed in the inhomogeneous H0.2/0.5/0.8 and H0/0.2/0.8/1 may be attributed to the presence of a nonorthorhombic phase adjacent to the ferroelectric orthorhombic phase, leading to a reduction in ferroelectric domain numbers, domain wall pinning, and charge aggregation [46,47]. Moreover, the absolute values of ±*V*c remain between 0.96 and 1.24 V for H0.2/0.5/0.8 within 109 cycles. Under the same conditions, the inhomogeneous H0.2/0.5/0.8 thin films exhibit robust endurance characteristics along with larger remnant polarizations and smaller coercive voltages without obvious fatigue after 109 cycles. ## 4 Conclusions

MFM-structured ferroelectric capacitors with thin films H0.5, H0.2/0.5/0.8, and H0/0.2/0.8/1 were successfully designed and fabricated in this study. HRTEM and GIXRD measurements confirmed the formation of the ferroelectric o-phase in all three cases, and the designed structures were validated by EDS. The influence of Hf and Zr on the ferroelectricity, endurance, and dielectric properties of Hf0.5Zr0.5O2 thin films was systematically investigated. Notably, outstanding frequency stability, large remnant polarization, great endurance, and high dielectric tunability can be achieved simultaneously in this study. Compared with the conventional composition-uniform Hf0.5Zr0.5O2 ferroelectric thin films, the inhomogeneous distribution of Hf and Zr significantly enhanced the endurance while maintaining excellent ferroelectric performance. The polarization of inhomogeneous thin films H0.2/0.5/0.8 exhibits excellent frequency stability along with a smaller coercive voltage between 100 Hz and 5 kHz. Moreover, after 109 cycles, the 2*P*r remains at ~94% of the initial value (~38 μC/cm2) without hard breakdown. This work provides a novel and effective solution for realizing both high ferroelectric performance and excellent endurance of HfO2-based thin films.
