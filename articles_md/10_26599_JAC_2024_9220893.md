## 1 Introduction

Dielectricly tunable materials, owing to the nonlinearity of their dielectric constants with applied electric fields, are considered to have widespread applications in many electronic tunable devices, such as resonators, phase shifters, and tunable filters [1−4]. To satisfy the requirements of the above devices, dielectrics with large dielectric tunability, low dielectric loss, and high breakdown strength (BDS) are essential [5−7]. To achieve high dielectric tunability and low dielectric loss simultaneously, the figure of merit (FOM), defined as FOM = tunability/tan*δ*, is generally introduced to characterize the tunable performance [8,9]. Furthermore, breakdown strength is considered another important parameter for power equipment with desired tunable properties [10,11]. A higher breakdown strength always means better reliability and stability when tunable devices work under a high applied electric field [12].

The research systems for tunable materials include (Pb,Sr)TiO3 (PST), Pb(Zr,Ti)O3, (Ba,Sr)TiO3 (BST), Ba(Zr,Ti)O3, (Sr,Bi)TiO3, and HfO2 [13−17]. Each system has prominent advantages in the application of tunable devices. Therefore, (Pb,Sr)TiO3 has become one of the mainstream choices for tunable materials due to its high dielectric tunability of approximately 70%, low dielectric loss of 0.001, and excellent FOM of nearly 700 [13]. However, the high toxicity of Pb greatly limits further research and application of PST because of its ability to harm human health and the environment. As another important candidate for electronic tunable elements, (Ba,Sr)TiO3 has drawn considerable attention and has been extensively studied because of its large dielectric tunability and good thermal stability [18]. However, the relatively high dielectric loss makes practical applications difficult. Specifically, the tunability of 44.6% and the dielectric loss of 1.01% for typical Ba0.6Sr0.4TiO3 ceramics lead to an unsatisfactory FOM of only 44 [19]. Therefore, there is an urgent need to develop environmentally lead-free materials with superior tunability that can compete with lead-based PST materials.

To achieve excellent tunable properties, extensive research on (Ba,Sr)TiO3 has been carried out from the perspective of doping methods and improved fabrication processes [20−23]. On the one hand, the addition of acceptor dopants such as Mn3+, Cr3+, and Fe3+, which act as electron acceptors to absorb conduction electron-generated conduction electrons during the sintering process, can significantly reduce the dielectric loss [24,25]. On the other hand, introducing the donor dopant La3+ to replace Ba2+ would facilitate the formation of cation vacancies owing to charge compensation, which could improve the domain-wall mobility and thereby enhance the dielectric constant and dielectric tunability [26,27]. However, the inevitable deterioration of dielectric tunability could be found in acceptor-doped ceramics, while semiconducting behavior and increased dielectric loss would occur in donor-doped samples [28,29]. In other words, either approach could realize improved dielectric tunability and lowered dielectric loss simultaneously, resulting in limited room for obvious enhancement of FOM. In addition to the doping approach, the fabrication method also plays a crucial role in determining the physical and electrical properties of prepared ceramics [30,31]. It has a marked effect on microstructures, element distribution, grain growth, etc., thereby affecting the properties [32,33]. Homogeneous grains, dense microstructures, and uniform element distributions are believed to be profitable for both FOM and breakdown strength, resulting in superior tunable properties [34]. Conventionally, the conventional solid-phase reaction method is widely used because of its simplicity and low cost [35]. However, ceramics prepared by such methods inevitably exhibit unexpected microstructures, such as inhomogeneous element distributions, abnormal grain growth, and generation of pores, which can lead to the deterioration of physical and electrical properties [36,37]. In contrast, the wet-chemical method, owing to its high diffusion kinetics, results in homogeneous element distributions, uniform microstructures, and thus enhanced dielectric properties [38,39]. However, its high cost limits its industrial applications. To achieve compositional homogeneity and satisfy the low-cost requirements of industrial production, a modified solid-state reaction method named the semi-solution method was reported to prepare ZnO ceramics [40]. In the semi-solution method, additives are converted to the aqueous form of soluble metallic salt while the raw matrix materials remain in the solid form. More uniform grain boundaries and element distributions were achieved in the ZnO ceramics, resulting in enhanced electrical properties such as breakdown strength, energy discharge capability, and withstanding capacity [41].

In our previous work, we systematically investigated the effect of different acceptor dopants (Mn, Cr, and Fe) on the tunable properties of acceptor–donor codoped BST [42]. Mn-codoped ceramics exhibited the best tunable properties (FOM = 472) because of their small ionic radius and low electronegativity. In the present study, considering that improved microstructures achieved by modified synthesis could be beneficial for further enhancing tunable properties, we employed a semi-solution (SS) method that benefited microstructures with a homogeneous distribution of dopants and fine grains and a narrow distribution of grain sizes in the prepared BST ceramics. Higher dielectric tunability, lower dielectric loss, and larger FOM were obtained for the La/Mn codoped samples prepared using the semi-solution method. Consequently, enhanced tunable properties were achieved. The optimum tunable properties occurred at La/Mn = 0.75 at%, with a dielectric tunability of 85.0%, a dielectric loss of 0.0011, and an excellent FOM of 773, which are obviously superior to those of traditional lead-based tunable materials. Moreover, the breakdown field strength also improved to 133.30 kV/cm. Furthermore, the effect of the fabrication method on the microstructures and tunable properties of BST ceramics was discussed in detail.

### 2.1 Fabrication

Ceramic samples with a nominal composition of (Ba0.675Sr0.325)1−*x*La*x*Ti1−*x*Mn*x*O3 (*x* = 0.25%, 0.5%, 0.75%, and 1.0%) were prepared by the conventional solid-state (CS) reaction method (termed samples C-0.25, C-0.5, C-0.75, and C-1.0) and the semi-solution method (named samples S-0.25, S-0.5, S-0.75, and S-1.0), respectively. The process of fabricating the ceramic samples via CS and SS is displayed in detail in Fig. 1. In the semi-solution method, additives are turned into the aqueous form of soluble metallic salt while the raw matrix materials remain in the solid form, aiming for a more adequate and uniform mixture. The following analytical-grade raw materials were used: BaCO3 (99.8%), TiO2 (99.99%), SrCO3 (99.99%), La2O3 (99.9%), Mn2O3 (98%), La(NO3)3·6H2O (99.9%), and Mn(NO3)2·4H2O (98%). All the raw materials used in the solid-state reaction method were fully dried and then mixed depending on the stoichiometric formula. For the semi-solution method, the aqueous forms of La(NO3)3·6H2O and Mn(NO3)2·4H2O were mixed with BaCO3, SrCO3, and TiO2 powders. All these mixtures were treated by ball milling with alcohol for 4 h. The slurries obtained by the two methods were dried at 100 °C for approximately 12 h and then calcined in air at 1150 °C for 2 h for chemical synthesis. Next, the calcined powders were ball-milled again for 8 h and then fully dried at 120 °C for 12 h. Afterwards, 8 wt% polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) solution was added to the powders as the granulation binder. All the powders acquired by both methods were pressed into disk-shaped pellets and sintered in air at 1330 °C for 4 h at a heating rate of 100 °C/h to obtain dense ceramics.

### 2.2 Characterizations

The phase structures were identified by using an X-ray diffractometer (XRD; D8 ADVANCE, Bruker, Germany) in the range of 20°–80°. The morphology and elemental distribution of the sintered ceramics were studied by a scanning electron microscope (SEM; VE9800S, Keyence, Japan) and a tungsten filament scanning electron microscope (TFSEM; SU3500, Techcomp Ltd., China) equipped with an energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS). Each sample with a different composition was measured 8 times by the Archimedes method, and the average value was taken as the actual density. The variation in the dielectric constant with temperatures in the range of −50–100 °C was measured at 10 kHz by an LCR analyzer (3532-50, HIOKI, Japan). The electric field dependence of the dielectric constant in the range of 0–20 kV/cm was examined at room temperature by a broadband dielectric spectrometer (Concept 43, Novocontrol, Germany) equipped with a high voltage module (HVB 4000). The variation of the dielectric loss with frequency varied from 1 to 105 Hz was measured by a broadband dielectric spectrometer (Concept 80, Novocontrol, Germany). The samples were immersed in silicone oil to carry out the direct-current (DC) breakdown test. The test was performed at a DC ramp rate of 1 kV/s using a computer control voltage breakdown testing system (HJC-100kV, Huayang Instrumentation Co., Ltd., China).

Figure 2(a) shows the XRD patterns of codoped BST ceramics acquired by the two preparation methods at room temperature (approximately 25 °C). All the samples exhibited good crystallinity and possessed a pure perovskite structure, indicating that La3+ and Mn3+ were incorporated into the BST lattice to form a stable solid solution. Furthermore, fine scanning was carried out, and the magnified pattern at approximately 2*θ* = 45° is displayed in Fig. 2(b). A symmetric (200) diffraction peak can be observed in each obtained sample, suggesting that all the samples are in the pseudocubic phase at room temperature. Furthermore, it can be concluded that the La and Mn dopants have an obvious effect on the unit cell. With increasing La/Mn content, the (200) diffraction peak shifts toward higher angles, indicating shrinkage of the lattice in the BST ceramics. When the additive content increases to 1.0 at%, a significant decrease in the dielectric tunability occurs. When *T*C moves far from room temperature, the contributions to the dielectric tunability provided by the polar clusters weaken, resulting in deteriorated dielectric tunability [54]. The superior tunability of the samples prepared by the semi-solution method can be attributed to the larger grains and better-distributed microstructures. The dielectric tunability of the samples prepared by the semi-solution method reaches the highest value of 91.0% when the content of La/Mn is 0.25 at%, exceeding that of lead-based materials such as Pb(Zr,Ti)O3 (55%), (Pb,Sr)TiO3 (70%), and Pb[(Mg,Nb)(Sc,Nb)]O3 (75%) [13,14,55].

These created conduction electrons can jump between different Ti4+ ions in the perovskite structure and lead to the reduction of Ti4+ to Ti3+, which is considered the main source of dielectric loss [59]. More importantly, as environmentally friendly materials, BST-based materials with superior tunable properties have great potential for use in tunable devices.

Since tunable devices typically work under a highly applied electric field, tunable materials with high BDS are needed. The two-parameter Weibull distribution is always employed to perform the statistical analysis of BDS, as described in Eq. (7) [70,71]:

where *E* refers to the measured DC breakdown strength; *P*(*E*) is the cumulative failure probability; *α* is the scale parameter used to represent the characteristic DC breakdown strength at *P*(*E*) = 63.2%; *β* is the shape parameter used to evaluate the scatter of the measured data. Figure 9 shows the Weibull plots of breakdown strength for the samples prepared by the two methods, and the shape parameter (*β*) is between 20 and 30 for each sample, indicating good reliability of the fitted results [72]. In addition, the characteristic DC breakdown strength of each sample is calculated and recorded in Table 3. The results indicate that samples prepared by the semi-solution method present better BDS, benefiting from the more uniform grain size and increased relative density [73]. In addition, BDS first increases and then decreases with increasing La/Mn content, which is in agreement with the trend of the relative density. In other words, the improved BDS realized by the semi-solution method implies better reliability and stability of tunable microwave devices [12].

## 4 Conclusions

In summary, La/Mn codoped BST ceramics were fabricated via a conventional solid-state reaction method and the semi-solution method. The semi-solution method played an important role in the microstructures of the BST ceramics. Denser microstructures, more homogeneous grains, and more uniform dopant distributions could be obtained by the semi-solution method. Moreover, a sharper dielectric peak, higher dielectric tunability, and lower dielectric loss could be observed in the samples prepared by the semi-solution method, which is associated with the improved microstructure. The optimum tunable properties of sample S-0.75 include a high dielectric tunability of 85.0%, a low dielectric loss of 0.0011, and an excellent FOM of 773, which are superior to those of traditional lead-based tunable materials such as Pb(Zr,Ti)O3, (Pb,Sr)TiO3, and Pb[(Mg,Nb)(Sc,Nb)]O3. Furthermore, the breakdown field strength also improved by 23.6%, benefiting from increased densification. All the results suggested that the semi-solution method rendered BST ceramics more promising for application in tunable devices.
