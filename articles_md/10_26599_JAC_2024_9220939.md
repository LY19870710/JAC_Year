## 1 Introduction

Dielectric capacitors have garnered significant attention and have found widespread applications in fields such as electromagnetic weapons, military industries, and communications [1−3]. The increasing demand for high-quality dielectric capacitors has greatly spurred research on dielectric ceramics with characteristics such as high energy storage density, high efficiency, high power density, and fast charging and discharging time [4]. Ceramic capacitors, in comparison to batteries and supercapacitors, offer advantages such as high operating voltages, ultra-fast charge/discharge time, and mechanical/thermal durability [5]. In the pursuit of energy storage materials, relaxation ferroelectrics (RFEs) have emerged as highly promising candidates, surpassing paraelectrics (PEs), ferroelectrics (FEs), and antiferroelectrics (AFEs) due to their higher dielectric constants, superior energy storage efficiencies, excellent fatigue resistance, and enhanced stability [6−8]. Notably, extensive research on lead-free RFE ceramic capacitor applications has been reported over the past decade, with a focus on perovskite-based relaxation ceramics, yielding remarkable results [6,9−11]. Among the various classes of ferroelectric materials, tungsten bronze (TB) ferroelectric ceramics have garnered significant attention due to their structural flexibility and wide range of application prospects [12,13]. TB materials, with the chemical formula (A1)2(A2)4(C)4B10O30 and connected by the common vertices of oxygen octahedra, exhibit a complex structure and rich compositional modulation, resulting in three different crystallographic sites: pentagonal (A2), tetragonal (A1), and triangular (C) sites. This unique configuration contributes to the excellent dielectric properties of these materials [14,15]. The relaxation properties of dielectric materials can be effectively induced by the ordered/disordered random distribution resulting from the coexistence of cations with different valence states and radii at the A and B sites. Furthermore, the presence of ferroelectric ions occupying the B sites causes shifts and deflections along the *c*-axis in the BO6 octahedra, which are considered to be the primary source of ferroelectricity [16]. Therefore, by leveraging the differences in the coordination radii and chemical valences of the A-site ions, as well as the substitution of B-site ions with different electronic configurations, it is possible to enhance the dielectric and ferroelectric properties of TB ceramics.

High maximum polarization (*P*max) and low remanent polarization (*P*r) are crucial factors for achieving high energy density and efficiency. Cao *et al.* [17] synthesized Sr1.82Gd0.12NaNb5O15, resulting in energy storage density of 2.37 J·cm−3 and efficiency of 94.4%. Feng *et al*. [18] utilized the intrinsic properties of high *P*max, low *P*r, and relaxation behavior in the Sr4Na2Nb10O30 (SNN) system to obtain energy storage density of 1.7 J·cm−3 and efficiency of 80% in Sr3SmNa2Fe0.5Nb9.5O30 dielectric ceramics. Additionally, the breakdown field strength plays a significant role in achieving excellent energy storage performance. Luo *et al*. [19] employed a B-site engineering strategy to prepare a series of Sr0.6Ba0.4Nb2O6 ceramics with outstanding energy storage performance, where Ta5+ substitution induced enhanced relaxation behavior and increased breakdown field strength, resulting in a high recoverable energy density of 3.95 J·cm−3 along with excellent efficiency of 93%. Furthermore, through a two-step sintering method, Luo *et al*. [20] successfully reduced the grain size while increasing the breakdown field strength in Sr0.7Ba0.3Nb2O6 (SBN70), leading to both energy storage density (*W*rec ≈ 3.2 J·cm−3) and efficiency (*η* ≈ 90%). In Sr5BiTi3Nb7O30 tungsten–bronze relaxation ferroelectric ceramics, Zheng *et al*. [21] achieved both high energy storage density (3.72 J·cm−3) and ultra-high efficiency (94.2%) at a field strength of 380 kV·cm−1. Si *et al*. [22] introduced Bi3+ and Ti4+ into the SNN system to synergistically regulate the relaxation ferroelectric properties and finally obtained excellent energy storage density (*W*rec ≈ 4.08 J·cm−3) and high efficiency (*η* ≈ 84.59%) in Sr1.85Bi0.15NaNb4.85Ti0.15O15 ceramics, in addition to the ceramic exhibiting high current density (863.06 A·cm−2) and large power density (94.94 MW·cm−3). Therefore, the Sr1.85Bi0.15NaNb4.85Ti0.15O15 ceramics have great potential for dielectric capacitor applications. Peng *et al*. [23] tuned the storage performance of TTB ceramics from a multiscale tuning strategy including grain, domain and macroscopic perspectives, and a ceramic was finally obtained with ultra-high energy storage density of 5.895 J·cm−3 and high efficiency of 85.37% at field strength of 574 kV·cm−1.

In this study, relaxation behavior was induced by substituting A-site ions with different valence states and radii (Ba2+, Sr2+, Sm3+) and doping Zr4+ with various electronic configurations at the B-site to create an unfilled tungsten−bronze system, thereby enhancing the disorder of the system [24]. It has been demonstrated that relaxation behavior is closely associated with the formation of polar nanoregions (PNRs) [25]. The observed relaxation behavior contributes positively to energy storage due to the rapid polarization facilitated by low-energy barriers in PNRs [26]. Our approach involves breaking long-range ferroelectric ordering and promoting weak coupling mechanisms to form PNRs, leading to high energy storage density and efficiency, as depicted in Fig. 1. Additionally, we achieved a compositionally adjusted Curie peak near room temperature in Sr4.5−*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 3.5), combining both high energy storage density and efficiency. Furthermore, we successfully enhanced the energy storage density of Sr4.5−*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 4.5) by reducing the grain size to increase the breakdown field strength.

## 2 Materials and methods

The Sr4.5−*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4, 4.5) compounds were synthesized using high-purity raw materials, including SrCO3 (99.95%), BaCO3 (99.95%), Sm2O3 (99.99%), ZrO2 (99.99%), and Nb2O5 (99.99%), in stoichiometric ratios as determined by their respective compositions. The initial mixture of the raw materials was homogenized with anhydrous ethanol medium and ZrO2 beads for a duration of 6 h in a high-energy ball mill, followed by drying at a temperature of 120 °C in an oven. Subsequently, the calcination process was carried out at a temperature of 1000 °C for a period of 4 h to ensure the complete reaction and formation of the desired phase. To further enhance the homogeneity and reduce the particle size, another round of ball milling was performed for an additional 6 h using a high-energy ball mill, followed by drying at a temperature of 120 °C in an oven. Finally, the dried powder underwent cold isostatic pressing (256 MPa for 10 min) to form pellets with a diameter measuring approximately 8 mm, which were then sintered at temperatures ranging from 1320–1370 °C to obtain dense ceramic samples. For the electrical properties tested, the thickness of the test sample for the dielectric constant was 1.2 mm (±0.1 mm), the electrode area was 50.24 mm2, the thickness of the test sample for ferroelectric and charge and discharge was 0.08 mm (±0.01 mm), and the electrode area was 3.14 mm2.

The phase composition of the Sr4.5−*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4, 4.5) ceramics was determined using an X-ray diffractometer (PANalytical, Empyrean, Cu Kα1) with a scanning range of 10°–70° (2*θ*) and a step size of 0.017°. The microscopic surface morphology of the sample was captured using an emission scanning electron microscope (S-4800, Hitachi). Raman spectra were recorded using a laser confocal microscope Raman spectrometer (DXR, Thermo Fisher Scientific). To investigate the electrical properties, the ceramic sample was polished, coated with silver paste electrodes (8 mm in diameter) on both sides, and fired at 650 °C for 30 min. The dielectric constant (*ε*r) and dielectric loss (tan*δ*) as functions of temperature were measured using a precision LCR meter (TZDM-200-300, Harbin Julang Technology). Ferroelectric hysteresis loops were obtained using an instrument (TF 2000E Analyzer, Aix ACCT). Discharging performances were investigated using a dielectric charge test system (CFD-005, TG Technologies). Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) analysis was employed to examine the surface morphology of the powders by a transmission electron microscope (JEM 2100F, JEOL).

## 3 Results and discussion

The phase and crystal structure of the Sr4.5−*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4, 4.5) ceramics were analyzed using X-ray diffraction (XRD), and the results are presented in Fig. 2(a). All ceramics, except Sr4.5−*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 4.5), exhibited excellent agreement with the standard card (COD#96-400-1509) for a single tetragonal tungsten–bronze structure with a space group of *P*4*bm*. Additionally, a comparison with HighScore Plus revealed the presence of a diffraction peak corresponding to BaNb2O6 (JCPDS#01-077-0589, orthorhombic) in Sr4.5−*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 4.5), which can be attributed to the larger coordination radius of Ba2+ ions (CN = 15, *r* = 1.65 Å; CN = 12, *r* = 1.61 Å) compared to Sm3+ ions (CN = 12, *r* = 1.24 Å). Consequently, it is difficult for the fifteen-coordinate Ba2+ ions in the tungsten−bronze system to re-enter the twelve-coordinate A1 position after entering the A2 position. Moreover, the diffraction peak at approximately 2*θ* = 32° shifts toward lower angles, as shown in Fig. 2(b), with increasing *x*.

The diffraction spots were successfully calibrated within the space group for P*4*bm*, with cell parameters of *a* = *b* ≈ 12.494 Å, and *c* ≈ 3.952 Å, confirming a lattice spacing of 0.86 nm corresponding to the (110) crystallographic plane and further verifying that the synthesized ceramic samples were TTB structures.

To further investigate the structure and phase composition of the Sr4.5−*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4, 4.5) ceramic samples, Raman scattering spectroscopy was performed on the Sr4.5−*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4, 4.5) ceramic samples, and the results are presented in Fig. 3(h). Three typical Raman peaks of tetragonal tungsten-bronze were observed for the Sr4.5-*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4, 4, 4.5) ceramics in the vicinity of the 200, 630, and 840 cm−1 wavenumbers. The observed Raman peaks include the Zr/Nb–O stretching vibration modes (V1, V2) and O–Zr/Nb–O bending vibration mode (V5), while Raman shifts below 200 cm−1 correspond to external vibrations caused by A-site cation movement relative to the BO6 octahedron [29−31]. Notably, typical tetragonal tungsten–bronze and orthorhombic BaNb2O6 Raman peaks (160 and 670 cm−1) were simultaneously observed in the Sr4.5−*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 4.5) ceramic sample [32]. The V5 peak in the tungsten–bronze structure corresponds to the O–B–O bending vibrational mode, and with Ba2+ doping, the V5 peak gradually sharpens, indicating a decrease in the half-height width and distortion of the BO6 octahedron. Figure 3(h) shows that the Raman peak below 200 cm−1 becomes more pronounced, and the overall peak positions shift toward higher frequencies, indicating the disruption of long-range ordered ferroelectric domains due to Ba2+ doping at A-sites, leading to the distortion of B-site octahedra and contributing to the generation of relaxation ferroelectricity [33].

The dielectric properties of the Sr4.5−*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4, 4.5) ceramics were measured in the temperature range of −150 to 250 °C, as depicted in Figs. 4(a)–4(e). Temperature-dependent variations were observed in the dielectric constant (*ε*r) and dielectric loss (tan*δ*), exhibiting characteristic relaxation behavior. With increasing frequency, the dielectric peak shifted toward higher temperatures and displayed frequency dispersion. Moreover, a wide temperature range exhibited dispersion of the dielectric peak, indicating typical relaxation dielectric properties [34]. As shown in Table 1, substituting Ba2+ ions gradually increased the Curie temperature (*T*C) of the Sr4.5−*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4, and 4.5) ceramics to −34, 12, 21, 64.3, and 48.1 °C, respectively, where the test frequencies were 1, 5, 10, 50, and 100 kHz. Notably, at *x* = 3.5, the Curie peak approached room temperature, which is advantageous for subsequent energy storage tests. However, due to the influence of BaNb2O6 compound formation at Sr4.5−*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 4.5), a significant decrease in the Curie peak temperature (48.1 °C) was observed. The relaxation behavior observed in Sr4.5−*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4, 4.5) ceramics can be attributed to an uneven distribution of differently charged Nb5+ and Zr4+ ions at B-sites along with BO6 octahedral distortion caused by increased disorder at A-sites; this disruption breaks long-range ordering, leading to PNRs [35]. The breakdown field strengths of the five ceramics Sr4.5−*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4, 4.5) were 220, 310, 310, 350, and 460 kV·cm−1, respectively. In general, the disordered occupation of Ba2+, Sr2+, and Sm3+ at the A-site along with Zr4+ doping at the B-site induces distortion in the oxygen octahedral structure, leading to localized structural order/disorder and weak dipole formation, which disrupts long-range ferroelectric ordering, resulting in PNR formation, causing relaxation in Sr4.5−*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4, 4.5) ceramics and contributing to a low *P*r value. The total energy storage density (*W*total), effective energy storage density (*W*rec), and efficiency (*η*) shown in Fig. 6(a) were calculated using Eqs. (7)–(9) [43−45]:

High *P*max and low *P*r contribute to enhancing the energy storage performance; thus, the efficiencies of the five components are 95.3%, 95.2%, 93.8%, 93.9%, and 81.5%, as shown in Fig. 8(b). The exceptional recoverable efficiency can be attributed to the relaxation properties of the Sr4.5−*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4, 4.5) ceramics, which have been demonstrated previously through dielectric constant tests. Combined with previous findings on dielectric constants, it is evident that Sr4.5−*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 3.5) exhibits a higher polarization value due to its Curie peak near room temperature while maintaining both a high energy storage density and high efficiency. Furthermore, we compared several conventional tungsten–bronze ferroelectric ceramics (SBNs and SNNs), which are known for their excellent energy storage properties, in the 200–700 field strength range in recent years (2022–2024). As shown in Fig. 8(c), where color is used to differentiate field strength and shape, Sr4.5–*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 3.5) combines high energy storage density with high efficiency to demonstrate certain advantages [19,21−23,46−55].

The excellent energy storage characteristics are also demonstrated in Sr4.5–*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 4.5), which can be attributed to the significant reduction in grain size compared to the other four compositions, resulting in an increase in resistivity and subsequently enhancing the breakdown field strength (Fig. 8(d)). Moreover, at a voltage of 500 V, the resistivity increases in a composition-dependent manner, with Ba4.5Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 displaying significantly greater resistivity than the other four compositions due to its smaller grain size, which facilitates more grain boundaries and consequently enhances the breakdown field strength.

where *E*BDS is the breakdown field strength of dielectric ceramics, and *G* is the grain size. This relationship reveals that the dielectric breakdown field strength of dielectric ceramics increases with decreasing grain size, with smaller grains resulting in a higher breakdown field strength.

Notably, at an electric field strength of 240 kV·cm−1, a remarkable maximum current of 23.91 A was achieved. This exceptional performance was further demonstrated by the underdamping charge/discharge behavior illustrated in Fig. 8(g), where *C*D = 713.38 A·cm−2 and *P*D = 87.51 MW·cm−3 were obtained for the Sr4.5−*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 3.5) ceramics. Consequently, Sr4.5−*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 3.5) ceramics exhibit outstanding energy storage capabilities along with excellent charge/discharge performance for various applications.

## 4 Conclusions

In this study, the ceramics with the composition Sr4.5−*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4, 4.5) were synthesized using the solid-state reaction method. Among these ceramics, the Sr4.5–*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 3.5) ceramic demonstrated compatibility with a high energy storage density (*W*rec) of 4.31 J·cm−3 and an ultrahigh efficiency of 93.8% at a field strength of 310 kV·cm−1, indicating its potential for practical applications in energy storage systems. By utilizing the large resistivity of Sr4.5−*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 4.5), along with a small grain size, an enhanced breakdown field strength was achieved, resulting in an impressive energy storage density of 5.3 J·cm−3 at 460 kV·cm−1. Furthermore, under overdamping conditions at a field strength of 400 kV·cm−1, Sr4.5−*x*Ba*x*Sm0.5Zr0.5Nb9.5O30 (*x* = 3.5) exhibited excellent discharge performance, with a discharge energy (*W*dis) of up to 2.27 J·cm−3 within an extremely short discharge time (*t*0.9 ≈ 34 ns). Additionally, a remarkable charge/discharge performance characterized by *C*D = 713.38 A·cm−2, and *P*D = 87.51 MW·cm−3 was achieved under high underdamping conditions at a field strength of 240 kV·cm−1. Therefore, this work provides valuable insights into achieving high-performance energy storage properties even at low electric fields.
