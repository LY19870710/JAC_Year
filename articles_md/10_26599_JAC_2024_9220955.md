## 1 Introduction

Two-dimensional (2D) transition metal carbides or nitrides (MXenes; M*n*+1X*n*T*x*, *n* = 1–3; M = transition metals such as Sc, Ti, Zr, Hf, V, Nb, Ta, Cr, and Mo; X = C or N; and T*x* = surface functional groups such as –F, –Cl, –O, and –OH) are 2D van der Waals crystalline materials used for energy storage, printed electronics, electromagnetic interference shielding, and many other applications [1]. Ti3C2, the first MXene, was prepared by selectively etching Ti3AlC2 with HF, thereby eliminating Al atoms from Ti3AlC2 [2]. Various MXenes have subsequently been obtained via acid etching via aqueous HF, LiF+HCl mixtures [3] or ammonium bifluoride ((NH4)HF2) [4]; molten salts such as ZnCl2 and Lewis acids [5]; electrochemical etching via NaOH or KOH [6]. However, corrosive reactants and violent chemical reactions affect the 2D topological integrity of MXenes, affecting their performance, such as their thermal stability, oxidation resistance, energy storage, catalytic properties, and electromagnetic properties.

These shortcomings can be attributed to the inefficient etching of Al in the MAX phase. During Al etching, chemical reactions occur in the Al and MX layers, resulting in defects on the MXene surface due to the removal of M atoms. This phenomenon can be prevented by timely removal of the Al layer. Thus, highly efficient strategies for Al etching, such as the reaction between an AlLi alloy and ethanol, have been proposed [7]. In this study, MXenes were fabricated via H2O etching of MAX phases. The reactions occurred at room temperature and acid neutral to obtain MXenes that are stable and ecofriendly, easy to fabricate, have few layers and good crystallinity. Moreover, epitaxial self-intercalation yields stable Cr2CT*x* MXenes via *in situ* growth of 2D layered double hydroxides [8,9] (2D-LDHs; [LiAl2(OH)6]F∙*y*H2O and LiAl2(OH)7∙*y*H2O herein). The 2D Moiré superlattice Cr2CT*x* MXene synthesized via this strategy exhibited room-temperature ferromagnetism. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first 2D Cr2CT*x* MXene with experimental room-temperature ferromagnetism.

## 2 Materials and methods

**Fabrication of Cr****2****(AlLi)C**. Commercially produced pure Cr powder (99.5% purity, China's Sinopharm), graphite (99.95% purity, China’s Sinopharm), and an AlLi20 alloy (Al : Li ≈ 1 : 1, homemade) were used as raw materials. They were mixed by roll ball milling (100 r/min, ball-to-powder weight ratio = 10 : 1) for 10 h at a molar ratio of Cr : AlLi20 : C = 2 : 1.4 : 1 in a glove box filled with argon (Lab2000, Etelux, USA). The mixture was then poured into a cold-pressing steel mold under an axial pressure of 150 MPa to obtain a preform in the glove box. The preform was subsequently removed via an airlock, followed by pressureless sintering at 1300 °C under a protective argon atmosphere and a holding time of 10 min in a pressureless sintering furnace (1000-4560-FP20, Thermal Technology, USA). After the furnace cooled, Cr2(AlLi)C bulk materials were synthesized, which were smashed with a hammer and ground to a powder with a mortar.

**Fabrication of Cr****2****C MXene**. The fewlayer-Cr2C (FL-Cr2C) MXene functionalized with OH or O was fabricated using only deionized water. The as-obtained solid products contained Al(OH)3, LiAl2(OH)7∙*y*H2O, and Cr2CT*x* (T = OH or O). To fabricate FL-Cr2C MXenes enriched with OH and F, Cr2(AlLi)C powder was poured into a glass beaker containing saturated NaF aqueous solution (Cr2(AlLi)C : NaF solution = 5 g : 100 mL; the same ratio was used during etching with pure water).

The synthesis of Cr2C with water was as follows: After 2 h of reaction with deionized H2O and simultaneous ultrasonic shaking, the reaction mixture was separated via static placing (2–4 h) to remove Al(OH)3 and LDH (both floating in liquid). After the supernatant was discarded and the solid product was retained, it was repeatedly washed with water up to 10 times to ensure the complete removal of Al(OH)3 and LDH. MXene was isolated from the solid via high-density liquid centrifugal extraction (utilizing ~2.5 g/cm3 of ZnCl2 aqueous solution). Following washing with water to eliminate ZnCl2, Cr2CT*x* MXene was placed on filter paper after vacuum filtration and dried at 323 K.

In summary, pure FL-Cr2CT*x* generally results from a three-stage purification process: (1) the mixture was soaked in hot water (353 K, 2 L, 2–4 h) followed by 10 sequential pourings; (2) the extraction with ZnCl2 and centrifugation (3000 r/min for 5 minutes with ZnCl2 aqueous solution); (3) the samples were washed with water, filtered, and then dried at 323 K. For the reaction with saturated NaF aqueous solution, the solid was washed with water (0.25 g/L Na3AlF6 was dissolved in H2O at 293 K) in the first step to remove Na3AlF6 (*P*21/*n*).

**Characterizations**. The lattice structures of the as-obtained products were characterized via an X-ray diffractometer (XRD; Smart Lab 9, Rigaku, Japan). The microstructure of the Pt-sprayed Cr2CT*x* was observed via a scanning electron microscope (JSM-7001F, JEOL, Japan) equipped with an energy-dispersive spectrometer (EDS; X-Max, Oxford, UK). The microstructure of the material was observed via a scanning transmission electron microscope (JEM-F200, JEOL, Japan) equipped with an EDS detector (JED-2300T, JEOL, Japan). The ferromagnetism of Cr2CT*x* was tested via a vibrating sample magnetometer (7404, LakeShore, USA), and the MXene photopermeability was determined via optical microscopy.

**DFT simulation**. DFT+U calculations were computed on a supercomputer (built by Beijing Jiaotong University, China) equipped with the Vienna *ab initio* simulation package (VASP, v6.2.0) via the exchange-correlation function GGA-PBE, with spin polarization (ISPIN = 2), force convergence conditions (EDIFF = 1E-08 eV, EDIFFG = −2.5E-02 eV/A), smearing value (ISMEAR = 0, SIGMA = 0.02), cutoff energy (ECUT = 500 eV), ISIF = 3 and PREC = accurate, Hubbard U correction (LDAUTYPE = 2, U = 3.3 0 0, J = 0.3 0 0, LMAXMIX = 4). All the execution files were generated by Vaspkit.

**Room-temperature ferromagnetic Cr****2****CT*****x*** **MXene**. Figure 3(a) shows the magnetic hysteresis of Cr2(AlLi)C (blue line), indicating a typical linear curve that corresponds to a paramagnetic material. Interestingly, the as-obtained products exhibit typical ferromagnetic material properties (Fig. 3(a), orange curve). Thus, the reaction of Cr2(AlLi)C and H2O yields ferromagnetic materials. Using the simple process of elimination, we can suppose that Cr2CT*x* (T = OH, O) MXene is the only probable material that exhibits room-temperature ferromagnetism because LDHs and Al(OH)3 do not contain elements such as Cr, Mn, La, Fe, Co, and Ni (a discussion of the impact of impurities such as Cr2O3 on ferromagnetism is presented in Section 3.1). After dehydration during the drying process, Cr2CO2 could be the correct chemical formula of Cr2CT*x* (T = OH, O) MXene. The calculated band structures of Cr2CO2 MXene in Fig. 3(b) show a tiny band gap (0.75 eV) at the Fermi level (EF), implying that a semimetal material [10] is present in the 2D Cr2CO2 MXene; these results are similar to the results of the reported calculations of Cr2CO2 MXene [11,12]. The density of state (DOS) shows typical spins of semimetallic ferromagnets [13,14], such as CrO2, whose energy gap at the Fermi level is essentially in spin-down states. Cr2CT*x* MXene has poor electrical conductivity because Pt spraying is required for observing MXene via SEM, and it is widely recognized that the semimetal electrical conductivity is deficient. These findings support the semimetallic nature of Cr2CT*x* MXene. On the basis of the low band gap estimated via DFT calculations, Cr2CO2 MXene can be considered a potential stabilized room-temperature ferromagnetic semiconductor. The specific saturation magnetization was 0.26 emu/g, which was several orders of magnitude greater than that of V2C MXene [15]. Figure 3(c) shows a strong relationship between the magnetic moments and axial angles of the lattice parameters of Cr2CO2 MXene based on the regression analysis of the optimized geometry used to determine ferromagnetism. The increasing α and decreasing β indicate a shift between the bilayers of Cr2CO2 MXene. The geometry optimization shows a positive correlation between the magnetic moment of Cr2CO2 MXene and the shift level of the bilayer Cr2CO2 MXene (Fig. 3(c)). Moreover, as bilayer Cr2CO2 MXene shifts to a higher level, the magnetic moment at 0 K increases from 7.19*μ*B (the original magnetic moment is 6*μ*B per Cr) to 15.75*μ*B per Cr8C4O8 (~148.48 emu/g). Figure 3(d) shows the advanced stage of this work (the real-line and light yellow triangle) in terms of thickness, specific saturation magnetization (*B*s, emu/g) and Curie temperature compared with other 2D ferromagnetic materials, such as V2C [16], Ta7S12 [17], ABC-TLG/hBN film [18], twisted double-trilayer CrI3 [19], Fe3GeTe2 [20], and Mn-rich MnSb2Te4 [21]. Detailed information is depicted in Table S2 in the ESM. The highest temperature reached 353 K during the reaction and drying, confirming that the Curie temperature exceeded 353 K.

### 3.1 Discussion on impact of impurities on ferromagnetism

The XRD result in Fig. 1(a-iii) shows that the only phase in our VSM test is Cr2C MXene, thereby clearly presenting ferromagnetism resulting from Cr2C MXene. However, one may be concerned with the impact of extremely trace impurities such as Cr2O3 beyond those detected by XRD on ferromagnetism. First, it is crucial to track trace impurities introduced by Cr2(AlLi)C. The VSM examination of Cr2(AlLi)C yields a paramagnetic nature, emphasizing the paramagnetic nature of these impurities introduced by Cr2(AlLi)C. Second, if ferromagnetic impurities such as Cr2O3 are generated during the reaction between Cr2(AlLi)C and H2O, Cr2O3 must be from the oxidized Cr2C MXene, which is also one of the reasons for the anisotropic Heisenberg model causing ferromagnetism in a two-dimensional system unless Cr2O3 is independently separated from the surface of Cr2C MXene. However, a normal Cr2O3 particle is antiferromagnetic, whereas a Cr2O3 film can be ferromagnetic [22]. In fact, the oxidation of MXenes at room temperature mainly generates oxide particles because C needs a relatively high temperature to become CO [23]. The structure of one layer of the Cr2O3 film attached to the surface of Cr2C MXene is Cr2CO2 MXene itself. We suppose that this structure is the ideal Cr2CO2 MXene crystalline structure and exceeds the value of Cr2C MXene itself.

### 3.2 What is epitaxial self-intercalation mechanism by H2O etching of Cr2(AlLi)C?

The nucleation and epitaxial growth of LDHs are the first steps in the epitaxial self-intercalation mechanism, which is caused by the chain reaction between Li and H2O, which yields a broad response even with a minor trigger. The H ions generated by the ionization of H2O first enter the Li sites and react with them to generate H2 gas and Li ions, as shown in the lower left of Fig. 4. During this process, Al is activated into a higher-energy state and reacts with H ions, forming stable Al ions after the energy decreases. The accumulation zone of Al and Li ions is the nucleation site of LDHs assisted by OH ions released by H2O ionization (the dark blue hexagonal area in the top left zone of Fig. 4); the epitaxial growth based on the substrate of hcp Cr2C proceeds as indicated by the arrows. Figure S4 in the ESM shows the SEM images of the reaction of Cr2(AlLi)C with H2O at 0.5, 1, and 1.5 h. Figure S4(a) in the ESM shows a reacted Cr2(AlLi)C grain exposed to H2O for 0.5 h, exhibiting potential interstitials due to AlLi layer dissolution. Figure S4(b) in the ESM shows the onset of 2D LDH and 2D Cr2C formation during this period.

Self-intercalation by H2O etching of Cr2(AlLi)C via 2D LDHs and Li ions is the second step, as shown in the middle of Fig. 4. 2D LDHs are novel 2D solid materials with higher strength than the liquid intercalator [24]. The layer of the solid intercalator (single-layered 2D LDH) grows like a pushed nanowedge to create a lateral force that separates the single-layered 2D Cr2C. During this process, H2 gas bubbles spontaneously apply pressure on both sides. In other words, the epitaxial growth of LDHs generates a force in the heading direction that is applied to both sides and amplified by the pressure imparted by H2 gas bubbles. The Li-lacking layers in the middle of the two Li-rich layers generate nano-Al(OH)3 during the first step, and the self-intercalations in these layers are dominated by Li ions. Figure S4(c) in the ESM portrays the surface condition after 1 h, revealing a roughened texture due to prolonged reaction, and Fig. S4(d) in the ESM reveals an increase in particle-like Al(OH)3 generation during this time. The transverse force promoted the epitaxial growth of LDHs, and the process was repeated until the force in the heading direction and the lattice resistance of Cr2(AlLi)C were balanced. Some LDHs grow epitaxially around the same layer, separating Cr2(AlLi)C when they are meeting. Single-layered Cr2C MXene is fabricated if the next layer is processed in the same way. When not meeting with another LDH, a piece of Cr2C is stripped from bulk Cr2(AlLi)C, thus forming the small-size Cr2C MXene. A few-layer Cr2C MXene is fabricated if two Li-rich layers that produce LDHs meet far apart. Cr2C then absorbs O or OH functional groups to form FL-Cr2CTx*, as shown in the middle of Fig. 4. Figure S4(e) in the ESM presents the residual Cr2(AlLi)C after 1.5 h of continuous reaction, revealing a disrupted layered structure, and Fig. S4(f) in the ESM displays detached 2D Cr2C from Cr2(AlLi)C. Epitaxial self-intercalation contributes to the formation of Moiré superlattices by force during the epitaxial growth of LDHs, as shown on the right of Fig. 4.

### 3.3 Why does fabricated Cr2CT<i>x</i> have room-temperature ferromagnetism?

The 2D isotropic Heisenberg models show that an isotropic coupling system (*Jx* = *J**y* = *J**z*) cannot be ferromagnetic or antiferromagnetic at nonzero temperature on the basis of Eq. (4) [25]:

(4) |Sz|ConstT1/21|ln|h||1/2

where * Sz is the spin projection operator, T* is the thermodynamic temperature, Const represents a constant, and *h* is the external magnetic field along the *z*-axis. When *J**x* = *J**y* ≠ *Jz*, the same results are obtained for fields aligning in the *x* or *y* direction; however, spontaneous magnetization for a small field in the *z* direction reaches an infinite volume limit of magnetization per local zone. Single-layer Cr2CO2 belongs to this situation because its lattice has high-level symmetry in the *x*–*y* plane but varies along the *z*-axis (Fig. 2(g)). The local ion anisotropy can break the rule of 2D isotropic Heisenberg in 2D van der Waals crystals [26−28]. Figure 2(g) shows that the monolayer Cr2CO2 MXene generates local coupling anisotropy by shifting the Cr-1 and Cr-2 layers, causing a difference in the spin interaction between the zones 1 and 2; this finding corresponds with the calculated function between the lattice parameter and magnetic moment (Fig. 3(c)). The local twisted or shifted Moiré superlattices observed via TEM (Fig. 2) also show this local coupling anisotropy, which generates spontaneous magnetization on the *z*-axis. This phenomenon is not discussed here for the sake of brevity. The anisotropic Heisenberg Hamiltonian (*H*) is as Eq. (5) [26]:

(5) H=∑i,jJijSi⋅Sj+∑iA(Siz)2−gμB∑iBSiz

where *S**i* is the spin operator on the site *i*, and *J**ij* (> 0 for ferromagnetism) is the exchange interaction between the sites *i* and *j*, *A* is the single-ion anisotropy, *g* is the Landé *g*-factor, *μ*B is the Bohr magneton, *B* is the external magnetic field on the *z**-***axis, and * Siz is the spin projection operator on the site i* on the *z**-***axis, which exhibits a commutation relation between * −gμB∑iBSiz and  ∑iSiz, and the mean-field approximation shows the function between the Cuire temperature (T*c) and exchange interaction (*J**ij*) is as Eq. (6) [29]:

(6) Tc=−2S(S+1)3kB(1n∑i≠jJij+A)

where *n* is the number of neighboring sites, *S* is the magnetic moment of a single Cr ion, and *k*B is the Boltzmann constant. Thus, the Curie temperature increases as the number of layers increases (as verified in 2D van der Waals crystals [26,29]) because the sublattices in a unit cell accumulate. For example, * Jij is added between the double sublattices in the model of two sublattices in one unit cell, which increases the Curie temperature. Alternatively, the renormalized spin-wave theory can be used to obtain the ratio of magnetizations Eq. (7) [26]:

(7) MM0=1−1nNS∑±∑k(exp(ℏω±kBT)−1)−1

where M* is the magnetization, *M*0 is the saturation magnetization, and *N* unit cells and *n* sublattices are present in one unit cell (* ± represents the two magnon branches due to the two sublattices, k* represents the wave vector, and *ħω* is the spin-wave spectrum). The Curie temperature is defined in this function when *M*/*M*0 = 0; *n* maintains a positive correlation with the Curie temperature. Thus, ML-Cr2CT*x* has better ferromagnetic properties at room temperature than does FL-Cr2CT*x*. However, *T*c of ML-Cr2CT*x* is considerably lower than that at room temperature [6] because the van der Waals force in multiple layers increases as the number of layers increases. Interestingly, the effect of the local anisotropy of Cr2C MXenes caused by Moiré superlattices also decreases. Studies on heterostructures and interfacial engineering of 2D magnets have confirmed the negative correlation between the number of layers and magnetization [30,31]; these results correspond with those related to studies on Moiré superlattices [32,33]. FL-Cr2CT*x* MXene fabricated by H2O etching of Cr2(AlLi)C combines the strong magnetization of Moiré superlattices with the layer-dependent ferromagnetism of van der Waals crystals to maintain room-temperature ferromagnetism for few layers (~10–50 layers on the basis of light permeability and electron transmission under high-resolution transmission (HRTEM) modeling with thicknesses lower than 20 nm). In summary, the Curie temperature strongly affects the number of layers of van der Waals crystals in Cr2C MXene.

In the H2O-etching strategy, the epitaxial self-intercalation mechanism controls the fabrication of FL-Cr2CT*x*, impacting its ferromagnetism. Theoretical calculations must be performed to determine the room-temperature ferromagnetism in Cr2CT*x* MXene [34,35]; however, the present other strategy destroyed the expression of this nature. The typically fabricated ML-Cr2CT*x* is accordion-like [36], which is considerably thicker to form twisted or shifted FL-Cr2CT*x*. Supposedly, the ferromagnetism of Cr2C MXenes is controlled by the thickness (or the number of layers); FL-Cr2CT*x* is twisted or shifted to form Moiré superlattices to enhance ferromagnetism by generating local coupling anisotropy and changing the spin system. Lattice integrity also affects ferromagnetism. In electrochemistry [6], the electromagnetic field may affect the ferromagnetism of Cr2C MXenes; FL-Cr2C and monolayer Cr2C MXenes do not show any ferromagnetism because this 2D effect is based on topological singularity and integrity. If the lattice integrity is affected by fabrication, it affects the topological singularity and integrity. In summary, epitaxial self-intercalation by H2O etching of Cr2(AlLi)C poses several problems: (1) this mechanism decreases the layer thickness and causes twisted or shifted effects in the Cr2C MXene due to the force in the epitaxial growth of LDHs; (2) H2O etching at room temperature retains the lattice integrity due to the acid–base neutral reaction and relatively smooth reaction; thus, the proposed strategy stabilizes Cr2C MXene.

## 4 Conclusions

The following conclusions were obtained from this study:

1) Cr2CT*x* (T = O, F, and OH) MXene was synthesized on the basis of the epitaxial self-intercalation of H2O-etching Cr2(AlLi)C.

2) Cr2CT*x* MXenes and LDHs were obtained via epitaxial self-intercalation. The epitaxial growth controlled the shapes of the LDHs, which formed a standard hexagon. LDHs act as solid intercalation agents to assist in the formation of FL-Cr2CT*x* MXenes. Epitaxial self-intercalation dominated the fabrication of Cr2CT*x* MXene.

3) The room-temperature ferromagnetism of 2D Cr2CT*x* MXene was experimentally demonstrated via the proposed novel strategy. The specific saturation magnetization of Cr2CT*x* MXene at room temperature is dozen times greater than that of the reported V2C MXene. Compared with many other 2D materials, the as-fabricated Cr2CT*x* MXene exhibited enhanced room-temperature ferromagnetism; Cr2CT*x* MXene can be used as a 2D ferromagnetic semiconductor for potential applications.

4) Cr2CT*x* MXene has enhanced crystallinity, fewer defects, and high performance in terms of ferromagnetism. The proposed approach is environmentally friendly, highly productive, and safe.
